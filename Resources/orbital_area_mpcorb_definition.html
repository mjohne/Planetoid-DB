Orbital Area (Aorb) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Planar Elliptic Area Geometry, Kepler’s Second Law Areal Velocity Coupling, and Evaluation from MPCORB

1. Fundamental Planar-Geometric & Astrodynamical Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In analytical conic geometry and two-body celestial mechanics, the orbital area (also termed the enclosed elliptic orbital area, swept orbital area per revolution, or in German astrometry Bahnfläche)—typically denoted by Aorb or A (distinguished carefully from the semi-major axis a)—is the total two-dimensional Euclidean surface area enclosed within the closed instantaneous osculating Keplerian ellipse in the orbital plane. It is a dimensional area quantity expressed in square Astronomical Units (AU2).

Integrating the polar areal element dA = ½ r2 dν over one complete revolution (0 ≤ ν < 2π) around the primary focus F1 (the Sun)—or equivalently applying Green’s theorem to the perifocal Cartesian parametrization xω(E) = a cos E − ae, yω(E) = b sin E—yields the exact closed-form expression for the orbital area Aorb:

Aorb = 1 2 ∫02π r(ν)2 dν = π a b = π a2 √ 1 − e2 = π a √ q · Q

where b = a√(1 − e2) = √(qQ) is the semi-minor axis, equal to the geometric mean of the perihelion distance q and aphelion distance Q. Unlike the orbital perimeter L (which requires non-elementary elliptic integrals), the orbital area is purely elementary and satisfies the following bounds and asymptotic regimes:

  • Strict Boundedness (0 < Aorb ≤ πa2): At fixed semi-major axis a, the enclosed orbital area decreases strictly monotonically with increasing eccentricity e according to the geometric contraction factor √(1 − e2) = b/a.
  • Circular Maximum (e = 0 ⇒ Aorb = πa2): By the isoperimetric inequality in the Euclidean plane, a circular orbit encloses the maximum possible area πa2 for a given semi-major axis a.
  • Degenerate Collinear Collapse (e → 1− ⇒ Aorb → 0+): As e → 1− at finite a, b → 0+ and the enclosed area collapses to zero (Aorb → 0 AU2), even though the orbital perimeter remains strictly bounded below by 4a > 0.
  • Parabolic Divergence (e = 1): Because a parabola is an unbounded open curve (a → ∞), it encloses no finite planar area (Aorb = +∞).

2. Kepler’s Second Law, Areal Velocity & Angular Momentum Coupling

In two-body Newtonian gravitation, the orbital area Aorb provides the direct geometric bridge between Kepler’s second and third laws and the specific orbital angular momentum h:

2.1 Constant Areal Velocity (dA/dt)

By Kepler’s second law (law of equal areas), the radius vector from the Sun (F1) to the orbiting minor planet sweeps out equal planar areas in equal intervals of time. Integrating over one complete sidereal orbital period P = 2π√(a3/μ), the constant areal velocity Ȧ is:

Ȧ = dA dt = Aorb P = π a2 √(1 − e2) 2 π a3/2 / √μ = 1 2 √ μ a (1 − e2) = h 2

where h = |r × v| = √(μp) is the magnitude of the specific orbital angular momentum vector and p = a(1 − e2) is the semi-latus rectum. Thus, the orbital area satisfies Aorb = ½ h P.

2.2 Mean Anomaly (M) as Normalized Swept Area Fraction

Because the areal sweep rate is strictly constant, the elapsed time Δt = t − T0 since perihelion passage, the mean anomaly M (in radians), and the swept focal sector area Aswept(t) are directly proportional to the total orbital area Aorb:

Aswept(t) Aorb = t − T0 P = M 2 π = E − e sin E 2 π

2.3 3D Oriented Areal Vector (Aorb)

In three-dimensional J2000.0 ecliptic coordinates, the oriented orbital area vector Aorb = Aorb Ŵ points along the positive orbit normal Ŵ = (sin i sin Ω, −sin i cos Ω, cos i)T, so its projection onto the reference ecliptic plane (xy-plane) has signed area Aecl = Aorb cos i.

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database published by the International Astronomical Union’s Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC):

  • Implicit Derived Quantity (Not Tabulated): The orbital area Aorb is not stored as an explicit column in MPCORB.DAT. Instead, it is computed directly from the osculating numeric eccentricity e (columns 71–79, format F9.7) and osculating semi-major axis a (columns 93–103, format F11.7) via Aorb = π a2 √(1 − e2).
  • Guaranteed Finite & Strictly Positive Across All MPCORB.DAT Records: Because every record in MPCORB.DAT represents a bound elliptic minor planet orbit (0 ≤ e < 1, a > 0), the orbital area satisfies 0 < Aorb ≤ πa2 for 100% of catalog records, attaining its upper bound πa2 for circular orbits (e = 0).
  • Osculating Heliocentric Reference & Unit: Derived from MPCORB elements at the record’s packed Epoch (columns 21–25) in the J2000.0 mean ecliptic plane, Aorb is an instantaneous osculating planar area expressed in square Astronomical Units (AU2) (1 AU2 ≡ 2.237952291797 × 1022 m2).
  • Applications in Solar System Dynamics:
    • Areal Sweep Rate & Specific Angular Momentum Verification: Computing Ȧ = Aorb / P = h / 2 as an invariant check in numerical orbit propagators.
    • Eccentricity-Induced Area Compression (√(1 − e2)): Quantifying how much smaller the enclosed orbital area of an eccentric asteroid or comet is compared to a circular orbit of identical orbital period P (and identical semi-major axis a).
Numerical Stability Note: When computing √(1 − e2) for high-eccentricity objects in MPCORB.DAT where e → 1− (such as Damocloids or long-period minor planets with e > 0.999), evaluate the radicand in factored form Math.Sqrt((1.0 - e) * (1.0 + e)) rather than Math.Sqrt(1.0 - e * e) to avoid squaring-induced precision loss near 1.0.
