Angular Velocity

Instantaneous Heliocentric Rate of Change of True Anomaly

1. Formal Definition

In heliocentric orbital mechanics applied to the Minor Planet Center Orbit Database (MPCORB), the instantaneous orbital angular velocity (ω or ν̇) is the time rate of change of the minor planet's true anomaly ν (or, equivalently, its argument of latitude u = ωp + ν or true longitude l = Ω + ωp + ν) measured within the instantaneous osculating orbital plane with respect to the heliocenter.

In vector form, the orbital angular velocity vector ω is directed along the orbital pole unit vector ĥ (normal to the osculating orbital plane, inclined by i to the J2000 ecliptic) with scalar magnitude ν̇ = dν/dt. It must be strictly distinguished from the mean daily motion n = dM/dt stored directly in MPCORB.DAT (columns 81–91): whereas n is a constant orbital-averaged angular rate, ν̇(t) varies continuously around an eccentric orbit in inverse proportion to the square of the heliocentric distance.

2. Mathematical Formulation from MPCORB Elements

Directly from Kepler's second law (conservation of specific orbital angular momentum h = r2 ν̇), the instantaneous angular velocity is determined by the semimajor axis a (AU, MPCORB.DAT cols 93–103), eccentricity e (cols 71–79), mean motion n (cols 81–91), and true anomaly ν:

ν̇ = dν / dt = h / r2 = √(μ p) / r2 = n · (1 + e cos ν)2 / (1 − e2)3/2 (Eq. 1)

where:

  • n = √(μ / a3) is the unperturbed Keplerian mean motion (in rad/day, or multiplied by 180/π for deg/day as tabulated in MPCORB.DAT).
  • h = √(μ a(1 − e2)) = n a2 √(1 − e2) is the specific angular momentum in AU2 day−1.
  • r = a(1 − e2) / (1 + e cos ν) = a(1 − e cos E) is the instantaneous heliocentric distance in AU.

Expressed in terms of the eccentric anomaly E, the instantaneous angular velocity is:

ν̇ = n · √(1 − e2) / (1 − e cos E)2 (Eq. 2)

3. Dynamic Range & Angular Rates in MPCORB

Because ν̇ ∝ r−2, eccentric minor planets (such as Apollo/Amor near-Earth asteroids, Damocloids, or Jupiter-family comets) exhibit dramatic variations in angular velocity between perihelion and aphelion:

Unit Conventions: In MPCORB.DAT, mean motion n is reported in degrees per day (deg/d) to 7 decimal places (e.g., n ≈ 0.2141 deg/d for (1) Ceres). Accordingly, ν̇ is naturally expressed in deg/d, arcsec/h (1 deg/d = 150 arcsec/h), or SI rad/s (1 deg/d = (π/180) / 86400 ≈ 2.0201 × 10−7 rad/s). Note that ν̇ is the heliocentric orbital angular rate, not the apparent topocentric sky-plane proper motion (μα cos δ, μδ), which also incorporates Earth's orbital motion and observer-target geometry.
