Tangential Velocity Component

Transverse In-Plane Orbital Velocity Component Orthogonal to the Radius Vector

1. Formal Definition

In heliocentric two-body astrodynamics applied to the Minor Planet Center Orbit Database (MPCORB), the tangential velocity component (vθ or vt, also termed the transverse or azimuthal velocity component) is the orthogonal projection of a minor planet's instantaneous heliocentric orbital velocity vector (v) onto the unit vector (θ̂) lying within the osculating orbital plane perpendicular to the heliocentric radius vector (r̂) in the direction of orbital motion.

Together with the radial velocity component vr, the tangential velocity component forms an orthogonal polar decomposition of the planar orbital velocity vector: v = vrr̂ + vθθ̂, such that the total orbital speed satisfies v2 = vr2 + vθ2. Note that vθ is distinct from the tangent to the elliptical trajectory itself (which coincides with the full velocity vector v), except at the apsides (ν = 0° and 180°) where vr = 0.

2. Mathematical Formulation from MPCORB Elements

Using the osculating semimajor axis a (AU, MPCORB.DAT cols 93–103), eccentricity e (cols 71–79), and true anomaly ν derived from the mean anomaly M0 (cols 27–35) and mean daily motion n (cols 81–91), the tangential velocity component is governed by the conservation of specific orbital angular momentum h:

vθ = v · θ̂ = r (dν / dt) = h / r = √(μ / p) · (1 + e cos ν) (Eq. 1)

where:

  • h = ||r × v|| = √(μ p) = √(μ a(1 − e2)) is the magnitude of the specific relative angular momentum vector (in AU2 day−1).
  • p = a(1 − e2) is the semi-latus rectum in AU.
  • r = p / (1 + e cos ν) = a(1 − e cos E) is the instantaneous heliocentric distance in AU.
  • μ = k2 is the heliocentric gravitational parameter (k = 0.01720209895 AU3/2 day−1).

Expressed in terms of the eccentric anomaly E, vθ takes the form:

vθ = (√(μ a (1 − e2))) / (a(1 − e cos E)) = (n a √(1 − e2)) / (1 − e cos E) (Eq. 2)

3. Extremal Values & Flight-Path Angle Relationship

Because specific angular momentum h is an invariant of unperturbed Keplerian motion, vθ is strictly positive (vθ > 0) for all bound prograde/retrograde two-body orbits when θ̂ is defined along the direction of increasing true anomaly ν, and varies inversely with heliocentric distance r:

Orbital Point Heliocentric Distance r Tangential Velocity vθ
Perihelion (ν = 0°) — Global Maximum q = a(1 − e) vθ,max = √(μ/a) · √((1 + e)/(1 − e)) = vperi
Semi-latus Rectum (ν = ±90°) r = p = a(1 − e2) vθ = √(μ / p)
Semimajor Axis Crossing (E = ±90°) r = a vθ = √(μ(1 − e2) / a)
Aphelion (ν = 180°) — Global Minimum Q = a(1 + e) vθ,min = √(μ/a) · √((1 − e)/(1 + e)) = vaph

The ratio of the radial to tangential velocity components defines the flight-path angle γ (the angle between the velocity vector v and the local horizontal plane normal to r):

tan γ = vr / vθ = (e sin ν) / (1 + e cos ν) (Eq. 3)
