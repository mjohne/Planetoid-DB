Current Orbital Velocity (v(t)) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Vis-Viva Formulation, Radial & Transverse Kinematics, and Propagation from MPCORB

1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Kinematic Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In two-body Keplerian celestial mechanics, current orbital velocity—more strictly termed instantaneous heliocentric orbital speed v(t) = ∥v(t)∥ = ∥dr/dt∥ (or, in vectorial form, the instantaneous heliocentric state velocity vector v(t) ∈ ℝ3)—is the magnitude (and direction) of the minor planet’s inertial velocity relative to the Sun (or Solar System barycenter in osculating J2000.0 ecliptic/equatorial coordinates) at a specified evaluation epoch t (either the osculating catalog epoch t0 or a propagated observation epoch tobs in Terrestrial Time TT), expressed in km/s or AU/day (1 AU/day = 1731.4568368 km/s).

Under conservation of specific mechanical orbital energy ε = v2/2 − μ/r = −μ/(2a) in the heliocentric two-body problem, the instantaneous scalar orbital speed v(t) at heliocentric radial distance r(t) = ∥r(t)∥ is governed exactly by the vis-viva equation:

v(t) = √ μ⊙ ( 2 r(t) − 1 a )

where μ⊙ = G(M⊙ + m) ≈ GM⊙ = 1.32712440041 × 1011 km3 s−2 = k2 AU3 day−2 (with the Gaussian gravitational constant k = 0.01720209895 rad day−1 AU1/2, k2 = 2.959122082855911 × 10−4 AU3/day2, and 1 AU = 149,597,870.700 km), a is the osculating semi-major axis (AU), and r(t) is the instantaneous heliocentric distance obtained by solving Kepler’s equation M(t) = E(t) − e sin E(t):

r(t) = a(1 − e cos E(t)) = a(1 − e2) 1 + e cos ν(t)

Substituting r(t) into the vis-viva relation yields closed-form expressions directly in terms of the eccentric anomaly E(t) or true anomaly ν(t):

v(t) = √ μ⊙ a · 1 + e cos E(t) 1 − e cos E(t) = √ μ⊙ a(1 − e2) (1 + 2e cos ν(t) + e2)

Key kinematic properties and vector decompositions of v(t) include:

  • Apsidal Extrema & Circular Reference Speed:
    • Perihelion speed (r = q = a(1 − e), E = 0°): vmax = vq = √[μ⊙(1 + e) / (a(1 − e))].
    • Aphelion speed (r = Q = a(1 + e), E = 180°): vmin = vQ = √[μ⊙(1 − e) / (a(1 + e))].
    • Circular speed at semi-major axis (r = a, E = 90°, 270°): vc = √(μ⊙ / a) = √(vq vQ) ≈ 29.7846918 / √aAU km/s.
  • Polar (Radial & Transverse) Decomposition in the Orbital Plane:
    vr(t) = ṙ = √ μ⊙ p e sin ν(t),      vθ(t) = rν̇ = √ μ⊙ p (1 + e cos ν(t)),      v(t) = √(vr2 + vθ2)
    where p = a(1 − e2) is the semi-latus rectum, vr > 0 on the outbound leg (0° < ν < 180°), vr < 0 on the inbound leg (180° < ν < 360°), and vr = 0 at the apsides.
  • 3D Inertial Heliocentric J2000.0 Ecliptic Velocity Vector (v(t)): In perifocal coordinates (P toward perihelion, Q along ν = 90°):
    vperifocal(t) = √(μ⊙ a) r(t) [ −sin E(t) P̂ + √(1 − e2) cos E(t) Q̂ ]
    which rotates into the J2000.0 ecliptic frame via the 3-1-3 Euler matrix Rz(−Ω) Rx(−i) Rz(−ω) using the argument of perihelion ω, longitude of the ascending node Ω, and inclination i.

2. Contrast Among Orbital Velocity Metrics

In astrodynamical applications built on MPCORB.DAT, current orbital velocity (v(t)) denotes the instantaneous Keplerian speed at epoch t, distinct from apsidal extrema, circular reference velocity, and topocentric sky-plane angular rates:

Velocity Metric Mathematical Formulation Typical Unit Physical & Astrometric Distinction
Current Orbital Velocity (v(t)) √[μ⊙ (2/r(t) − 1/a)] km/s or AU/day Instantaneous heliocentric inertial speed at epoch t (depends on a, e, and M(t))
Perihelion Velocity (vmax / vq) √[μ⊙(1 + e) / (a(1 − e))] km/s or AU/day Upper bound of orbital speed attained at r = q (M = 0°)
Aphelion Velocity (vmin / vQ) √[μ⊙(1 − e) / (a(1 + e))] km/s or AU/day Lower bound of orbital speed attained at r = Q (M = 180°)
Mean Circular Velocity (vc) √(μ⊙ / a) = 2πa / P km/s or AU/day Speed of a fictitious circular orbit of radius a (not the time-averaged speed for e > 0)
Apparent Sky Motion (μsky) √[(α̇ cos δ)2 + δ̇2] arcsec/min or deg/day Observer-dependent geocentric/topocentric angular rate projected onto the celestial sphere

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database published by the International Astronomical Union’s Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC):

  • Implicit Derived Quantity (Not Tabulated): MPCORB.DAT stores osculating heliocentric Keplerian orbital elements in the J2000.0 ecliptic frame and does not explicitly tabulate v(t) or v(t). Instead, scalar speed v(t) is derived directly from four fixed-width columns in every MPCORB.DAT record (and the full 3D velocity vector v(t) additionally uses ω, Ω, and i from columns 38–69):
    • Packed epoch t0 (columns 21–25, format A5, 0h TT);
    • Mean anomaly at epoch M0 (columns 27–35, format F9.5, degrees);
    • Orbital eccentricity e (columns 71–79, format F9.7, dimensionless);
    • Daily mean motion n (columns 81–91, format F11.8, deg/day) and semi-major axis a (columns 93–103, format F11.7, AU).
  • Two-Epoch Evaluation Convention (t = t0 vs. t = tobs):
    • At Osculating Catalog Epoch (t = t0): Solve Kepler’s equation directly for M0 without temporal propagation.
    • At Current Observation Date (t = tobs, e.g., 2026-11-18): Propagate the mean anomaly linearly via M(tobs) = [M0 + n(tobs − t0)] mod 360° (using n = (180/π)k a−3/2 = 0.9856076686 a−3/2 deg/day if recomputed from a for strict two-body consistency), solve E − e sin E = M(tobs), compute r(tobs) = a(1 − e cos E), and evaluate the vis-viva speed v(tobs).
  • Physical & Observational Significance in Minor-Planet Studies:
    • Impact & Encounter Energetics: Determines the heliocentric kinetic energy per unit mass (v2/2) and asymptotic encounter velocity (v∞ = ∥vast − v⊕∥) during planetary close approaches and B-plane impact analyses.
    • Radar & Spectroscopic Doppler Shift: The radial velocity component vr(t) and geocentric range rate ṙΔ govern relativistic Doppler frequency offsets (Δf/f0 ≈ −2ṙΔ/c) in planetary radar astrometry.
Osculating Two-Body vs. Perturbed N-Body Precision Note: Propagating M(tobs) = M0 + n(tobs − t0) with fixed elements (a, e, i, Ω, ω) yields the exact unperturbed two-body Keplerian velocity from the osculating epoch t0. Over multi-year spans or during close planetary encounters, planetary perturbations alter the osculating elements; high-precision ephemerides therefore integrate the equations of motion numerically (e.g., RK4/RKF45 or Gauss-Radau) before evaluating v(tobs).

The table below summarizes the fixed-width columns (1-based indexing) in a standard 202-character MPCORB.DAT record required to compute v(t) (and optionally the 3D vector v(t)):

Parameter Columns (1-based) 0-Based Slice Fortran Format Unit & Role
Epoch (t0) 21–25 line[20:25] A5 Packed MPC date (0h TT); reference epoch t0

ω, Ω, i (Orientation) 38–69 line[37:69] 3F10.5 Degrees (°); needed only for 3D ecliptic vector v(t)
