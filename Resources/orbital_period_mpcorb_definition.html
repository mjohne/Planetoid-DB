Orbital Period (P) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Kepler’s Third Law, Mean Daily Motion Coupling, Synodic Period, and Evaluation from MPCORB

1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Temporal Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In two-body celestial mechanics and solar system astrometry, the orbital period (specifically the osculating sidereal orbital period, also termed the revolution period or in German astrometry siderische Umlaufzeit)—denoted by P or T—is the time interval required for a minor planet to complete one full 360° (2π rad) revolution around the Sun along its instantaneous osculating Keplerian ellipse relative to the inertial J2000.0 reference frame. It is a dimensional time quantity expressed in days (d), Julian years (yr, 365.25 d), or seconds (s).

By Kepler’s third law (harmonic law) in the heliocentric two-body approximation, the square of the sidereal orbital period P is strictly proportional to the cube of the osculating semi-major axis a and inversely proportional to the heliocentric gravitational parameter μ = G(M☉ + m) ≈ GM☉ = k2 (for massless test bodies m ≪ M☉):

P = 2 π n = 2 π √ a3 μ = 2 π k a3/2 = 360° ndeg/d

where k = 0.01720209895 AU3/2 d−1 is the classical IAU (1976) Gaussian gravitational constant (yielding 2π/k = 365.2568983263 d AU−3/2, the Gaussian sidereal year), and n = k a−3/2 is the osculating mean daily motion. Key physical and dynamical properties of P include:

  • Strict Independence from Orbital Eccentricity (e) and Inclination (i): In pure two-body Newtonian mechanics, P depends exclusively on the semi-major axis a (and hence on the specific orbital energy ϵ = −μ/(2a)), remaining invariant across all bound eccentricities 0 ≤ e < 1.
  • Strictly Positive & Finite Across All Bound Orbits (0 < P < +∞): For any elliptic orbit (a > 0, e < 1), P is strictly finite and positive; as e → 1− at fixed perihelion q, a = q/(1 − e) → +∞ and P → +∞ (parabolic escape limit).
  • Osculating Instantaneous Nature: Because planetary perturbations continuously vary a(t) and n(t), the period derived from a single MPCORB.DAT epoch represents the instantaneous osculating two-body period at that epoch rather than a long-term barycentric or anomalistic mean period.

2. Unit Conversions, Synodic Period & Mean-Motion Resonances

In practical minor planet ephemeris computation and dynamical classification, the sidereal orbital period P is expressed across multiple standard time units and coupled to Earth’s orbital motion:

2.1 Standard Time Unit Representations

Given the semi-major axis a in AU (or mean daily motion n in deg/day), the sidereal period P is evaluated across standard astronomical and SI units via:

Pdays = 360 ndeg/d = 365.2568983263 · aAU3/2   |   Pyr (Julian) = Pdays 365.25   |   Psec = 86,400 · Pdays

Note that using the IAU 2012 exact astronomical unit (1 AU ≡ 149,597,870,700 m) and the IAU 2015 nominal solar mass parameter (GM☉ = 1.3271244 × 1020 m3 s−2) yields kIAU2015 = 0.0172020989484 AU3/2 d−1, agreeing with the classical Gaussian constant k to better than 1 part in 1011.

2.2 Synodic Orbital Period (Psyn)

While P measures revolution relative to the fixed stars, the synodic period Psyn—the mean interval between successive oppositions or conjunctions as observed from Earth (sidereal period P⊕ = 365.256363 d)—is governed by:

1 Psyn = | 1 P − 1 P⊕ |   ⇒   Psyn = P · P⊕ |P − P⊕|

For co-orbital Near-Earth Asteroids (NEAs) with a ≈ 1.000 AU (P ≈ P⊕), Psyn becomes very large (decades to centuries), whereas for distant Trans-Neptunian Objects (P ≫ P⊕), Psyn approaches P⊕ (≈ 1 yr).

2.3 Mean-Motion Commensurabilities (Kirkwood Gaps & Resonances)

Commensurabilities between the minor planet’s orbital period P and Jupiter’s orbital period PJ ≈ 11.862 yr (P / PJ = p / q, equivalently a = aJ (p/q)2/3) define major dynamical populations and Kirkwood gaps across the Main Belt (e.g., 4:1 at 2.065 AU, 3:1 at 2.502 AU, 5:2 at 2.825 AU, 2:1 at 3.278 AU, Hilda group 3:2 at 3.972 AU, and Jovian Trojans 1:1 at 5.204 AU).

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database published by the International Astronomical Union’s Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC):

  • Implicit Derived Quantity (Directly Coupled to Tabulated n and a): The orbital period P is not stored as a separate column in MPCORB.DAT. Instead, MPCORB.DAT explicitly tabulates both the osculating mean daily motion n in degrees per day (columns 81–91, format F11.8) and the osculating semi-major axis a in AU (columns 93–103, format F11.7), which are redundant representations of P via P = 360° / n = (2π/k) a3/2.
  • Precision Comparison Between 360°/n and (2π/k)a3/2: Because n is formatted to 8 decimal places (F11.8) while a is formatted to 7 decimal places (F11.7), for large Main-Belt and outer-solar-system objects (a > 1 AU, n < 1°/d), computing P from a via P = (2π/k) a3/2 generally retains higher relative significant-digit precision than dividing by n (especially for distant TNOs where n ≈ 0.001°/d has only 5 significant digits in F11.8).
  • Strictly Finite Across All MPCORB.DAT Entries: Because MPCORB.DAT contains exclusively bound elliptic orbits (0 ≤ e < 1, a > 0, n > 0), the orbital period P is strictly positive and finite for every record in the catalog. (Unbound hyperbolic/parabolic comets are distributed separately in COMET.DAT.)
Precision & Consistency Note: When propagating mean anomaly M(t) = M0 + n (t − t0) in an MPCORB ephemeris engine, ensure internal self-consistency between n and a: either derive n = kdeg a−3/2 directly from the parsed semi-major axis a (with kdeg = 0.985607668601425° AU3/2 d−1), or use the tabulated n consistently for both P and M(t) so that M(t0 + P) − M(t0) ≡ 360° exactly.
