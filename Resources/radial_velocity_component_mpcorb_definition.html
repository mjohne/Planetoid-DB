Radial Velocity Component

Heliocentric Rate of Change of Orbital Radius in Keplerian Two-Body Motion

1. Formal Definition

In the context of heliocentric two-body orbital mechanics and the Minor Planet Center Orbit Database (MPCORB), the radial velocity component (vr or ṙ) is the scalar projection of a minor planet's instantaneous heliocentric orbital velocity vector (v) onto the heliocentric radial unit vector (r̂ = r / ||r||) directed away from the Sun (the primary focus of the Keplerian ellipse).

Physically, vr quantifies the instantaneous rate of change of the heliocentric distance r with respect to dynamical time (TT / TDB). Unlike spectroscopic radial velocity measured along an observer's topocentric or geocentric line of sight, the orbital radial velocity component in MPCORB-derived ephemerides strictly denotes the component parallel to the Sun–body radius vector within the instantaneous osculating orbital plane.

2. Mathematical Formulation from MPCORB Elements

Each record in MPCORB.DAT provides the osculating heliocentric Keplerian elements at a specified epoch t0: semimajor axis a (in AU, columns 93–103), orbital eccentricity e (columns 71–79), and mean anomaly M0 (in degrees, columns 27–35), along with the mean daily motion n (in deg/day, columns 81–91).

vr = v · r̂ = dr / dt = √(μ / p) · e sin(ν) (Eq. 1)

where:

  • μ = GM⊙ = k2 is the heliocentric gravitational parameter, with the Gaussian gravitational constant k = 0.01720209895 AU3/2 day−1 (corresponding to μ ≈ 2.959122082855911 × 10−4 AU3 day−2, or 1.32712440018 × 1011 km3 s−2).
  • p = a(1 − e2) is the semi-latus rectum (parameter) of the conic section in AU.
  • ν (or f) is the true anomaly, derived by propagating M(t) = M0 + n(t − t0), solving Kepler's equation M = E − e sin(E) for the eccentric anomaly E, and transforming via tan(ν/2) = √((1 + e)/(1 − e)) tan(E/2).

Expressed directly in terms of the eccentric anomaly E and heliocentric distance r = a(1 − e cos E), the radial velocity component avoids trigonometric singularity near circular limits:

vr = (n a2 e sin E) / r = (√(μ a) / r) · e sin E (Eq. 2)

3. Kinematic Behavior & Orbital Regimes

Orbital Position / Anomaly Sign of vr Physical Interpretation
Perihelion (ν = 0°, E = 0°) vr = 0 Minimum heliocentric distance (r = q = a(1−e)); velocity is purely tangential.
Outbound Leg (0° < ν < 180°) vr > 0 Body recedes from the Sun (dr/dt > 0). Peak positive vr occurs at ν = 90° (cos E = e).
Aphelion (ν = 180°, E = 180°) vr = 0 Maximum heliocentric distance (r = Q = a(1+e)); velocity is purely tangential.
Inbound Leg (180° < ν < 360°) vr < 0 Body approaches the Sun (dr/dt < 0). Peak negative vr occurs at ν = 270°.
Circular Orbit (e = 0) vr ≡ 0 Constant heliocentric radius throughout the entire orbit.

4. MPCORB Data Mapping & Unit Conversion

Because MPCORB.DAT stores osculating elements at a standard tabular epoch (typically updated every 200 days at 0h TT) without including non-gravitational or planetary perturbations in the raw two-body formulation, vr computed strictly from MPCORB fields represents the unperturbed Keplerian radial velocity. Converting from astronomical units per day (AU/d) to SI units (km/s) uses the IAU 2012 exact astronomical unit definition (1 AU = 149,597,870.7 km; 1 day = 86,400 s):

vr [km/s] = vr [AU/d] × (149,597,870.7 / 86,400) ≈ vr [AU/d] × 1731.4568368 (Eq. 3)
