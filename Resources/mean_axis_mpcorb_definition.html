“Mean Axis” (2ā / Dmean) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Perturbation-Averaged Full Orbital Span, and Geometric Means Derived from MPCORB

1. Terminological & Astrodynamical Definition

Nomenclature Scope: In celestial mechanics and orbital astrometry, the expression “mean axis” is not a single standalone parameter in the IAU MPCORB.DAT schema, but denotes one of two well-defined physical or geometric quantities derived from an orbit's principal dimensions:
  1. Dynamical Mean Full Axis (2ā): Twice the mean semi-major axis (ā)—the full apsidal diameter of a minor planet's orbit after eliminating short-period planetary perturbations and solar barycentric reflex oscillations.
  2. Geometric Mean Axis (Dmean): The arithmetic or geometric average of the full major axis (2a) and full minor axis (2b) of an instantaneous Keplerian orbital ellipse, i.e., Darith = (2a + 2b)/2 = a + b or Dgeom = √(4ab) = 2√(ab).

Within small Solar System body dynamics and the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) catalog, the primary scientific meaning is the dynamical mean full axis (2ā), representing the perturbation-averaged full spatial diameter of the orbital torus. Whereas the instantaneous osculating major axis (2aosc = q + Q) fluctuates continuously due to gravitational interactions with the major planets, the mean full axis 2ā isolates the secularly invariant scale of the trajectory.

2. Mathematical Formulations

2.1 Dynamical Mean Full Axis (2ā)

In the N-body Solar System, the instantaneous osculating major axis 2aosc(t) obeys Lagrange's variational equation driven by the planetary disturbing function ℛ:

d(2aosc) dt = 4 n a ∂ℛ ∂λ

Averaging over the fast orbital angles (mean longitude λ of the minor planet and λj of the perturbing planets) via canonical Lie-series perturbation theory or numerical filtering over time interval T yields the mean full axis 2ā:

2ā = 2 T ∫t0 − T/2t0 + T/2 aosc(t) dt = ⟨q + Q⟩T

By Poisson's theorem, 2ā is free of secular drift at first and second order in planetary masses outside mean-motion resonances.

2.2 Geometric Mean Axes of an Elliptic Orbit (Darith and Dgeom)

In pure ellipse geometry, comparing the full major axis (2a) and full minor axis (2b = 2a√(1 − e2)) defines two scalar mean diameters:

Darith = 2a + 2b 2 = a + b = a &Bigl(1 + √(1 − e2)&Bigr)
Dgeom = √ (2a)(2b) = 2√(ab) = 2a(1 − e2)1/4

Note the geometric significance of Dgeom: a circle of diameter Dgeom has the exact same enclosed area A = π(Dgeom/2)2 = πab as the orbital ellipse, whereas a circle of diameter Darith approximates the orbital perimeter C ≈ πDarith to first order in e2.

3. Relationship to the MPCORB Database

The MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) catalog published by the IAU Minor Planet Center stores osculating heliocentric elements at a single reference epoch, rather than time-averaged or proper elements:

  • Source Columns in MPCORB.DAT: MPCORB.DAT stores the osculating heliocentric semi-major axis aosc in columns 93–103 (format F11.7, in AU) and the osculating eccentricity eosc in columns 71–79 (format F9.7) at the record's packed Epoch (columns 21–25).
  • Extracting the Dynamical Mean Full Axis (2ā): Because MPCORB.DAT contains instantaneous heliocentric elements, outer Solar System bodies (Centaurs and Trans-Neptunian Objects) exhibit annual/Jovian reflex oscillations in 2aosc of up to ±0.6–3.0 AU. Converting MPCORB osculating elements into barycentric state vectors and averaging over multiple orbital periods yields the invariant dynamical mean full axis 2ā.
  • Computing Geometric Mean Axes from MPCORB: At any given MPCORB epoch, the instantaneous geometric mean diameters Darith and Dgeom are computed directly in Astronomical Units (AU) from columns 71–79 and 93–103 without numerical integration.
Domain Restriction (0 ≤ e < 1): Both the dynamical mean full axis (2ā) and the geometric mean diameters (Darith, Dgeom) require bound elliptic orbits (0 ≤ e < 1), which comprise the standard catalog entries in MPCORB.DAT. At the circular limit (e = 0), all definitions converge to the orbital diameter (2a = 2b = Darith = Dgeom).
