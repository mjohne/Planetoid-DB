Specific Orbital Energy (ε)

Vis-Viva Mechanical Energy Invariant, Gravitational Binding & Energy Partition in MPCORB.DAT

1. Formal Definition

In astrodynamics, celestial mechanics, and derived dynamical invariants computed from the Minor Planet Center Orbit Database (MPCORB), the specific orbital energy (ε or Espec, also termed the vis-viva energy) of an orbiting minor body is the constant sum of its specific kinetic energy (v2 / 2) and specific gravitational potential energy (−μ / r) per unit reduced mass in the heliocentric two-body problem:

ε = v22 − μr = −μ2a ∈ (−∞, +∞) (Eq. 1)

where:

  • a is the osculating semimajor axis tabulated directly in MPCORB.DAT (columns 93–103, in astronomical units AU), satisfying a > 0 for bound elliptical orbits (ε < 0), a → ∞ for parabolic trajectories (ε = 0), and a < 0 for hyperbolic escape trajectories (ε > 0).
  • μ = GM☉(1 + m/M☉) ≈ GM☉ is the heliocentric gravitational parameter, equal to μ = k2 = 2.959122082855911 × 10−4 AU3/d2 (Gaussian gravitational constant squared) or μ = 1.32712440018 × 1020 m3/s2 = 1.32712440018 × 1011 km3/s2 = 887.12815 km2/s2 · AU in SI/astronautical units.
  • r = ‖r‖ and v = ‖v‖ are the instantaneous heliocentric distance and speed; because ε depends solely on a and is strictly independent of orbital eccentricity e and inclination i, all Keplerian orbits sharing the same semimajor axis a possess identical specific orbital energy.
Figure 1: Heliocentric gravitational potential well U(r) = −μ / r illustrating the conservation of specific orbital energy ε = v2 / 2 − μ / r = −μ / (2a). For any bound minor planet in MPCORB.DAT (a > 0), ε < 0 is constant throughout the orbit: as heliocentric distance r oscillates between perihelion q = a(1 − e) and aphelion Q = a(1 + e), specific kinetic energy K = v2 / 2 = ε + μ / r trades off exactly against specific potential energy U(r).

2. Evaluation from MPCORB.DAT & Vis-Viva Conservation

In MPCORB.DAT, the semimajor axis a is tabulated directly in columns 93–103 (in AU, formatted as F11.7), and the mean daily motion n is tabulated in columns 81–91 (in deg/day, formatted as F11.8). Because Kepler's third law links them via nrad2 a3 = μ (where nrad = n(π / 180°) in rad/day), the specific orbital energy ε can be evaluated directly from a or cross-checked against n:

ε = −μ2a = −12 (μ nrad)2/3 = −GM☉2 aAU · AU (Eq. 2)

Equating Eq. (1) and Eq. (2) yields the vis-viva equation, which determines the instantaneous heliocentric orbital speed v(r) at any distance r = a(1 − e cos E), as well as the specific kinetic energy K(r) = v(r)2 / 2 and specific potential energy U(r) = −μ / r:

v(r) = √2 &left(ε + μr&right) = √μ &left(2r − 1a&right),    ⟨K⟩t = −ε = μ2a,    ⟨U⟩t = 2ε = −μa (Eq. 3)

where ⟨K⟩t and ⟨U⟩t denote the time-averaged specific kinetic and potential energies over one complete orbital period P = 360° / n, satisfying the virial theorem (2⟨K⟩t + ⟨U⟩t = 0).

3. Orbital Energy Regimes & Dynamical Classification

Orbital Regime Specific Energy (ε) Semimajor Axis (a) & Eccentricity (e) Dynamical Behavior in Solar System
Bound Elliptical (Minor Planets) ε = −μ / (2a) < 0 0 < a < ∞,   0 ≤ e < 1 Closed periodic orbit; body cannot escape solar gravity (v < vesc = √2μ / r).
Parabolic Escape Boundary ε = 0 a → ∞,   e = 1 Zero asymptotic speed (v∞ = 0); body reaches r → ∞ with vanishing kinetic energy.
Hyperbolic Escape / Interstellar ε = +v∞2 / 2 = −μ / (2a) > 0 a < 0,   e > 1 Unbound trajectory (e.g., 1I/‘Oumuamua, 2I/Borisov) with excess speed v∞ = √2ε > 0.

4. Astrodynamical & Observational Significance

  • Binding Energy & Gravitational Capture: The magnitude |ε| = μ / (2a) represents the specific mechanical energy required to unbind the minor body from the Sun's gravitational well and eject it to infinity (ε = 0). Conversely, planetary close encounters (such as Jupiter flybys of Jupiter-family comets or Oort-cloud comets) alter ε by Δε = −μ Δ(1/a) / 2, either capturing long-period comets (Δε < 0) or ejecting bodies onto hyperbolic escape trajectories (ε + Δε > 0).
  • Mean-Motion Resonances & Kirkwood Gaps: Because ε is uniquely tied to a (and orbital period P ∝ (−ε)−3/2), resonant asteroid populations (such as the Hilda group at 3:2 or Jupiter Trojans at 1:1) occupy narrow bands of specific orbital energy, whereas chaotic diffusion near mean-motion resonances pumps eccentricity without changing ε until planetary encounters occur.
  • Tisserand Parameter Invariant: In the circular restricted three-body problem (Sun–Jupiter–asteroid), the Tisserand invariant TJ = aJ / a + 2√(a / aJ)(1 − e2) cos i couples the dimensionless specific energy (aJ / a = ε / εJ) with angular momentum orientation to separate dynamical comet families from main-belt asteroids.
