1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Geometric Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In two-body Keplerian celestial mechanics, the angular momentum factor (also termed eccentricity angular-momentum reduction factor or semi-minor axis ratio, denoted fh(e) = √(1 − e2)) is the dimensionless function of orbital eccentricity e (0 ≤ e < 1) that scales the circular-orbit specific angular momentum hc = √(μ⊙a) to the actual specific relative orbital angular momentum h = ‖r × v‖ of an elliptic orbit with the same semi-major axis a.

Because the semi-latus rectum satisfies p = a(1 − e2) and h = √(μ⊙p), the specific orbital angular momentum and areal velocity dA/dt = h/2 factorize as:

Key Dynamical & Geometric Identities

  • Maximum Angular Momentum at Fixed Energy: Because 0 < √(1 − e2) ≤ 1 on 0 ≤ e < 1, a circular orbit (e = 0) possesses the maximum possible specific angular momentum hc = √(μ⊙a) for a given specific orbital energy ε = −μ⊙/(2a).
  • Geometric Mean of Apsidal Factors: √(1 − e2) = √[(1 − e)(1 + e)] = √(qQ) / a, so the semi-minor axis b = √(qQ) is the geometric mean of perihelion and aphelion distances.
  • Delaunay Canonical Action Variable: In Hamiltonian celestial mechanics, the second Delaunay momentum is G = L√(1 − e2) = h (where L = √(μ⊙a)), and the Lidov–Kozai integral Θ = (1 − e2) cos2 i couples eccentricity and inclination oscillations.
  • Collinear Degeneracy (e → 1−): As e → 1− at fixed a, fh(e) → 0, corresponding to radial rectilinear infall (h → 0, q → 0).

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

  • Direct Evaluation from Eccentricity e: Evaluated directly from columns 71–79 (F9.7) of MPCORB.DAT via sqrt((1.0 − e) * (1.0 + e)).
  • Orbital Plane Velocity & State-Vector Transformations: In the perifocal coordinate frame (P, Q), the velocity vector is v = (μ⊙/h)[−sin ν P + (e + cos ν) Q], where h is scaled by √(1 − e2).
  • Dynamical Family & Secular Resonance Classification: In asteroid-family identification (Hierarchical Clustering Method in proper elements ap, ep, sin ip) and Tisserand-parameter filtering, √(1 − e2) quantifies the angular-momentum deficit relative to a circular coplanar orbit.
