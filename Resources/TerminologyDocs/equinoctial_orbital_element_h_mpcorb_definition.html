Equinoctial Orbital Element (h) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Non-Singular Broucke–Cefola & Walker Formulations, Equinoctial Reference Frame, and Evaluation from MPCORB

1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Geometric Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In non-singular celestial mechanics and perturbation theory, the equinoctial orbital element h (the quadrature component of the eccentricity vector in the equinoctial coordinate frame, paired with k = e cos(Ω + Iω))—is a dimensionless canonical/non-singular parameter designed to eliminate the 0/0 coordinate singularity of the classical Keplerian argument of perihelion ω at zero eccentricity (e → 0) and the longitude of the ascending node Ω at zero inclination (i → 0°).

In the standard direct equinoctial orbital element set (a, h, k, p, q, λ) formulated by Broucke & Cefola (1972) and Walker, Ireland & Owens (1985), h and its conjugate cosine partner k are defined in terms of the classical Keplerian eccentricity e, argument of perihelion ω, longitude of the ascending node Ω, and retrograde factor I ∈ {+1, −1}:

h = e sin(ω + I Ω) = e sin ϖeq  ,     k = e cos(ω + I Ω) = e cos ϖeq

where I = +1 for prograde (direct) orbits (0° ≤ i < 180°, standard direct equinoctial convention) and I = −1 for retrograde equinoctial formulations (0° < i ≤ 180°, shifting the coordinate singularity from i = 0° to i = 180°), and ϖeq = ω + IΩ is the equinoctial longitude of perihelion. Geometrically, h is the projection of the dimensionless Laplace–Runge–Lenz eccentricity vector e onto the ĝ basis axis of the equinoctial frame (f̂, ĝ, ŵ):

h = e · ĝ  ,     k = e · f̂  ,     e = √(h2 + k2)  ,     ϖeq = atan2(h, k)

Key mathematical and astrodynamical properties of the equinoctial element h include:

  • Strict Unit-Disk Boundedness (−1 < h < +1, h2 + k2 = e2 < 1): For all bound elliptic orbits (0 ≤ e < 1), the pair (k, h) lies strictly inside the open unit disk k2 + h2 < 1, attaining h = 0 whenever e = 0 (circular orbit) or ω + IΩ = 0°, 180°.
  • Complete Removal of Near-Circular & Low-Inclination Singularities: In Lagrange’s planetary equations and Gauss’s variational equations, classical derivatives dω/dt and dΩ/dt contain divisors e and sin i that blow up as e → 0 or i → 0°. The time derivatives dh/dt and dk/dt remain smooth, analytic, and non-singular across e = 0 and i = 0°.
  • Distinction from Specific Angular Momentum (hang) and Absolute Magnitude (H): Care must be taken in notation: lowercase h denotes the equinoctial eccentricity sine component (dimensionless), whereas uppercase H in MPCORB.DAT (columns 9–13) denotes the photometric absolute magnitude (mag), and hang = √(μp) denotes specific orbital angular momentum (AU2/d).

2. Complete Equinoctial Set & Position/Velocity Mapping

Within the six-element equinoctial state (a, h, k, p, q, λ), h operates alongside the remaining five elements to permit singularity-free propagation and Cartesian state conversion:

p = tanI(i/2) sin Ω  ,    q = tanI(i/2) cos Ω  ,    λ = M + ω + I Ω

where λ is the mean longitude (or equinoctial mean longitude for I = −1). Given (a, h, k, λ), the generalized equinoctial Kepler’s equation solves directly for the eccentric longitude F = E + ω + IΩ without ever referencing ω or Ω individually:

λ = F + h cos F − k sin F  ,     r = a (1 − k cos F − h sin F)

Furthermore, in secular perturbation theory (Laplace–Lagrange theory for asteroid families and proper elements), the pairs (k, h) = e(cos ϖ, sin ϖ) and (q, p) = tan(i/2)(cos Ω, sin Ω) serve as the fundamental complex state variables z = k + ih = e exp(iϖ) whose linear eigenmodes define proper eccentricity ep and forced eccentricity eforced.

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database published by the International Astronomical Union’s Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC):

  • Implicit Derived Quantity (Not Tabulated): MPCORB.DAT distributes orbits exclusively in classical Keplerian elements and does not store h (or k, p, q, λ) as explicit columns. Instead, h is derived directly from three osculating angular/eccentricity columns in every record:
    • Argument of perihelion ω (columns 38–46, format F9.5, degrees);
    • Longitude of the ascending node Ω (columns 49–57, format F9.5, degrees);
    • Numeric eccentricity e (columns 71–79, format F9.7, dimensionless);
    • Optional: Inclination i (columns 60–68, format F9.5, degrees) if switching to the retrograde equinoctial chart (I = −1 when i > 90°).
  • Convention Specification (I = +1 Direct vs. I = −1 Retrograde): Standard direct equinoctial elements use I = +1 (h = e sin(ω + Ω)) universally across all prograde and moderately inclined orbits, which covers over 99.98% of MPCORB.DAT (where i = 180° is absent). When working with retrograde Centaurs or Damocloids (i > 90°), explicitly document whether the direct (I = +1) or retrograde (I = −1) equinoctial chart is used.
  • Applications in MPCORB Orbit Fitting & Propagation:
    • Differential Correction of Low-e / Low-i Main-Belt & TNO Orbits: Classical TNOs (e.g., cold classical Kuiper Belt objects with e ≈ 0.01, i ≈ 1°) have poorly constrained ω and Ω individually, whereas (h, k) remain tightly constrained and well-conditioned in least-squares covariance matrices.
    • Encke & Variation-of-Parameters Integrators: Eliminates small-divisor stiffness in Gauss/Lagrange planetary equations during numerical integration of nearly circular asteroids.
Implementation & Angle-Wrapping Note: When computing h = e * Math.Sin(omega + I * Omega) in double precision, convert ω and Ω from degrees to radians first, and optionally reduce (ω + IΩ) mod 360° prior to trigonometric evaluation to preserve full 53-bit significand accuracy. Always pair h with k = e cos(ω + IΩ) when inverting back to e = Math.Hypot(h, k) and ϖeq = Math.Atan2(h, k).
