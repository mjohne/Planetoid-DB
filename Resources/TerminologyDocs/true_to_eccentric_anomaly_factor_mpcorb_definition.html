True-to-Eccentric Anomaly Conversion Factor (√[(1 + e)/(1 − e)]) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Tangent Half-Angle Mapping Between Eccentric and True Anomaly, and Derivation from MPCORB

1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Geometric Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In two-body Keplerian celestial mechanics, the true-to-eccentric anomaly conversion factor (also termed Gauss’s anomaly factor or half-angle anomaly multiplier, denoted fν,E(e) = √[(1 + e) / (1 − e)]) is the dimensionless function of orbital eccentricity e (0 ≤ e < 1) that relates the tangent of half the true anomaly tan(ν/2) to the tangent of half the eccentric anomaly tan(E/2).

From the geometric projections of the position vector in the orbital plane (r cos ν = a(cos E − e) and r sin ν = a√(1 − e2) sin E), applying the tangent half-angle identity tan(θ/2) = sin θ / (1 + cos θ) yields the exact bijection on (−π, π):

tan ν2 = √ 1 + e 1 − e tan E2   ⟺   fν,E(e) ≡ tan(ν/2) tan(E/2) = √ 1 + e 1 − e

Mathematical & Astrodynamical Properties

  • Quadrant-Preserving Bijection via atan2: Because fν,E(e) ≥ 1 > 0 for all 0 ≤ e < 1, ν and E always lie in the same half-plane and quadrant, enabling the singularity-free implementation ν = 2 atan2(√(1 + e) sin(E/2), √(1 − e) cos(E/2)).
  • Inverse Mapping (ν → E): Converting observed or target true anomaly ν back to eccentric anomaly E uses the exact reciprocal factor tan(E/2) = √[(1 − e)/(1 + e)] tan(ν/2).
  • Exact Algebraic Identity with Perihelion Velocity Factor: fν,E(e) = √[(1 + e)/(1 − e)] = vq / vc = √(Q / q), linking angular anomaly stretching near perihelion directly to the apsidal speed amplification.
  • Hyperbolic Analog (e > 1): For hyperbolic trajectories, the trigonometric eccentric anomaly E is replaced by the hyperbolic eccentric anomaly H, giving tan(ν/2) = √[(e + 1)/(e − 1)] tanh(H/2).

2. Contrast Across Anomaly-Conversion Formulations

Conversion Formulation Equation Numerical Properties
Half-Angle Factor Form tan(ν/2) = √[(1 + e)/(1 − e)] tan(E/2) Single precomputed factor per orbit; fast when E ≠ ±π
Two-Argument atan2 Form ν = 2 atan2(√(1 + e) sin(E/2), √(1 − e) cos(E/2)) Zero division-by-zero risk at aphelion (E = π); quadrant-exact
Direct Cartesian-Projection Form ν = atan2(√(1 − e2) sin E, cos E − e) Avoids half-angle trigonometric calls; uses √(1 − e2)

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

  • Second Step of Every MPCORB.DAT Ephemeris Propagation: MPCORB.DAT provides mean anomaly M at epoch (columns 27–35, F9.5, degrees), mean daily motion n (columns 81–91, F11.8), and eccentricity e (columns 71–79, F9.7). Propagating to any observation time t first advances M(t) = M0 + n(t − t0), solves Kepler’s equation M(t) = E − e sin E for E, and then applies √[(1 + e)/(1 − e)] to obtain true anomaly ν.
  • Batch Ephemeris Optimization: Because √[(1 + e)/(1 − e)] depends only on e, it is evaluated once per minor-planet record and reused across all time steps of an ephemeris table or sky-plane search grid.
