Readable Designation (Columns 167–194)

Human-Readable Permanent Number, WGSBN Name & Provisional Designation Field (FORTRAN A28) in MPCORB.DAT

1. Formal Definition

In the Minor Planet Center Orbit Database (MPCORB), the Readable Designation (Desigread, officially specified by the IAU Minor Planet Center as the “Readable designation” field in columns 167–194, 1-indexed, 28-character ASCII string formatted as FORTRAN A28, with 0-based substring slice [166..194]) is the unencoded, human-readable IAU minor planet identifier—comprising either a permanently numbered designation (with optional IAU-approved name) or a provisional/survey discovery designation—that corresponds bijectively to the 7-character base-62 packed primary key in columns 1–7:

Desigread = trim(Record[166 : 194]) ⇔ Unpack7(Record[0 : 7]) (Eq. 1)

where:

  • Field Boundaries: Columns 167–194 span 28 ASCII characters, preceded by a space at column 166 (immediately after the 4-hexdigit classification/attribute flags in columns 162–165) and followed immediately (with no intervening blank column—or utilizing column 194 as trailing padding) by the 8-character Date of Last Observation (YYYYMMDD) in columns 195–202.
  • Dual Internal Sub-Alignment Architecture: Unlike purely left-justified strings, the 28-character field exhibits a structured internal alignment distinguishing numbered minor planets from unnumbered provisional discoveries:
    • Numbered Minor Planets ((N) Name or (N)): The parenthesized permanent catalog number (N) is aligned so that its closing parenthesis ) sits consistently near column 174 (for 1- to 5-digit numbers) or shifts right for 6-digit numbers, followed by a single space and the official IAU name (up to 18 characters, e.g., (1) Ceres, (99942) Apophis, (136199) Eris, or (654321) if numbered but unnamed).
    • Unnumbered Provisional Designations: Right-shifted into the secondary name sub-column starting around column 176 (leaving leading spaces in columns 167–175, e.g., 2025 QA14 or 6344 P-L).

3. Fixed-Width Format Specifications & Parsing Rules

  • Exact Column Slice: Columns 167–194 (1-indexed), corresponding to 0-based substring line[166..194] (length 28 characters).
  • Critical Boundary Check at Column 194: Because the Date of Last Observation (YYYYMMDD) begins immediately at column 195 (0-based index 194), the longest possible 28-character designation (such as (123456) SeventeenCharName, length 26, or a 27-character designation ending at column 193) leaves column 194 as a space separator; however, robust parsers must slice strictly by fixed column offsets line[166..194].Trim() rather than splitting on whitespace!
  • Bijection with Columns 1–7: For any valid record in MPCORB.DAT, unpacking the 7-character primary designation in columns 1–7 (e.g., 00001 → (1); K25Q14A → 2025 QA14) must match the designation prefix of columns 167–194, while columns 167–194 uniquely supply the human-readable proper name (e.g., Ceres, Apophis), which is not encoded anywhere else in the 202-column record.

4. Worked Examples from MPCORB.DAT

The following two records from MPCORB.DAT (epoch K25BL = 2025 Nov 21.0 TT = JD 2461000.5) contrast a permanently numbered, named minor planet with an unnumbered provisional discovery:

Example 1: (1) Ceres — Numbered & Named Minor Planet ( (1) Ceres)

Excerpt of MPCORB.DAT columns 151–202 for 00001: 

JPL 48     1000   (1) Ceres                  20251018
  • Raw 28-Char Slice (Cols 167–194): " (1) Ceres " → trimmed: "(1) Ceres".
  • Structural Breakdown: 3 leading spaces align (1) so ) sits at column 172 (or 174 in newer builds), followed by a space and the name Ceres, padded with trailing spaces up to column 194 immediately before 20251018 (col 195).

Example 2: 2025 QA14 — Provisional Discovery Designation ( 2025 QA14)

Excerpt of MPCORB.DAT columns 151–202 for K25Q14A: 

MPCLINUX   8403            2025 QA14          20250911
  • Raw 28-Char Slice (Cols 167–194): " 2025 QA14 " → trimmed: "2025 QA14".
  • Provisional Cycle Calculation: Half-month Q = August 16–31, 2025; second letter A = 1st in cycle; cycle number 14 → 14 × 25 + 1 = 351st minor planet reported in the second half of August 2025.
