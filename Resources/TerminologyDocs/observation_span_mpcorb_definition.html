Observational Arc Geometry & Covariance • MPCORB Reference

Observation Span (Δtarc, Columns 128–136)

Polymorphic Year-Range (YYYY-YYYY) & Single-Opposition Day-Arc (NNNNN days) Specification in MPCORB.DAT

1. Formal Definition

In the Minor Planet Center Orbit Database (MPCORB), the Observation Span (also termed the observational arc or “Year of first and last observation / Arc length”, stored in columns 128–136, 1-indexed, as a 9-character polymorphic ASCII field formatted as FORTRAN A9, with 0-based substring slice [127..136]) quantifies the temporal baseline Δtarc between the earliest (tfirst) and latest (tlast) accepted astrometric observations used in the orbit determination:

Δtarc = tlast − tfirst,    Field[127 : 136] = { YYYYfirst-YYYYlast  (Nopp > 1),    I5(Ndays) days  (Nopp = 1) (Eq. 1)

where:

  • Nopp is the Number of Oppositions stored immediately beforehand in columns 124–126 (I3, 0-based slice [123..126]).
  • Multi-Opposition Regime (Nopp > 1): Columns 128–136 contain a 9-character hyphen-delimited Gregorian year interval YYYY-YYYY (e.g., 1801-2025 or 2004-2021), encoding the calendar year of tfirst and the calendar year of tlast (the latter matching the leading 4 digits of the Date of Last Observation in columns 195–202).
  • Single-Opposition Regime (Nopp = 1): Columns 128–136 switch representation to an integer day count right-justified in 5 columns followed by a space and the literal unit days: NNNNN days (e.g., 14 days, 237 days, or 1 days), giving the exact integer-rounded temporal span Ndays = ⌊(tlast − tfirst) / 1 d + 0.5⌋.
Index 4 = '-': Hyphen separates start & end years. • Year Span: ΔY = Ylast − Yfirst (up to >220 years). • Orbital Precision: Multi-year baseline breaks degeneracy between (a, e) and nodal/apsidal rates, yielding σa/a ∼ 10−8–10−10 (σn ∝ Δtarc−3/2). Note: Two oppositions in the same calendar year still format as YYYY-YYYY (e.g. "2024-2024"). Branch B: Single-Opposition (Nopp = 1) Format: "NNNNN days" (e.g. " 14 days") • Suffix " days": Slice [0..5] parses as integer Ndays. • First Obs Date: tfirst = tlast (Cols 195–202) − Ndays. • Short-Arc Regime: When Ndays < 30 d, curvature is weakly constrained (σn ∝ Δtarc−3/2), causing rapid along-track ephemeris dispersion. Note: Same-night discovery arcs appear as " 0 days" or " 1 days".

2. Astrodynamical Scaling Laws Governed by Observation Span

In orbit determination theory, the observational arc length Δtarc is the dominant geometric lever arm determining the formal covariance matrix of the orbital elements:

  • Mean-Motion and Semi-Major Axis Uncertainty Scaling: For Nobs uniformly distributed astrometric measurements of individual positional precision σast spanning a single-opposition arc Δtarc (short compared to the orbital period P), the formal standard uncertainties in mean motion n and semi-major axis a scale according to the classical Gauss–Moulton / Bernstein–Khushalani power laws:
    σn ∝ σast√Nobs · Δtarc3/2,    σaa = 23 σnn ∝ Δtarc−3/2 (Eq. 2)
    Doubling the observation span Δtarc reduces the uncertainty in a and n by a factor of 23/2 ≈ 2.83, whereas doubling Nobs without extending Δtarc yields only a √2 ≈ 1.41 improvement.
  • Transition from Single-Opposition (Ndays) to Multi-Opposition (YYYY-YYYY): Once an asteroid is recovered at a second solar opposition (Nopp ≥ 2, typically Δtarc ≳ 1–2 years), the baseline spans a substantial fraction of a full heliocentric revolution, collapsing the range-rate degeneracy and transitioning the MPCORB representation from an integer day count (NNNNN days) to a calendar year range (YYYY-YYYY).
  • Secular Non-Gravitational Parameter Detectability: Multi-decade observation spans (e.g., Δtarc ≥ 15–30 years) allow secular orbital perturbations such as the Yarkovsky transverse drift (da/dt, quadratic in time Δλ ∝ Δtarc2) to accumulate above astrometric noise, enabling direct dynamical mass/density constraints on near-Earth asteroids.

3. Parsing Grammar & Edge Cases in MPCORB.DAT

Case / Feature Exact Pattern in Cols 128–136 Parsing Rule & Astrodynamical Interpretation
Multi-Opposition Year Range YYYY-YYYY
(e.g., 1801-2025)		 Detected when span[4] == '-' (and Nopp > 1). Start year = int(span[0..4]), end year = int(span[5..9]). Note that approximate calendar span is Ylast − Yfirst years, while the exact end date tlast is given in columns 195–202.
Single-Opposition Day Span NNNNN days
(e.g., 14 days)		 Detected when span.EndsWith("days") (and Nopp == 1). Integer arc days = int(span[0..5].Trim()). Exact first observation date is tfirst = tlast − Ndays.
Zero-Day Single-Night Arc 0 days Occurs when all Nobs measurements (e.g., a discovery tracklet of 3–4 exposures over 45 minutes) fall on the same UTC calendar date (tfirst and tlast have identical YYYYMMDD).
Long Single-Opposition Arc (>1 Year) 412 days Distant objects (Centaurs, TNOs, Jupiter Trojans) or favorable low-eccentricity NEAs can remain observable for >365 days within a single solar opposition; columns 128–136 continue using NNNNN days (up to 5 digits, i.e., 99999 days) until Nopp ≥ 2.

4. Worked Examples from MPCORB.DAT

The following two records from MPCORB.DAT (epoch K25BL = 2025 Nov 21.0 TT = JD 2461000.5) contrast the multi-opposition YYYY-YYYY format with the single-opposition NNNNN days format:

Example 1: (1) Ceres — 224-Year Multi-Opposition Span (1801-2025)

Excerpt of MPCORB.DAT columns 118–202 for 00001: 

... 11432  98 1801-2025 0.61 M-v 30h JPL 48     1000   (1) Ceres                  20251018
  • Parsed Span (Cols 128–136): 1801-2025 (Nopp = 98 oppositions, Nobs = 11,432 observations).
  • Astrodynamical Interpretation: Spans 224 years from Giuseppe Piazzi's discovery observations in January 1801 through 2025 October 18 (20251018 in columns 195–202), constraining Ceres's semi-major axis to sub-meter accuracy.

Example 2: Single-Opposition Discovery — Integer Day Arc ( 14 days)

Excerpt of MPCORB.DAT columns 118–202 for a single-opposition NEO: 

...    27   1    14 days 0.28 M-v 38k MPCLINUX   8403            2025 QA14          20250911
  • Parsed Span (Cols 128–136): " 14 days" → Ndays = 14 days (Nopp = 1, Nobs = 27).
  • Astrodynamical Interpretation: Subtracting 14 days from tlast = 2025-09-11 (20250911 in columns 195–202) yields the exact discovery date tfirst = 2025-08-28 UTC.
