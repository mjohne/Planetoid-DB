Areal Velocity

Rate of Area Swept by the Heliocentric Position Vector (Kepler's Second Law)

1. Formal Definition

In heliocentric celestial mechanics and the analysis of osculating orbits from the Minor Planet Center Orbit Database (MPCORB), the areal velocity (dA / dt or Ȧ) is the rate at which the heliocentric position vector r of a minor planet sweeps out geometric area A within its instantaneous osculating orbital plane.

By Kepler's Second Law of Planetary Motion (the law of equal areas), the areal velocity of a body moving under a central inverse-square gravitational force is strictly constant throughout its entire unperturbed orbit. Consequently, unlike radial, tangential, or angular velocity—all of which oscillate with true anomaly—the areal velocity is a fundamental dynamical invariant of the osculating orbit directly proportional to the specific orbital angular momentum h.

2. Mathematical Formulation from MPCORB Elements

Consider an infinitesimal triangular sector swept by the radius vector r over time interval dt, with differential area dA = ½ ||r × dr|| = ½ r2 dν. Dividing by dt yields the areal velocity vector Ȧ and its scalar magnitude Ȧ:

Ȧ = ½ (r × v) = ½ h  ⇒  dA / dt = h / 2 = ½ √(μ a(1 − e2)) (Eq. 1)

where:

  • a is the osculating semimajor axis in AU (MPCORB.DAT cols 93–103).
  • e is the osculating orbital eccentricity (MPCORB.DAT cols 71–79).
  • h = √(μ p) is the magnitude of the specific orbital angular momentum vector in AU2 day−1.
  • μ = k2 is the heliocentric gravitational parameter (k = 0.01720209895 AU3/2 day−1).

Equivalently, since the total area of the orbital ellipse is Atotal = π a b = π a2 √(1 − e2) and the orbital period is P = 2π / n, the areal velocity can also be written directly in terms of the mean motion n (in rad/day):

dA / dt = Atotal / P = ½ n a2 √(1 − e2) (Eq. 2)
