Is Potentially Hazardous? (IsPHA)

Earth MOID Proximity Threshold, Absolute Magnitude Cutoff & MPCORB Bitmask in MPCORB.DAT

1. Formal Definition

In planetary defense, near-Earth object (NEO) population dynamics, and derived classification flags computed from the Minor Planet Center Orbit Database (MPCORB), the boolean predicate “Is potentially hazardous?” identifies whether a minor planet belongs to the official IAU Minor Planet Center / NASA CNEOS class of Potentially Hazardous Asteroids (PHAs). A body is classified as a PHA (IsPHA = true) if and only if it simultaneously satisfies two strict orbital and photometric criteria:

IsPHA ⇔ &left( MOID⊕ ≤ 0.05 AU &right) ∧ &left( H ≤ 22.0 mag &right) (Eq. 1)

where:

  • MOID⊕ is the Earth Minimum Orbit Intersection Distance (in astronomical units, AU), defined as the global minimum Euclidean distance between the osculating Keplerian ellipse of the minor planet and the osculating Keplerian ellipse of Earth (0.05 AU = 7,479,893.5 km ≈ 19.46 LD, where 1 LD = 384,400 km is the mean Earth–Moon lunar distance).
  • H is the absolute visual magnitude tabulated directly in MPCORB.DAT (columns 9–13, formatted as F5.2 in the IAU H–G magnitude system). Under the canonical PHA calibration visual geometric albedo of pV = 0.14, the brightness cutoff H ≤ 22.0 mag corresponds to a minimum effective diameter threshold of:
    D(H = 22.0, pV = 0.14) = 1329.0√0.14 · 10−22.0 / 5 = 3551.90 × 3.98107 × 10−5 = 141.4 m ≈ 140 m.
  • Direct Catalog Flag in MPCORB.DAT: In addition to first-principles evaluation via Eq. (1), MPCORB.DAT encodes a 4-character hexadecimal orbital-type bitmask in columns 162–165, where bit 0x0800 (mask (flags & 0x0800) != 0) explicitly designates a Potentially Hazardous Asteroid (PHA).
Figure 1: Two-dimensional classification domain for Potentially Hazardous Asteroids (PHAs) in (MOID⊕, H) space. An object in MPCORB.DAT satisfies “Is potentially hazardous?” if and only if its Earth Minimum Orbit Intersection Distance is ≤ 0.05 AU (≈ 19.46 lunar distances) and its absolute magnitude is H ≤ 22.0 mag (D ≳ 140 m for pV = 0.14).

2. Mathematical Formulation of MOID⊕ & MPCORB Bitmask Decoding

Unlike the perihelion distance q = a(1 − e), which tests only radial overlap with 1 AU, MOID⊕ accounts for the full three-dimensional orientation (i, Ω, ω) of both orbits. Parameterizing the asteroid's heliocentric position vector rast(f1) by its true anomaly f1 ∈ [0, 2π) and Earth's heliocentric position vector r⊕(f2) by f2 ∈ [0, 2π) (neglecting instantaneous orbital phase along each ellipse), MOID⊕ is the global minimum of the squared Euclidean distance function D2(f1, f2) = ‖rast(f1) − r⊕(f2)‖2:

MOID⊕ = min(f1, f2) ∈ [0, 2π)2 ‖rast(f1) − r⊕(f2)‖2,    s.t.   ∂D2∂f1 = 0,   ∂D2∂f2 = 0 (Eq. 2)

Because two confocal ellipses can possess up to four local distance minima (including two near the ascending and descending nodes of the asteroid's orbit relative to the ecliptic), computing MOID⊕ requires global minimization over (f1, f2)—typically solved via Baluev–Mikryukov algebraic resultant methods or coarse grid seeding followed by damped Newton–Raphson refinement. In MPCORB.DAT, columns 162–165 provide a 4-hex-digit bitfield summarizing the MPC's precomputed dynamical and PHA flags:

Hex Mask (Cols 162–165) Bit Index Orbit / Hazard Classification Defining Orbital & Photometric Conditions
0x0002 Bit 1 Aten a < 1.0 AU and aphelion distance Q = a(1 + e) > 0.983 AU (Earth-crossing NEO).
0x0003 Bits 0–1 Apollo a ≥ 1.0 AU and perihelion distance q = a(1 − e) ≤ 1.017 AU (Earth-crossing NEO).
0x0004 Bit 2 Amor a > 1.0 AU and 1.017 AU < q ≤ 1.300 AU (Earth-approaching NEO).
0x0800 Bit 11 Potentially Hazardous Asteroid (PHA) MOID⊕ ≤ 0.05 AU and H ≤ 22.0 mag (subset of NEOs; predominantly Apollos and Atens, plus low-q Amors).

3. Necessary Perihelion/Aphelion Bounds & Astrodynamical Caveats

  • Necessary (but Not Sufficient) Radial Intersection Bounds: Because Earth's orbit spans heliocentric distances r⊕ ∈ [q⊕, Q⊕] = [0.98329 AU, 1.01671 AU], any asteroid with MOID⊕ ≤ 0.05 AU must satisfy q ≤ Q⊕ + 0.05 = 1.06671 AU and Q ≥ q⊕ − 0.05 = 0.93329 AU. However, an object with q < 1.0 AU and high inclination i whose nodes lie far from 1 AU can still have MOID⊕ > 0.05 AU and thus fail the PHA criterion.
  • PHA Status Does Not Imply Imminent Impact: A PHA classification measures purely geometric orbit proximity (MOID⊕ ≤ 0.05 AU) and minimum impact energy (D ≳ 140 m, corresponding to kinetic energies Ekin ≳ 60–100 Mt TNT capable of regional/continental devastation), independent of whether Earth and the asteroid currently occupy the close-approach point simultaneously.
  • Secular Evolution of MOID⊕: Planetary gravitational perturbations (primarily from Jupiter, Venus, and Earth) cause an asteroid's osculating (e, i, Ω, ω)—and therefore MOID⊕—to evolve over decadal to millennial timescales. Consequently, non-PHA NEAs near the boundary (MOID⊕ ≈ 0.05–0.08 AU, H ≤ 22.0) can transition into or out of PHA status across successive MPCORB.DAT epochs.

4. Worked Examples from MPCORB.DAT

The following two numbered Near-Earth Objects from MPCORB.DAT (epoch K25BL = 2025 Nov 21.0 TT = JD 2461000.5) demonstrate how both the dual condition (MOID⊕ ≤ 0.05 AU and H ≤ 22.0 mag) and the MPCORB.DAT bitmask distinguish a PHA from a non-PHA NEO:

Example 1: (99942) Apophis — Aten-Class Potentially Hazardous Asteroid (IsPHA = true)

Excerpt from MPCORB.DAT (columns 1–103 and hex flag 0802 in columns 162–165): 

99942   19.09  0.15 K25BL 204.38214  126.67418  203.89612    3.34092  0.1914921  1.11268941   0.9224382  [... flags: 0802]
  • Extracted Parameters: H = 19.09 mag (columns 9–13), a = 0.9224382 AU, e = 0.1914921 (q = 0.74580 AU, Q = 1.09908 AU), i = 3.34092°, and hex flag 0x0802 = 0x0800 (PHA) | 0x0002 (Aten).
  • PHA Evaluation: Apophis's low inclination and node placement yield MOID⊕ = 0.00026 AU ≈ 38,900 km (0.101 LD ≤ 0.05 AU), while H = 19.09 mag ≤ 22.0 mag (D ≈ 340–370 m). Because both criteria in Eq. (1) hold and (0x0802 & 0x0800) != 0, IsPHA = true.

Example 2: (433) Eros — Amor-Class Non-Hazardous NEO (IsPHA = false)

Excerpt from MPCORB.DAT (columns 1–103 and hex flag 0004 in columns 162–165): 

00433   10.41  0.46 K25BL  71.23914  178.92841  304.27012   10.82756  0.2227214  0.55978912   1.4582734  [... flags: 0004]
  • Extracted Parameters: H = 10.41 mag (columns 9–13), a = 1.4582734 AU, e = 0.2227214 (q = 1.13349 AU), and hex flag 0x0004 (Amor).
  • PHA Evaluation: Although Eros easily satisfies the size threshold (H = 10.41 mag ≤ 22.0 mag, D ≈ 16.84 km), its perihelion distance q = 1.13349 AU keeps its entire orbit well outside Earth's aphelion (MOID⊕ = 0.1484 AU > 0.05 AU). Because the proximity condition fails and (0x0004 & 0x0800) == 0, IsPHA = false.
