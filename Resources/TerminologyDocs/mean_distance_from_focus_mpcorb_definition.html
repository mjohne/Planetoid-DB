Mean Distance from Focus (a vs. ⟨r⟩) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Averaging Conventions Across Orbital Anomalies, and Evaluation from the IAU Minor Planet Center Orbit Catalog

1. Dual Scientific Meaning: Classical Synonym vs. Dynamical Averages

In celestial mechanics, orbital astrometry, and classical astronomical nomenclature, the expression “mean distance from focus” (or simply mean distance) carries a crucial terminological distinction that must be resolved rigorously when processing records from the MPCORB catalog:

  1. Classical Keplerian Definition (Identical to the Semi-Major Axis a): Historically and throughout standard astrometric literature (including IAU Minor Planet Center conventions), the “mean distance” of a minor planet from the primary focus (the Sun) is defined as the arithmetic mean of its minimum (perihelion, q) and maximum (aphelion, Q) heliocentric distances—which equals the eccentric-anomaly average ⟨r⟩E and is identically equal to the semi-major axis a:
    rmean, classical ≡ q + Q 2 = a(1 − e) + a(1 + e) 2 = 1 2π ∫02π r(E) dE = a
  2. Analytical Integral Averages Over Other Orbital Angles/Time (⟨r⟩M and ⟨r⟩ν): Because a minor planet moves slower near aphelion and faster near perihelion in accordance with Kepler’s second law, its time-averaged (mean-anomaly) distance from the focus exceeds a, whereas its true-anomaly-averaged distance is smaller than a.

2. Exact Analytical Formulations Across Orbital Averaging Measures

For any bound Keplerian orbit (0 ≤ e < 1) with semi-major axis a, semi-minor axis b = a√(1 − e2), and semi-latus rectum p = a(1 − e2), the instantaneous heliocentric distance r admits three distinct closed-form integral averages depending on the uniform integration variable:

2.1 Eccentric-Anomaly Average (⟨r⟩E) — Classical “Mean Distance”

Using the Keplerian radius relation r(E) = a(1 − e cos E), integrating uniformly over the eccentric anomaly E cancels the cosine term identically:

⟨r⟩E = 1 2π ∫02π a(1 − e cos E) dE = a = q + Q 2

This exact identity explains why 18th- and 19th-century celestial mechanicians (including Gauss in Theoria Motus) referred to a interchangeably as the semi-major axis and the mean distance (distantia media), and why Kepler’s third law is traditionally stated as: “The square of the orbital period is proportional to the cube of the mean distance from the Sun.”

2.2 Time / Mean-Anomaly Average (⟨r⟩t = ⟨r⟩M)

When averaging heliocentric distance over uniform elapsed time t (equivalently over the mean anomaly M = n(t − t0), where dM = (1 − e cos E) dE via Kepler’s equation M = E − e sin E), the body spends disproportionately more time in the aphelion half of its orbit (r > a), yielding:

⟨r⟩t = ⟨r⟩M = 1 P ∫0P r(t) dt = 1 2π ∫02π a(1 − e cos E)2 dE = a &Bigl( 1 + e2 2 &Bigr)

For any non-circular orbit (e > 0), the time-averaged distance from the focus strictly exceeds the semi-major axis by Δr = +½ae2. By contrast, the time-averaged inverse distance satisfies ⟨1/r⟩t = 1/a (consistent with the virial theorem, ⟨v2⟩t = μ/a).

2.3 True-Anomaly Average (⟨r⟩ν)

Averaging the polar conic equation r(ν) = p / (1 + e cos ν) uniformly over the true anomaly ν yields the semi-minor axis b:

⟨r⟩ν = 1 2π ∫02π a(1 − e2) 1 + e cos ν dν = a √ 1 − e2 = b = √ q Q

Together with the semi-latus rectum p = 2qQ/(q + Q), these measures satisfy the strict inequality chain for every elliptic orbit (0 < e < 1):

q < p < ⟨r⟩ν (= b) < ⟨r⟩E (= a) < ⟨r⟩t (= a(1 + e2/2)) < Q

3. Specific Status and Usage in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database distributed by the International Astronomical Union’s Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC):

  • Direct Storage of Classical Mean Distance (a): Every record in MPCORB.DAT directly tabulates the osculating heliocentric semi-major axis a in Astronomical Units (AU) in columns 93–103 (Fortran format F11.7). In MPC documentation, orbital element tables, and Minor Planet Circulars (MPCs), this column represents the classical mean distance from the focus (Sun) a = (q + Q)/2 = ⟨r⟩E.
  • Coupling to Mean Daily Motion (n) in MPCORB.DAT: Columns 81–91 (Fortran format F11.8) store the osculating mean daily motion n (in °/day), which is dynamically linked to the classical mean distance a via Kepler’s third law using the Gaussian gravitational constant k = 0.01720209895 rad/day (kdeg = 0.9856076686°/day):
    n = kdeg a3/2   ⟺   a = &Bigl( kdeg n &Bigr)2/3
  • Computation of Time-Averaged Distance (⟨r⟩t): When physical or thermal modeling of an MPCORB body requires the true time-averaged heliocentric distance (for example, in equilibrium insolation or Yarkovsky thermal drift integrals), it is not stored as a separate column in MPCORB.DAT; rather, it is evaluated from columns 71–79 (e) and columns 93–103 (a) via ⟨r⟩t = a(1 + e2/2).
  • Parabolic/Hyperbolic Limitation: Because a (and every radial mean ⟨r⟩) diverges to infinity as e → 1, MPCORB.DAT contains exclusively elliptic minor planet orbits (0 ≤ e < 1, a > 0). Parabolic and hyperbolic trajectories are segregated into COMET.DAT, where perihelion distance q replaces a.
Terminology Disambiguation for Software & UI Labels: In astrometric catalogs and legacy orbital software, labeling column 93–103 (a) as “Mean Distance” refers strictly to the geometric/eccentric-anomaly mean (q + Q)/2 = ⟨r⟩E. If displaying the time-averaged heliocentric distance a(1 + e2/2) alongside a, label it explicitly as “Time-Averaged Heliocentric Distance ⟨r⟩t” to prevent confusion with the semi-major axis.
