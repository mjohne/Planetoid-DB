Latus Rectum (2p) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Focal Chord Geometry, and Derivation from the IAU Minor Planet Center Orbit Catalog

1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Geometric Definition

In celestial mechanics, analytical conic geometry, and orbital astrometry, the latus rectum (from Latin latus, “side,” and rectum, “straight”; plural: latera recta), denoted algebraically as 2p (twice the semi-latus rectum p, occasionally 2ℓ), is the full focal chord of a Keplerian trajectory passing through the primary focus (occupied by the Sun) perpendicular to the line of apsides (the major axis).

Geometrically, the endpoints of the latus rectum correspond to the two orbital positions where the true anomaly equals ν = +π/2 rad (+90°) and ν = −π/2 rad (−90°), respectively. Because the trajectory is symmetric about the line of apsides, both endpoints lie at the exact same heliocentric distance r(±90°) = p from the Sun, so the total chord length spans 2p. Unlike the major axis (2a) and minor axis (2b), which diverge at the parabolic escape boundary (e = 1), the latus rectum 2p remains strictly finite and positive across all non-rectilinear conic sections—elliptic (0 ≤ e < 1), parabolic (e = 1), and hyperbolic (e > 1):

Lrectum = 2p = 2a(1 − e2) = 2q(1 + e) = 2 b2 a = 4 q Q q + Q

where a is the semi-major axis, e is the orbital eccentricity, b = a√(1 − e2) is the semi-minor axis, q = a(1 − e) is the perihelion distance, and Q = a(1 + e) is the aphelion distance. Notice the ordering of the three principal full chords of an elliptic orbit (0 < e < 1):

2p < 2b < 2a   ⟺   2a(1 − e2) < 2a√(1 − e2) < 2a

In terms of classical mean comparisons between the perihelion distance q and aphelion distance Q, the major axis (2a = q + Q) is twice their arithmetic mean, the minor axis (2b = 2√(qQ)) is twice their geometric mean, and the latus rectum (2p = 4qQ/(q + Q)) is twice their harmonic mean. For a strictly circular orbit (e = 0), all three full chords coalesce into the uniform orbital diameter (2p = 2b = 2a = 2r).

2. Physical & Dynamical Significance in Celestial Mechanics

In two-body astrodynamics, the latus rectum 2p is a direct measure of the specific relative orbital angular momentum (h = r × v) squared, independent of the specific orbital energy (ℰ).

2.1 Coupling to Specific Orbital Angular Momentum

Under a heliocentric inverse-square gravitational field with parameter μ = G(M☉ + m) ≈ GM☉, the full latus rectum is proportional to h2:

2p = 2 h2 μ   ⟺   h = √ μ (2p) 2

Thus, at a fixed heliocentric gravitational parameter μ, the latus rectum 2p uniquely determines the orbital angular momentum regardless of whether the trajectory is bound (0 ≤ e < 1), parabolic (e = 1), or hyperbolic (e > 1).

2.2 Conic Trajectory Equation & Focal Cross-Section

In polar coordinates centered on the Sun, the heliocentric radial distance r(ν) at true anomaly ν is governed by half the latus rectum (p):

r(ν) = (2p) / 2 1 + e cos ν = p 1 + e cos ν

Geometrically, the latus rectum 2p defines the full width of the conic section across the Sun perpendicular to the perihelion–aphelion axis, making it a critical scale parameter in solar-system dust/meteoroid stream cross-sections and orbital intersection geometry.

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database published by the International Astronomical Union's Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC), neither the semi-latus rectum (p) nor the full latus rectum (2p) is stored explicitly as a fixed-width column in MPCORB.DAT. Instead, 2p is computed deterministically from two primary osculating elements stored in each record:

  • Computational Derivation from MPCORB.DAT: Parse the osculating orbital eccentricity e (columns 71–79, Fortran format F9.7) and the osculating semi-major axis a (columns 93–103, Fortran format F11.7), and evaluate 2p = 2a(1 − e2) = 2a(1 − e)(1 + e).
  • Derivation from COMET.DAT (Parabolic/Hyperbolic Regime): For comets and interstellar objects parameterized by perihelion distance q and eccentricity e, evaluate 2p = 2q(1 + e). Notably, at the exact parabolic escape limit (e = 1), the latus rectum equals exactly four times the perihelion distance (2p = 4q).
  • Osculating Heliocentric Reference & Unit: Because a and e in MPCORB.DAT are osculating heliocentric elements valid at the record's packed Epoch (columns 21–25) in the J2000.0 mean ecliptic frame, the derived latus rectum 2p is likewise an instantaneous osculating heliocentric length expressed in Astronomical Units (AU) (1 AU ≡ 149,597,870,700 m).
Numerical Stability Tip for MPCORB Parsers: When computing the latus rectum 2p in IEEE-754 double precision from MPCORB.DAT for near-parabolic objects (e → 1−), factor the expression as latus_rectum = 2.0 * a * (1.0 - e) * (1.0 + e) rather than 2.0 * a * (1.0 - e * e) to minimize floating-point rounding loss in (1 − e).
