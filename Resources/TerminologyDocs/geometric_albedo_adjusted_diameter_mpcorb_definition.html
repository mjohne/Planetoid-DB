Geometric Albedo-Adjusted Diameter (D)

Photometric Diameter Scaling, Albedo Priors & Radiometric Error Propagation in MPCORB.DAT

1. Formal Definition

In planetary photometry, asteroid physical characterization, and derived physical parameters computed from the Minor Planet Center Orbit Database (MPCORB), the geometric albedo-adjusted diameter (D, in kilometers) is the effective area-equivalent spherical diameter of a minor body inferred from its tabulated absolute visual magnitude H (columns 9–13 of MPCORB.DAT, in the IAU H–G magnitude system) and an assumed or measured visual geometric albedo pV:

D(H, pV) = D0√pV · 10−H / 5 = 1329.0√pV · 10−0.2 H   km (Eq. 1)

where:

  • H is the absolute magnitude tabulated directly in MPCORB.DAT (columns 9–13, formatted as F5.2 in the Johnson V band), defined as the apparent visual magnitude the minor body would have if observed at zero solar phase angle (α = 0°) at unit heliocentric and geocentric distances (r = Δ = 1 AU).
  • pV ∈ (0, 1] is the visual geometric albedo (dimensionless), defined as the ratio of the body's zero-phase-angle brightness at α = 0° to that of an idealized, flat, perfectly diffusing Lambertian disk of the same cross-sectional area Aeff = π D2 / 4 illuminated normally. Because MPCORB.DAT does not store individual albedos, pV is supplied either from external radiometric/polarimetric catalogs (e.g., NEOWISE, IRAS, AKARI) or via taxonomically/dynamically calibrated prior ranges (typically pV ∈ [0.04, 0.25]).
  • D0 = 2 AU · 10V☉ / 5 ≈ 1329.0 km is the photometric diameter calibration constant (Fowler & Chillemi 1992; Bowell et al. 1989; Pravec & Harris 2007), derived from the apparent visual magnitude of the Sun at 1 AU (V☉ = −26.76 mag, giving D0 = 2 × 149597870.7 × 10−26.76/5 = 1328.9 km ≈ 1329 km).
Figure 1: Photometric scaling of the geometric albedo-adjusted diameter D(H, pV) = (1329 / √pV) · 10−0.2 H across the MPCORB.DAT absolute magnitude domain (H = 3 to 22 mag). Because D ∝ pV−1/2, a factor-of-6.25 variation in albedo between dark primitive asteroids (pV = 0.04) and bright silicaceous asteroids (pV = 0.25) produces a factor-of-2.5 spread in inferred diameter at fixed H.

2. First-Principles Derivation & Error Propagation

At zero solar phase angle (α = 0°) and unit distances (r = Δ = 1 AU), the visual flux Fast reflected by a spherical body of diameter D and geometric albedo pV is proportional to the product of its geometric cross-section (π D2 / 4) and pV, normalized by the solar flux F☉ at 1 AU:

FastF☉ = 10−0.4 (H − V☉) = pV π D2 / 4π (1 AU)2 = pV &left(D2 AU&right)2 (Eq. 2)

Solving Eq. (2) for D recovers Eq. (1), while solving for pV allows independent thermal-infrared or occultation diameters (Dobs) to be combined with MPCORB.DAT H values to derive pV = (1329.0 / Dobs)2 · 10−0.4 H. Taking partial derivatives of ln D = ln D0 − 12 ln pV − (ln 10 / 5) H yields the relative uncertainty σD / D:

σDD = √&left(ln 105 σH&right)2 + &left(σpV2 pV&right)2 ≈ √(0.46052 σH)2 + &left(σpV2 pV&right)2 (Eq. 3)

where a typical catalog magnitude uncertainty of σH = 0.25 mag induces an 11.5% diameter uncertainty, whereas an unmeasured albedo prior pV ∈ [0.05, 0.25] dominates the error budget with a ∼40–55% spread.

3. Taxonomic & Dynamical Albedo Priors for MPCORB.DAT

When individual radiometric albedos are unavailable for an entry in MPCORB.DAT, asteroid survey pipelines (such as the MPC/IAU Minor Planet Center, JPL Small-Body Database, and CNEOS) evaluate D(H, pV) using either the canonical single-value standard (pV = 0.14 or 0.15) or the bracketing range [D(H, 0.25), D(H, 0.05)] stratified by taxonomic/dynamical population:

Taxonomic / Dynamical Class Typical pV Range Nominal pV Scale Factor 1329 / √pV (km)
Cometary Nuclei / D- & P-Types (Trojans, Outer Belt) 0.03 – 0.07 0.045 6265 km
C-Complex (Carbonaceous, Outer Main Belt) 0.04 – 0.09 0.060 5426 km
Canonical Unclassified MPCORB Standard 0.05 – 0.25 0.140 3552 km
S-Complex (Silicaceous, Inner Main Belt & NEAs) 0.15 – 0.28 0.200 2972 km
V- & E-Types (Basaltic Vestoids, Hungarias) 0.30 – 0.55 0.400 2101 km

4. Astrodynamical & Observational Caveats

  • Slope Parameter (G) Coupling in MPCORB.DAT: In MPCORB.DAT, H (columns 9–13) is fitted jointly with the phase-curve slope parameter G (columns 15–19, defaulting to G = 0.15 when phase coverage is sparse). Because dark C-type asteroids typically exhibit steeper opposition surges (G ≈ 0.08–0.12) than bright S/E-type bodies (G ≈ 0.20–0.40), assuming G = 0.15 can bias H by ±0.15–0.30 mag, propagating into a 7–15% systematic shift in D(H, pV).
  • Non-Spherical Shape & Rotational Lightcurve Amplitude: Eq. (1) yields the diameter of an equivalent sphere with cross-sectional area Aeff = π D2 / 4. For elongated triaxial ellipsoids (abody > bbody ≥ cbody), D ≈ 2(abody bbody cbody)1/3 corresponds to the rotationally averaged projected area, while instantaneous apparent magnitudes oscillate by Δm = 2.5 log10(abody / bbody).
  • Cometary Coma Contamination: For active minor bodies in MPCORB.DAT exhibiting dust or gas comae near perihelion, the catalog magnitude reflects integrated coma + nucleus brightness rather than bare-nucleus HN, causing Eq. (1) to overestimate the true nuclear diameter unless quiescent-aphelion photometry is used.

5. Worked Examples from MPCORB.DAT

The following two numbered minor planets from MPCORB.DAT (epoch K25BL = 2025 Nov 21.0 TT = JD 2461000.5) illustrate the calculation of D(H, pV) using both measured radiometric albedos and standard unmeasured albedo brackets (pV ∈ [0.05, 0.25]):

Example 1: (1) Ceres — Dark G/C-Type Dwarf Planet (D = 939.4 km at pV = 0.090)

Excerpt from MPCORB.DAT (columns 1–103): 

00001    3.34  0.12 K25BL 231.53975   73.28579   80.25414   10.58688  0.0789158  0.21410467   2.7675059
  • Extracted Photometric Elements: H = 3.34 mag (columns 9–13) and G = 0.12 (columns 15–19).
  • Albedo-Adjusted Diameter Evaluation: Applying Dawn/radiometric visual geometric albedo pV = 0.090 in Eq. (1) gives D = (1329.0 / √0.090) · 10−0.2(3.34) = 4430.0 × 0.214783 = 951.5 km (closely matching Dawn's volume-equivalent sphere D = 939.4 km, which corresponds to pV = 0.0923 at H = 3.34). Without prior albedo knowledge, the standard pV ∈ [0.05, 0.25] bracket yields D ∈ [570.9 km, 1276.6 km] (nominal pV = 0.14 ⇒ 762.9 km).

Example 2: (433) Eros — Bright S-Type Near-Earth Asteroid (D = 16.84 km at pV = 0.250)

Excerpt from MPCORB.DAT (columns 1–103): 

00433   10.41  0.46 K25BL  71.23914  178.92841  304.27012   10.82756  0.2227214  0.55978912   1.4582734
  • Extracted Photometric Elements: H = 10.41 mag (columns 9–13) and steep S-type slope parameter G = 0.46 (columns 15–19).
  • Albedo-Adjusted Diameter Evaluation: Using NEAR Shoemaker's S-type visual geometric albedo pV = 0.250 in Eq. (1) yields D = (1329.0 / √0.250) · 10−0.2(10.41) = 2658.0 × 0.0082794 = 22.01 km at H = 10.41 (or 16.84 km at NEAR's mean HV = 10.99 mag, matching Eros's 34.4 × 11.2 × 11.2 km triaxial volume-equivalent diameter of 16.84 km). Under the canonical unclassified pV = 0.14 assumption, H = 10.41 gives D = 29.41 km.
