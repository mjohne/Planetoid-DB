Minor Axis (2b) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Transverse Orbital Diameter, and Derivation from the IAU Minor Planet Center Orbit Catalog

1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Geometric Definition

In celestial mechanics, orbital astrometry, and analytic conic geometry, the minor axis—denoted algebraically as 2b (twice the semi-minor axis b)—is the shortest principal diameter of a bound elliptic Keplerian trajectory (0 ≤ e < 1). Geometrically, it is the unique chord of the orbital ellipse that passes orthogonally through the geometric center of the ellipse, bisecting the line of apsides (the major axis 2a) at right angles and terminating at the two co-vertices (minor-axis vertices) of the orbit.

In terms of the canonical Keplerian orbital elements—the semi-major axis (a) and the orbital eccentricity (e)—the total scalar length of the minor axis is rigorously given by:

Dminor = 2b = 2a √ 1 − e2 = 2 √ q · Q

where q = a(1 − e) is the perihelion distance and Q = a(1 + e) is the aphelion distance. Equivalently, the minor axis 2b is twice the geometric mean of the apsidal distances, and satisfies the Euclidean right-triangle relation connecting the center, either focus (at distance ae from the center), and either co-vertex:

(2b)2 = (2a)2 − (2ae)2 = 4a2(1 − e2)

For a strictly circular orbit (e = 0), the minor axis equals the major axis and coincides with the uniform orbital diameter (2b = 2a = 2r). As e → 1−, the ratio 2b / 2a = √(1 − e2) → 0, producing an increasingly needle-like (high-eccentricity) elliptic trajectory.

2. Physical and Dynamical Significance

In two-body astrodynamics, the minor axis 2b measures the full transverse width of the orbital ellipse perpendicular to the line of apsides and is directly coupled to the specific orbital angular momentum (h), the total enclosed orbital area (Aorb), and the instantaneous heliocentric speed at the co-vertices.

2.1 Specific Orbital Angular Momentum & Enclosed Area

For a minor planet moving under the heliocentric gravitational parameter μ = G(M☉ + m) ≈ GM☉, the specific relative angular momentum magnitude h = |r × v| is directly proportional to the minor axis 2b at a given orbital energy (fixed a):

h = 2b 2 √ μ a   ⟺   2b = 2h √ a μ

The total geometric area enclosed by the orbital ellipse is expressed symmetrically in terms of the full major axis (2a) and full minor axis (2b) as:

Aorb = π a b = π 4 (2a)(2b)

2.2 Kinematics at the Minor-Axis Co-Vertices

When a minor planet passes either co-vertex (end of the minor axis 2b), its heliocentric radial distance equals exactly the semi-major axis (r = a), and its instantaneous heliocentric orbital speed equals the circular speed at distance a (v = √(μ/a)), directed parallel to the major axis.

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database maintained by the International Astronomical Union's Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC), neither the semi-minor axis (b) nor the full minor axis (2b) is explicitly tabulated. Instead, 2b is derived deterministically from two primary osculating elements stored in each record:

  • Computational Derivation from MPCORB.DAT: Parse the osculating orbital eccentricity e (columns 71–79, Fortran format F9.7) and the osculating semi-major axis a (columns 93–103, Fortran format F11.7), and evaluate 2b = 2a√(1 − e2).
  • Osculating Heliocentric Reference: Because a and e in MPCORB are osculating heliocentric elements valid at the record's packed Epoch (columns 21–25) and oriented in the J2000.0 mean ecliptic frame, the derived minor axis 2b is likewise an instantaneous osculating heliocentric quantity subject to planetary N-body perturbations (such as secular eccentricity cycles).
  • Unit of Measurement: Expressed in Astronomical Units (AU), where 1 AU ≡ 149,597,870,700 m (exact, per IAU 2012 Resolution B2).
Conic Section Restriction (0 ≤ e < 1): A real, finite minor axis (2b > 0) exists strictly for bound elliptic orbits (0 ≤ e < 1), which form the standard domain of MPCORB.DAT. At the parabolic limit (e = 1), 2b → ∞; for hyperbolic interstellar or comet trajectories (e > 1, a < 0), the conjugate axis is given by 2b = 2|a|√(e2 − 1), equal to twice the asymptotic impact parameter.

4. Fixed-Width Source Columns in MPCORB.DAT

The table below specifies the exact fixed-width columns (1-based indexing) in a standard 202-character MPCORB.DAT record required to compute the minor axis 2b:

Parameter Columns (1-based) Python Slice (0-based) Fortran Format Unit & Role
Epoch 21–25 line[20:25] A5 (Packed) Osculation reference epoch
e (Eccentricity) 71–79 line[70:79] F9.7 Dimensionless (0 ≤ e < 1); input for 2b
a (Semi-major axis) 93–103 line[92:103] F11.7 Astronomical Units (AU); input for 2b
2b (Minor axis) Derived (not stored) 2.0 * a * math.sqrt(1 - e**2) Computed Astronomical Units (AU); transverse orbital width

Example Record Calculation (00001 — (1) Ceres)

Col:  ... 71      79 ... 93       103 ...
Data: ... 0.0785354  ... 2.7653485    ...
          └───┬───┘      └────┬────┘
              e             a (AU)

Computation:
  Semi-minor axis (b) = 2.7653485 * sqrt(1 - 0.0785354^2) = 2.7568065 AU
  Minor axis     (2b) = 2 * 2.7568065 AU                   = 5.5136130 AU
  Major axis     (2a) = 2 * 2.7653485 AU                   = 5.5306970 AU
