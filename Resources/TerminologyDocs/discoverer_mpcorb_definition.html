Discoverer

Human Observer, Sky Survey Project, Robotic Facility & Pipeline Attribution in Minor Planet Metadata

1. Formal Definition & Schema Scope

In the broader Minor Planet Center ecosystem and extended minor-planet catalog databases (such as mpcorb_extended.json or relational MPC tables), the Discoverer (Disc) is an astrometric provenance attribute recording the human discoverer(s), astronomical observatory, dedicated sky survey project, or automated robotic pipeline credited with the initial discovery astrometric observations of a minor planet:

Discoverer(r) = Metadataext(Desigpacked(r)).discoverer_string ∈ ΣASCII* (Eq. 1)

where:

  • Architectural Separation from Core MPCORB.DAT: In the classic 202-character fixed-width MPCORB.DAT flat file, columns 1–202 are strictly dedicated to numerical orbital elements, epoch strings, uncertainty parameters, perturber flags, and names/designations. The Discoverer attribute is NOT stored within the 202-byte flat-file record; instead, it resides in auxiliary metadata tables maintained by the IAU Minor Planet Center and is cross-referenced via the minor planet's unique packed primary key (columns 1–7) or readable designation (columns 167–194).
  • Taxonomy of Discoverer Attribution: Discoverer credits fall into four distinct operational categories:
    1. Individual Visual / Photographic Discoverers: Human astronomers credited by surname or initials in historical discoveries (e.g., “G. Piazzi” for (1) Ceres, “J. Palisa”, “Max Wolf”).
    2. Dedicated Automated Sky Surveys & Projects: Major photographic and CCD surveys cataloging thousands of objects (e.g., “Palomar–Leiden Survey” [P-L], “LINEAR”, “NEAT”, “Lowell Observatory Near-Earth-Object Search” [LONEOS], “Spacewatch”, “Catalina Sky Survey” [CSS], “Pan-STARRS”).
    3. Modern Robotic Survey Telescopes: Automated wide-field survey facilities credited as the discoverer institution/telescope (e.g., “Mount Lemmon Survey”, “Siding Spring Survey”).
    4. Amateur Astrometric Discoverers: Dedicated amateur astronomers operating private robotic observatories who report astrometric discoveries validated by the MPC.

3. Software Engineering Integration & Database Architecture

  • Separation of Concerns: Because MPCORB.DAT is optimized for high-performance astrodynamic integration (containing precisely formatted orbital elements, ephemeris epochs, and uncertainty metrics in 202 columns), dynamic text strings like Discoverer names and bibliography citations are deliberately decoupled from the flat-file binary stream.
  • Relational & JSON Cross-Referencing: In modern Python or C# pipelines parsing MPCORB alongside extended metadata (e.g., mpcorb_extended.json or Lowell Astorb), the primary key (columns 1–7) acts as the foreign key joining the numerical state vector to the discoverer attribution string.
  • Distinction Between Discoverer and Observatory: While an observatory code (e.g., 703 for Catalina or F51 for Pan-STARRS 1) identifies where the discovery observations were reported, the Discoverer field specifically credits the individual(s) or survey project responsible for recognizing and submitting the initial moving object candidate.

4. Worked Examples from the MPCORB Ecosystem

The following two examples illustrate how the Discoverer attribute links to MPCORB.DAT records for a classical numbered asteroid versus a modern survey discovery:

Example 1: (1) Ceres — Historical Visual Discoverer

MPCORB.DAT record for 00001: 

00001    3.35  0.15 K25BL 231.53975   73.28579   80.24963   10.58790  0.0795763  0.21417972   2.7666198  0 MPO925614 11432  98 1801-2025 0.61 M-v 30h JPL 48     1000   (1) Ceres                  20251018
  • Primary Designation (Cols 1–7): 00001 → Readable Designation (Cols 167–194): "(1) Ceres"
  • Linked Discoverer Metadata: "G. Piazzi" (Giuseppe Piazzi, Palermo Astronomical Observatory, discovered January 1, 1801).

Example 2: 2025 QA14 — Modern Survey Discoverer

MPCORB.DAT record for K25Q14A: 

K25Q14A 27.64  0.15 K25BL 353.30750  347.90466  352.66257    5.38873  0.2564143  0.65913384   1.3073812  0 E2025-R84    27   1    14 days 0.28 M-v 38k MPCLINUX   8403            2025 QA14          20250911
  • Primary Designation (Cols 1–7): K25Q14A → Readable Designation (Cols 167–194): "2025 QA14"
  • Linked Discoverer Metadata: "Catalina Sky Survey" (or specific survey telescope station code).
