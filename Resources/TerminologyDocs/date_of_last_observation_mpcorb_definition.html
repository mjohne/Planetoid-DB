Date of Last Observation (tlast)

UTC Astrometric Endpoint (YYYYMMDD, Columns 195–202), Arc Reconstruction & Extrapolation Staleness

1. Formal Definition

In the Minor Planet Center Orbit Database (MPCORB), the Date of Last Observation (tlast, stored in columns 195–202, 1-indexed, as an 8-character Gregorian calendar string formatted as YYYYMMDD) is the UTC calendar date of the latest astrometric observation included in the orbital solution for a given minor planet:

tlast = maxk ∈ {1, …, Nobs} ⌊ tobs, k(UTC) ⌋day ≡ (Y4 M2 D2)10,    Δtstale = tepoch − tlast (Eq. 1)

where:

  • tobs, k(UTC) is the Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) observation timestamp of the k-th accepted astrometric measurement in the Minor Planet Center (MPC) observations file (Nobs total observations, tabulated in columns 118–122 of MPCORB.DAT).
  • YYYYMMDD (Y4 M2 D2, 0-based substring indices 194..202, length 8) encodes the 4-digit Gregorian year (Y4), 2-digit zero-padded month (M2 ∈ [01, 12]), and 2-digit zero-padded day of month (D2 ∈ [01, 31]) in UTC, truncated to integer days (dropping the sub-day decimal fraction published in the MPC 80-column astrometry record).
  • Δtstale = tepoch − tlast is the astrometric extrapolation baseline (staleness) between the last observed date tlast and the catalog osculating epoch tepoch (columns 21–25 in 5-character packed form). When tlast < tepoch, the orbital state vector at tepoch has been propagated forward purely via numerical integration without observational feedback over Δtstale.

Together with the arc span (columns 128–136) and osculating epoch (tepoch, columns 21–25), tlast defines both the start of the observational arc (tfirst = tlast − Δtarc for single-opposition objects) and the unguided propagation interval Δtstale = tepoch − tlast.

2. Astrodynamical Significance & Ephemeris Uncertainty Growth

In orbit determination and observational planning, tlast is the primary diagnostic indicator of ephemeris currency and recovery urgency:

  • Secular Along-Track Uncertainty Growth: Because the orbital semi-major axis a (and hence mean motion n = √μ☉ / a3) carries a formal least-squares uncertainty σn determined by the observed arc length Δtarc, the mean anomaly uncertainty σM(t) grows linearly with elapsed time Δt = t − tlast beyond the last astrometric measurement:
    σM(t) ≈ √σM02 + (σn · (t − tlast))2,    σalong-track(t) ≈ a · σM(t) (Eq. 2)
    For a newly discovered single-opposition NEO with a short arc (Δtarc ≈ 2–5 days), a stale tlast older than a few weeks can inflate sky-plane positional uncertainty from arcseconds to tens of degrees (“lost” object).
  • Reconstructing the First Observation Date (tfirst): In MPCORB.DAT, columns 128–136 encode either a year range YYYY-YYYY (for multi-opposition objects) or an integer arc length in days NNNNN days (for single-opposition objects). For single-opposition records, subtracting the integer arc length from tlast yields the exact calendar date of discovery/first observation: tfirst = tlast − Δtarc.
  • Time-Scale Distinction (UTC vs. TT): While orbital elements in MPCORB.DAT (such as epoch tepoch and perihelion passage Tp) are referenced to Terrestrial Time (TT), astrometric observations reported to the MPC are timestamped in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) (historically UT prior to 1972). Consequently, YYYYMMDD in columns 195–202 is strictly a UTC civil date; converting it to a Julian Date for comparison with TT epochs requires adding ΔT = TT − UTC (≈ 69.184 s in 2025–2026) when sub-minute precision is needed.

3. Related Temporal & Arc Columns in MPCORB.DAT

To interpret tlast completely within a fixed-width MPCORB.DAT parser, it is evaluated alongside four companion fields:

Columns (1-Based) 0-Based Slice Format Field Name & Relationship to Date of Last Observation
21–25 [20..25] A5 (Packed) Epoch of Osculation (tepoch): Standard TT catalog epoch (e.g., K25BL = 2025-11-21.0 TT) to which M0 and osculating elements are integrated.
118–122 [117..122] I5 Number of Observations (Nobs): Total accepted astrometric measurements spanning [tfirst, tlast].
124–126 [123..126] I3 Number of Oppositions (Nopp): Number of distinct solar oppositions across which observations were secured.
128–136 [127..136] A9 Year of First–Last Observation / Arc Length: Formatted as YYYY-YYYY when Nopp > 1 (ending in the year of tlast) or NNNNN days when Nopp = 1.
195–202 [194..202] I4,I2,I2 Date of Last Observation (tlast): Exact UTC calendar date YYYYMMDD of the final astrometric observation in the orbit fit.

4. Edge Cases & Data-Quality Diagnostics in MPCORB.DAT

  • Future-Dated Epoch vs. Past Last Observation (tlast < tepoch): The MPC publishes MPCORB.DAT at standard osculating epochs spaced every 200 days (e.g., K2555 = 2025-05-05, K25BL = 2025-11-21, K2669 = 2026-06-09). Therefore, even for an asteroid observed the night before catalog generation, tlast will frequently precede tepoch by tens of days.
  • Historical / Unrecovered Single-Opposition Objects: For lost minor planets observed briefly decades ago (e.g., 1979 XB or early Palomar–Leiden survey objects), tlast may lie 40–70 years prior to tepoch, signaling that direct two-body or perturbed propagation to the current epoch carries multi-degree sky-plane uncertainty.
  • Line-Length Validation Before Slicing: While standard MPCORB.DAT records are exactly 202 characters wide (excluding CR/LF), legacy or custom-filtered extracts occasionally omit trailing fields or truncate lines at column 194. Defensive parsers should verify line.Length >= 202 before parsing YYYYMMDD from columns 195–202.

5. Worked Examples from MPCORB.DAT

The following two records from MPCORB.DAT (osculating epoch K25BL = 2025 Nov 21.0 TT = JD 2461000.5) illustrate how columns 195–202 combine with the arc span (columns 128–136) and epoch (columns 21–25):

Example 1: (1) Ceres — Multi-Opposition Main-Belt Dwarf Planet (20251018)

Excerpt of MPCORB.DAT columns 118–202 for 00001: 

... 11432  98 1801-2025 0.61 M-v 30h Williams   1000   (1) Ceres                  20251018
  • Parsed Date of Last Observation: 20251018 → 2025 October 18 UTC (JDUTC = 2460966.5).
  • Arc & Staleness Synthesis: Spanning 1801-2025 across Nopp = 98 oppositions (Nobs = 11,432), Ceres's last observation occurred just Δtstale = 34.0 days prior to the K25BL osculating epoch (2025-11-21), yielding sub-milliarcsecond ephemeris precision.

Example 2: (99942) Apophis — Radar-Observed Aten PHA (20210512)

Excerpt of MPCORB.DAT columns 118–202 for 99942: 

...  7361  14 2004-2021 0.34 M-v 38k Giorgini   1D02   (99942) Apophis            20210512
  • Parsed Date of Last Observation: 20210512 → 2021 May 12 UTC (JDUTC = 2459346.5).
  • Arc & Staleness Synthesis: Following its favorable 2021 apparition (including Goldstone/Green Bank planetary radar astrometry), Apophis entered small solar elongation until its next optical/radar window, resulting in Δtstale = 1,654 days (4.53 years) relative to epoch K25BL; nevertheless, the 17-year radar-constrained arc (2004-2021) keeps its 2029 Earth-flyby uncertainty below a few kilometers.
