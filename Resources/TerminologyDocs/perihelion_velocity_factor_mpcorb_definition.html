Perihelion Velocity Factor (√[(1 + e)/(1 − e)]) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Dimensionless Amplification of Mean Circular Speed at Perihelion, and Derivation from MPCORB

1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Kinematic Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In two-body Keplerian celestial mechanics, the perihelion velocity factor (also termed pericentric speed amplification factor or maximum-to-circular velocity ratio, denoted fq(e) = √[(1 + e) / (1 − e)]) is the dimensionless, monotonically increasing function of orbital eccentricity e (0 ≤ e < 1) that scales the mean circular orbital velocity vc = √(μ⊙/a) to the instantaneous heliocentric perihelion velocity vq.

Dividing the perihelion speed vq = √[μ⊙(1 + e)/(a(1 − e))] by the energy-equivalent circular speed vc = √(μ⊙/a) isolates the pure eccentricity dependence:

fq(e) ≡ vqvc = √ 1 + e 1 − e = √Qq = 1 + e√(1 − e2) = √(1 − e2)1 − e

Kinematic & Asymptotic Properties

  • Bounds & Series Expansion: On 0 ≤ e < 1, 1 ≤ fq(e) < ∞, with fq(0) = 1. For small eccentricities, fq(e) = 1 + e + ½e2 + ½e3 + …, so vq exceeds vc by approximately 100e% when e ≪ 1.
  • Reciprocal Symmetry with Aphelion Velocity Factor: fq(e) · fQ(e) = 1, while their ratio gives fq(e) / fQ(e) = (1 + e)/(1 − e) = vq / vQ.
  • Dual Identity with Anomaly Conversion Factor: fq(e) is algebraically identical to tan(ν/2) / tan(E/2).
  • Parabolic Divergence (e → 1−): Because vc → 0 as a → ∞ while vq → √(2μ⊙/q) remains finite, fq(e) diverges as √2 (1 − e)−1/2.

2. Contrast Among Velocity Scaling Factors

Velocity Factor Formula Range on 0 ≤ e < 1 Role Relative to vc
Perihelion Velocity Factor (fq) √[(1 + e)/(1 − e)] [1, ∞) Amplifies vc to maximum orbital speed vq
Aphelion Velocity Factor (fQ) √[(1 − e)/(1 + e)] (0, 1] Attenuates vc to minimum orbital speed vQ (1/fq)
Time-Averaged Speed Factor (f⟨v⟩) 2 E(e) / π (2/π, 1] Attenuates vc to temporal mean speed ⟨v⟩t

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

  • Direct Evaluation from Eccentricity e: Computed directly from columns 71–79 (F9.7) of MPCORB.DAT via sqrt((1.0 + e) / (1.0 − e)) without needing semi-major axis a.
  • Single-Square-Root Computation of Both Apsidal Speeds: Multiplying vc = √(μ⊙/a) by fq(e) yields vq, while dividing vc by fq(e) immediately yields vQ.
  • Hyperbolic Caveat (e > 1): For hyperbolic objects in MPCORB.DAT, 1 − e < 0 makes fq(e) imaginary; compute vq = √[μ⊙(1 + e)/q] directly using q = a(1 − e) > 0.
