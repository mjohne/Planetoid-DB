Perihelion Date (Tq / Tp) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Keplerian Mean-Motion Kinematics, Elliptic vs. Open-Conic Parameterizations, and Derivation from MPCORB

1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Chronological Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In two-body Keplerian celestial mechanics and ephemeris chronology, the perihelion date (or time / epoch of perihelion passage, denoted Tq, Tp, T, or JDq, and paired half a revolution away on bound orbits with the aphelion date TQ)—is the instant in Terrestrial Time (TT, expressed as a Julian Date JDTT, Modified Julian Date MJD, or Gregorian calendar timestamp) at which a heliocentric body reaches the perihelion (q) of its osculating Keplerian conic—the point of minimum heliocentric radial distance rmin = q = a(1 − e), maximum orbital speed vmax = √[μ(1 + e) / (a(1 − e))] = √[μ(1 + e) / q], true anomaly ν = 0° (0 rad), eccentric anomaly E = 0°, and mean anomaly M = 0°.

Under unperturbed two-body Keplerian motion on a bound elliptic orbit (0 ≤ e < 1), the mean anomaly M evolves linearly with Terrestrial Time t according to:

M(t) = M0 + n (t − t0)   (mod 360°)

where t0 is the osculating epoch (JDTT), M0 is the mean anomaly at epoch t0 (degrees), and n is the daily mean motion (deg/day), related to the semi-major axis a (AU) via Kepler’s third law n = kdeg a−3/2 with the Gaussian gravitational constant k = 0.01720209895 rad/day (kdeg = 0.9856076686 deg/day, neglecting minor-planet mass m ≪ M⊙).

Setting M(Tq) ≡ 0° (mod 360°) yields the exact osculating perihelion date Tq (JDTT). Depending on the chronological convention required, three canonical branches of Tq are defined relative to the osculating epoch t0 and orbital period P = 360° / n (days):

Tq,nearest = t0 + ΔMq,nearest n  ,     ΔMq,nearest = [(−M0 + 180°) mod 360°] − 180° ∈ (−180°, +180°]
Tq,next = t0 + (−M0) mod 360° n  ,     Tq,prev = Tq,next − P

Key dynamical and chronological properties of the perihelion date Tq include:

  • Fundamental Sixth Orbital Element (Tq vs. M0): In classical six-element Keplerian parameterization (a, e, i, Ω, ω, Tq) or (q, e, i, Ω, ω, Tq), the perihelion date Tq serves directly as the sixth (time-of-passage) orbital element, uniquely fixing the body’s position along its orbit at any instant t via M(t) = n(t − Tq).
  • Universal Validity Across All Conic Sections (e ≥ 0): Unlike the aphelion date TQ—which exists only for bound elliptic orbits (0 ≤ e < 1)—every non-rectilinear Keplerian trajectory (elliptic 0 ≤ e < 1, parabolic e = 1 via Barker’s equation, and hyperbolic e > 1 via MH = e sinh H − H = k |a|−3/2(t − Tq)) possesses a unique, well-defined perihelion passage Tq.
  • Half-Period Conjugacy with the Aphelion Date (TQ): For bound orbits (0 ≤ e < 1), perihelion (M = 0°) and aphelion (M = 180°) are separated by ΔM = ±180°, displacing their osculating passage epochs by exactly half an orbital period:
    Tq = TQ ± P 2 = TQ ± 180° n
  • Circular Orbit Degeneracy (e = 0): For an exact circular orbit (e = 0), the perihelion direction (longitude of perihelion ϖ = Ω + ω) is geometrically indeterminate; by formal convention using the tabulated M0, the equations evaluate smoothly to the instant where M(t) ≡ 0° (mod 360°).

2. Architectural Distinction Between MPCORB.DAT and COMET.DAT

Within the IAU Minor Planet Center’s orbital element distribution suite, the parameterization of orbital phase differs fundamentally between minor planets and comets:

MPC Catalog File Target Population Radial Scale Element Time / Phase Element Status of Perihelion Date (Tq)
MPCORB.DAT Numbered & unnumbered minor planets (0 ≤ e < 1) Semi-major axis a (AU, cols 93–103) Mean anomaly M0 (deg, cols 27–35) at packed epoch t0 (cols 21–25) Derived quantity (Tq = t0 + ΔMq / n); not explicitly tabulated
COMET.DAT Comets & open-orbit objects (e < 1, e = 1, e > 1) Perihelion distance q (AU, cols 31–39) Perihelion date Tq directly tabulated as YYYY MM DD.DDDD (TT, cols 15–29) Primary tabulated element (needed because M0 and n are undefined when e ≥ 1)

Because MPCORB.DAT uses a common standard osculating epoch t0 across all ~1.4 million minor planets (updated every 200 days by the MPC), storing M0 at t0 avoids integrating each asteroid to its individual perihelion date during catalog generation, while allowing Tq to be recovered instantaneously via Tq = t0 + ΔMq / n.

3. Specific Status, Decoding, and Significance in MPCORB.DAT

To compute Tq from any elliptic record in MPCORB.DAT:

  • Three Fixed-Width Source Columns:
    • Packed Epoch t0 (columns 21–25, format A5, 0h TT): Encoded as CYYMD where century C ∈ {I: 1800, J: 1900, K: 2000, L: 2100}, YY is the 2-digit year, and M, D are base-36 digits (1–9, A=10 … V=31). For example, K25BL decodes to 2025-11-21 00:00:00 TT (JDTT = 2461000.5).
    • Mean anomaly at epoch M0 (columns 27–35, format F9.5, degrees): Orbital phase at t0.
    • Daily mean motion n (columns 81–91, format F11.8, deg/day)—or equivalently from semi-major axis a (columns 93–103, format F11.7, AU) via n = 0.9856076686 a−3/2.
  • Physical & Observational Significance in Minor-Planet Studies:
    • Maximum Heliocentric Insolation & Volatile Activation: At Tq, solar irradiance S(r) = S0 / r2 reaches its orbital peak Smax = S0 / q2, triggering dust ejection, sublimation-driven comae, and non-gravitational jet accelerations in active asteroids and main-belt comets (MBCs).
    • Meteoroid Stream & NEO Encounter Timing: For Near-Earth Objects (NEOs) and parent bodies of meteoroid showers, Tq anchors the orbital phase of peak thermal stress, tidal/rotational disruption risk, and inner-solar-system node crossings.
    • Peak Intrinsic Brightness: At fixed phase angle and geocentric distance, a minor planet reaches its maximum intrinsic solar illumination at Tq.
Osculating Two-Body Approximation Caveat: Computing Tq = t0 + ΔMq / n propagates the osculating Keplerian ellipse at t0 without planetary perturbations. Selecting the nearest perihelion branch (ΔMq,nearest ∈ (−180°, +180°], so |Tq,nearest − t0| ≤ P/2) minimizes perturbation drift between t0 and Tq; for bodies undergoing close planetary encounters (e.g., Apollo/Aten NEOs or Jupiter-family comets), numerical N-body integration provides sub-minute perihelion passage accuracy.

4. Fixed-Width Source Columns & Computation from MPCORB.DAT

The table below summarizes the fixed-width columns (1-based indexing) in a standard 202-character MPCORB.DAT record required to compute Tq:

Parameter Columns (1-based) 0-Based Slice Fortran Format Unit & Role
Epoch (t0) 21–25 line[20:25] A5 Packed MPC date (0h TT); reference Julian Date t0
M (M0, Mean anomaly) 27–35 line[26:35] F9.5 Degrees (°); orbital phase at t0 (M = 0° at perihelion)
n (Daily mean motion) 81–91 line[80:91] F11.8 Degrees per day (deg/day); angular rate dM/dt
Tq (Perihelion date) Derived (not stored) t0_jd + (((-M0 + 180) % 360) - 180) / n Computed Julian Date (JDTT) / Gregorian TT timestamp at M = 0°
