Numeric Eccentricity (e) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Dimensionless Conic Shape Parameter, Dynamical Invariants, and MPCORB Encoding

1. Fundamental Geometric & Astrodynamical Definition

Terminological Precision (“Numeric” vs. “Linear” Eccentricity): In analytical geometry and European celestial mechanics (e.g., German numerische Exzentrizität vs. lineare Exzentrizität), the qualifier “numeric” explicitly distinguishes the dimensionless ratio e = c/a from the dimensional linear eccentricity c = ae = √(a2 − b2) (which carries units of length, such as AU). In English astrodynamics and IAU Minor Planet Center documentation, “numeric eccentricity” is universally referred to simply as orbital eccentricity and denoted by the lowercase symbol e.

Mathematically, the numeric eccentricity e of a Keplerian trajectory is a non-negative, dimensionless scalar (e ≥ 0) that uniquely classifies the geometric shape and topological boundedness of the conic section traversed by a Solar System body around the Sun:

e = c a = √ 1 − b2 a2 = √ 1 − p a = Q − q Q + q = r ddir

where c is the linear eccentricity (distance from the ellipse center to either focus), a is the semi-major axis, b is the semi-minor axis, p is the semi-latus rectum, q is the perihelion distance, Q is the aphelion distance, and ddir is the perpendicular distance from the body to the primary directrix.

1.1 Conic-Section Classification Regimes

  • e = 0 (Circle): Degenerate ellipse with coincident foci (c = 0), constant heliocentric radius r = a = q = Q, and uniform orbital speed.
  • 0 < e < 1 (Ellipse — Bound Minor Planets): Closed periodic orbit with negative specific orbital energy (ℰ < 0). Every record in MPCORB.DAT belongs strictly to this domain.
  • e = 1 (Parabola — Zero-Energy Escape): Unbound open trajectory with zero specific orbital energy (ℰ = 0), infinite semi-major axis (a → ∞), and p = 2q.
  • e > 1 (Hyperbola — Interstellar/Unbound): Unbound open trajectory with positive specific orbital energy (ℰ > 0), negative semi-major axis (a < 0), and asymptotic excess speed v∞ = √(−μ/a).

2. Physical & Vectorial Invariants in Two-Body Dynamics

Dynamically, the numeric eccentricity e is the Euclidean norm of the conserved Laplace–Runge–Lenz (eccentricity) vector e, which points from the primary focus (the Sun) directly toward the perihelion vertex:

e = v × h μ − r r = &Bigl( v2 μ − 1 r &Bigr) r − r · v μ v  ,    e = |e|

In terms of the conserved specific orbital energy ℰ = v2/2 − μ/r = −μ/(2a) and specific relative orbital angular momentum h = |r × v| = √(μ p), the numeric eccentricity is governed by the fundamental energy–angular-momentum invariant:

e = √ 1 + 2 ℰ h2 μ2

Furthermore, e appears directly in Kepler’s equation relating mean anomaly M to eccentric anomaly E (M = E − e sin E), as well as in the eccentricity angle ϕ = arcsin(e) (historically tabulated in astronomical almanacs so that b = a cos ϕ and p = a cos2 ϕ).

3. Specific Encoding, Conventions & Role in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database published by the International Astronomical Union’s Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC):

  • Exact Fixed-Width Field in MPCORB.DAT: The osculating numeric eccentricity e is stored explicitly as a primary orbital element in columns 71–79 (0-based slice line[70:79]) using the fixed-point Fortran format specifier F9.7 (1 integer digit, decimal point, and 7 fractional digits, e.g., 0.0785354 for (1) Ceres).
  • Strict Domain Constraint (0 ≤ e < 1): By catalog specification, MPCORB.DAT includes only bound heliocentric minor planets with 0.0000000 ≤ e < 1.0000000. Objects whose osculating heliocentric eccentricity reaches or exceeds unity (e ≥ 1)—such as parabolic/hyperbolic comets and interstellar objects like 1I/ʻOumuamua (e ≈ 1.201) or 2I/Borisov (e ≈ 3.357)—are excluded from MPCORB.DAT and published in COMET.DAT (which uses format F10.8 for e).
  • Osculating Heliocentric Reference Frame: The tabulated value of e in MPCORB.DAT is an osculating heliocentric quantity referred to the solar center of mass (not the Solar System barycenter) at the record’s packed Epoch (columns 21–25) in the J2000.0 mean ecliptic coordinate system.
  • Universal Coupling to Derived Orbital Dimensions: Every derived radial and chord metric of an MPCORB record is generated by combining e (columns 71–79) with the semi-major axis a (columns 93–103):
    • Perihelion distance: q = a(1 − e)
    • Aphelion distance: Q = a(1 + e)
    • Linear eccentricity (focal offset): c = ae
    • Semi-minor axis: b = a√(1 − e2)
    • Semi-latus rectum (focal parameter): p = a(1 − e2)
Singularity & Precision Considerations in MPCORB Pipelines:
1) Low-eccentricity singularity (e → 0): When e approaches zero, the argument of perihelion ω and mean anomaly M become poorly conditioned (ill-defined at e = 0), and directrix distances (p/e) diverge. Equinoctial elements (heq = e sin(Ω + ω), keq = e cos(Ω + ω)) eliminate this coordinate singularity during numerical integration.
2) High-eccentricity cancellation (e → 1−): Because MPCORB.DAT truncates e to 7 decimal places (resolution Δe = 10−7), for an object with a = 1000 AU and e = 0.9990000, a 1-ULP rounding step in e shifts q = a(1 − e) by 10−4 AU (≈ 15,000 km).
