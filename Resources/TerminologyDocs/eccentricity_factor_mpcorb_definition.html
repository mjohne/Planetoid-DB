Eccentricity Factor in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Dimensionless Eccentricity Scaling Functions in Keplerian Mechanics, and Derivation from MPCORB

1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Mathematical Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In two-body Keplerian celestial mechanics and minor-planet catalog analysis, the eccentricity factor (also termed dimensionless eccentricity scaling factor or eccentricity multiplier) denotes a pure, dimensionless function of the orbital eccentricity e alone—independent of the semi-major axis a and solar gravitational parameter μ⊙—that modulates the circular-orbit baseline (e = 0, where the factor equals unity) to yield the corresponding geometric, kinematic, or anomaly-conversion quantity of an eccentric orbit.

In Keplerian orbital dynamics, almost every canonical property X(a, e) of a bound elliptic orbit (0 ≤ e < 1) factorizes multiplicatively into a circular-orbit scale X0(a) and a dimensionless eccentricity factor fX(e) normalized such that fX(0) = 1:

X(a, e) = X0(a) · fX(e),      with   fX(0) = 1

Depending on the physical quantity under evaluation, the term eccentricity factor refers specifically to one of the following foundational dimensionless functions of e:

A. Kinematic & Velocity Eccentricity Factors (Scaling vc = √(μ⊙/a))

fq(e) ≡ vq vc = √ 1 + e 1 − e   (Perihelion velocity factor, 1 ≤ fq < ∞)
fQ(e) ≡ vQ vc = √ 1 − e 1 + e = 1 fq(e)   (Aphelion velocity factor, 0 < fQ ≤ 1)
f⟨v⟩(e) ≡ ⟨v⟩t vc = 2 E(e) π ≈ 1 − 14e2 − 364e4   (Time-averaged speed factor, 2/π < f⟨v⟩ ≤ 1)

B. Geometric & Dynamical Eccentricity Factors (Scaling a and hc = √(μ⊙a))

  • Apsidal Distance Factors (1 ∓ e): Scale the semi-major axis a to perihelion distance q = a(1 − e) and aphelion distance Q = a(1 + e).
  • Semi-Minor Axis & Angular Momentum Factor (√(1 − e2)): Scales semi-major axis to semi-minor axis b = a√(1 − e2), circular area A = πa2√(1 − e2), and circular specific angular momentum h = √(μ⊙a)√(1 − e2).
  • Semi-Latus Rectum Factor (1 − e2): Scales semi-major axis to semi-latus rectum p = a(1 − e2) and specific angular momentum squared h2 = μ⊙a(1 − e2).
  • True-to-Eccentric Anomaly Conversion Factor (√[(1 + e)/(1 − e)]): Also serves as the exact tangent half-angle multiplier linking eccentric anomaly E to true anomaly ν via tan(ν/2) = √[(1 + e)/(1 − e)] tan(E/2).
Figure 1: Evolution of the four principal dimensionless eccentricity factors across 0 ≤ e ≤ 0.9. At e = 0, every factor equals 1; as e increases, fq(e) = √[(1+e)/(1−e)] diverges as (1 − e)−1/2, whereas fQ(e) vanishes as (1 − e)+1/2.

2. Taxonomy of Canonical Eccentricity Factors in Keplerian Mechanics

In astrodynamical software and derived-element tables built on MPCORB.DAT, isolating the dimensionless eccentricity factor separates semi-major-axis scaling (a) from orbital-shape modulation (e):

Eccentricity Factor Exact Formula Range on 0 ≤ e < 1 Circular Baseline & Derived Quantity
Perihelion Velocity Factor (fq) √[(1 + e) / (1 − e)] [1, ∞) Multiplies vc to give vq; also equals tan(ν/2) / tan(E/2)
Aphelion Velocity Factor (fQ) √[(1 − e) / (1 + e)] (0, 1] Multiplies vc to give vQ; exact reciprocal of fq (fqfQ = 1)
Time-Averaged Speed Factor (f⟨v⟩) 2 E(e) / π (2/π, 1] Multiplies vc to give ⟨v⟩t and 2πa to give perimeter Lellipse
Angular Momentum / Axis Factor (fh) √(1 − e2) (0, 1] Multiplies √(μ⊙a) to give h, and a to give semi-minor axis b
Semi-Latus Rectum Factor (fp) 1 − e2 (0, 1] Multiplies a to give semi-latus rectum p (fp = fh2)
Apsidal Distance Factors (fr,∓) 1 − e  and  1 + e (0, 1] & [1, 2) Multiply a to give perihelion q and aphelion Q

3. Specific Status and Role in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database published by the International Astronomical Union’s Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC):

  • Direct Evaluation from Tabulated Eccentricity e: MPCORB.DAT tabulates the osculating orbital eccentricity e in fixed-width columns 71–79 (Fortran format F9.7, 7 decimal places). Because every eccentricity factor depends exclusively on e, it can be evaluated directly from columns 71–79 without requiring semi-major axis a or mean motion n.
  • Computational Factorization & Decoupled Evaluation: In high-throughput catalog pipelines processing over 1.4 × 106 minor-planet records in MPCORB.DAT, computing vc once from a and multiplying by the respective eccentricity factors fq(e), fQ(e), and f⟨v⟩(e) exposes the pure departure from circular motion:
    • Perihelion & Aphelion Velocity Factors: vq = vc · fq(e) and vQ = vc / fq(e) require only a single square root √[(1 + e)/(1 − e)] for both apsidal speeds simultaneously.
    • True-Anomaly Kepler Solver Factor: The same factor fq(e) = √[(1 + e)/(1 − e)] converts eccentric anomaly E (solved from Kepler’s equation M = E − e sin E) to true anomaly ν at every propagation epoch.
  • Domain & Precision Behavior Across MPCORB Populations:
    • Main-Belt Asteroids (0 ≤ e ≲ 0.35): The apsidal velocity factor stays close to unity (1.00 ≤ fq(e) ≲ 1.44), while f⟨v⟩(e) > 0.969.
    • High-Eccentricity NEAs, Damocloids & Comets (0.7 ≤ e < 1): As e → 1−, fq(e) = √[(1 + e)/(1 − e)] grows steeply (e.g., fq(0.99) = 14.1067), magnifying numerical sensitivity to the F9.7 precision (Δe = ±5 × 10−8) of MPCORB.DAT via Δfq / fq = Δe / (1 − e2).
    • Hyperbolic Records (e > 1): Bound-orbit eccentricity factors containing (1 − e) or (1 − e2) become imaginary for e > 1 and must be replaced by their hyperbolic counterparts √(e2 − 1) and √[(e + 1)/(e − 1)].
Disambiguation Note: Because “eccentricity factor” is a contextual umbrella term in astrodynamics, always specify which scaling function is meant when documenting catalog columns—most commonly fq(e) = √[(1 + e)/(1 − e)] (perihelion velocity / true-anomaly multiplier), fQ(e) = √[(1 − e)/(1 + e)] (aphelion velocity multiplier), or fh(e) = √(1 − e2) (angular momentum / semi-minor axis multiplier).

4. Fixed-Width Source Column & Evaluation from MPCORB.DAT

In a standard 202-character MPCORB.DAT record, all dimensionless eccentricity factors depend solely on the eccentricity field in columns 71–79:

Parameter Columns (1-based) 0-Based Slice Fortran Format Unit & Role
e (Eccentricity) 71–79 line[70:79] F9.7 Dimensionless (0 ≤ e < 1 for bound orbits); sole input variable
fq(e) (Perihelion factor) Derived (not stored) sqrt((1+e)/(1-e)) Computed Dimensionless (≥ 1); vq / vc and tan(ν/2) / tan(E/2)
fQ(e) (Aphelion factor) Derived (not stored) sqrt((1-e)/(1+e)) Computed Dimensionless (≤ 1); vQ / vc = 1 / fq(e)
f⟨v⟩(e) (Mean-speed factor) Derived (not stored) 2 * E(e) / pi Computed Dimensionless (2/π < f ≤ 1); ⟨v⟩t / vc and Lellipse / (2πa)
fh(e) (Angular-momentum factor) Derived (not stored) sqrt(1 - e * e) Computed Dimensionless (≤ 1); h / √(μ⊙a) and b / a
