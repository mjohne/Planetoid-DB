True Longitude (Ltrue)

Three-Segment Actual Orbital Longitude, Keplerian Conversion & Ecliptic Projection in MPCORB.DAT

1. Formal Definition

In celestial mechanics, astrodynamics, and derived orbital parameterizations computed from the Minor Planet Center Orbit Database (MPCORB), the true longitude (Ltrue or ℓ) is the composite, broken-plane ("three-segment dog-leg") orbital phase angle that specifies the instantaneous actual angular position of a minor body along its osculating Keplerian orbit relative to the inertial J2000.0 vernal equinox (♈). For prograde orbits, Ltrue(t) is defined as the modular sum of the longitude of the ascending node (Ω), the argument of the perihelion (ω), and the instantaneous true anomaly (ν(t)):

Ltrue(t) = Ω + ω + ν(t) = ϖ + ν(t) = Ω + u(t) &pmod{360°} ∈ [0°, 360°) (Eq. 1)

where:

  • ϖ = (Ω + ω) &pmod{360°} is the longitude of perihelion, uniting the ecliptic azimuth Ω (from ♈ to the ascending node ☊) with the in-plane angle ω (from ☊ to perihelion P).
  • ν(t) (often denoted f(t) or θ(t)) is the instantaneous true anomaly measured at the Sun from perihelion P to the actual heliocentric position vector r(t) in the orbital plane.
  • u(t) = ω + ν(t) is the instantaneous argument of latitude measured in the orbital plane directly from the ascending node ☊ to the body's position vector r(t).
Figure 1: Three-segment broken-plane geometry of the true longitude (Ltrue = Ω + ω + ν) compared against the mean longitude (L = Ω + ω + M) and the projected heliocentric ecliptic longitude (λ). While Ltrue and L both sum segments across the ecliptic and orbital planes, λ projects the body's position vector r(t) orthogonally onto the J2000.0 ecliptic plane.

2. Mathematical Relationships & Heliocentric State Vector

Computing Ltrue(t) from a Keplerian record requires solving Kepler's equation E − e sin E = M(t) for the eccentric anomaly E(t) and transforming to the true anomaly ν(t) via the half-angle tangent identity:

ν(t) = 2 arctan &left;[ √&left( rac{1 + e}{1 − e} ight) tan rac{E(t)}{2} &right;], &quad; Ltrue(t) = (Ω + ω + ν(t)) &pmod{360°} (Eq. 2)

The angular rate of change of Ltrue under two-body motion follows directly from conservation of specific angular momentum h = r2 ˙ν = n a2 √(1 − e2):

rac{dLtrue}{dt} = rac{dν}{dt} = n rac{(1 + e cos ν)2}{(1 − e2)3/2} = n \left( rac{a}{r} ight)2 √(1 − e2) (Eq. 3)

Furthermore, grouping u = ω + ν = Ltrue − Ω expresses the J2000.0 heliocentric ecliptic position vector r = (X, Y, Z)T and the projected heliocentric ecliptic spherical coordinates (λ, β) cleanly in terms of Ltrue and Ω:

X = r [cos Ω cos(Ltrue − Ω) − sin Ω sin(Ltrue − Ω) cos i]
Y = r [sin Ω cos(Ltrue − Ω) + cos Ω sin(Ltrue − Ω) cos i]
Z = r sin(Ltrue − Ω) sin i, &quad; λ = atan2(Y, X) (Eq. 4)

3. Derivation from MPCORB.DAT & Comparison of Longitudes

MPCORB.DAT stores the osculating elements at epoch t0 (columns 21–103) from which Ltrue(t) is evaluated numerically. The table below contrasts the three distinct longitude definitions frequently encountered in orbital software:

Longitude Quantity Mathematical Formula Physical & Coordinate Interpretation
True Longitude (Ltrue) (Ω + ω + ν) &pmod{360°} Broken-plane ("dog-leg") actual orbital phase from J2000.0 equinox ♈; non-singular as e → 0 and i → 0°.
Mean Longitude (L) (Ω + ω + M) &pmod{360°} Broken-plane uniform-rate phase; differs from Ltrue by the equation of the center ν − M ≈ 2e sin M.
Ecliptic Longitude (λ) (Ω + atan2(sin u cos i, cos u)) &pmod{360°} Single-plane orthogonal projection of r(t) onto the J2000.0 ecliptic plane; differs from Ltrue whenever i ≠ 0° and u ≠ 90°k.

4. Dynamical & Astrodynamical Applications

  • Singularity-Free Actual Phase at e = 0 and i = 0°: Just as mean longitude L = Ω + ω + M regularizes the mean orbital phase when e → 0 or i → 0°, Ltrue = Ω + ω + ν provides a well-defined, continuous actual angular position for near-circular, low-inclination orbits (such as classical Kuiper belt objects or inner-belt asteroids).
  • True-Anomaly-Based Perturbation Equations (Gauss's Variational Equations): In numerical integration and analytical perturbation theory formulated with ν or Ltrue as the fast independent angular variable (e.g., Hansen–Tisserand or Sundman-regularized formulations), dLtrue/dt enters directly into the variational equations for the equinoctial elements (h, k, p, q).
  • Exact Nodal Crossings & Conjunction Geometry: Because u = Ltrue − Ω, the minor body crosses the J2000.0 ecliptic plane (Z = 0, β = 0°) precisely when Ltrue = Ω (ascending node, λ = Ω) and Ltrue = (Ω + 180°) &pmod{360°} (descending node, λ = Ω + 180°), at which points Ltrue and the projected ecliptic longitude λ are strictly identical.

5. Worked Examples from MPCORB.DAT

The following two numbered minor planets from MPCORB.DAT (epoch K25BL = 2025 Nov 21.0 TT = JD 2461000.5) illustrate the evaluation of Ltrue(t0) at epoch and its comparison against L0 and projected ecliptic longitude λ(t0):

Example 1: (1) Ceres — Low Eccentricity (Ltrue(t0) = 18.08116°)

Excerpt from MPCORB.DAT (columns 1–103): 

00001    3.34  0.12 K25BL 231.53975   73.28579   80.25414   10.58688  0.0789158  0.21410467   2.7675059
  • Extracted Epoch Elements: M0 = 231.53975°, ω = 73.28579°, Ω = 80.25414°, i = 10.58688°, and e = 0.0789158.
  • Solved True Anomaly & True Longitude at Epoch: Solving Kepler's equation yields eccentric anomaly E0 = 227.94383° and true anomaly ν0 = 224.54123° (equation of the center ν0 − M0 = −6.99852°). Summing 80.25414° + 73.28579° + 224.54123° = 378.08116° ≡ 18.08116° &pmod{360°} gives Ltrue(t0) = 18.08116° (compared to mean longitude L0 = 25.07968° and projected ecliptic longitude λ(t0) = 18.43195° at β(t0) = −9.35472°).
