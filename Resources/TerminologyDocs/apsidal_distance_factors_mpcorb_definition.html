Apsidal Distance Factors (1 − e, 1 + e) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Radial Extremum Scaling of the Semi-Major Axis, and Derivation from MPCORB

1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Geometric Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In two-body Keplerian celestial mechanics, the apsidal distance factors (also termed perihelion and aphelion distance factors or radial extremum multipliers, denoted fq,r(e) = 1 − e and fQ,r(e) = 1 + e) are the two dimensionless linear functions of orbital eccentricity e that scale the semi-major axis a to the minimum (perihelion, q) and maximum (aphelion, Q) heliocentric distances of a Keplerian orbit.

Evaluating the polar conic equation r(ν) = a(1 − e2) / (1 + e cos ν) at the two apsides—perihelion (ν = 0°, cos ν = +1) and aphelion (ν = 180°, cos ν = −1)—yields the exact factorization:

fr,−(e) ≡ qa = 1 − e,        fr,+(e) ≡ Qa = 1 + e

For bound elliptic orbits (0 ≤ e < 1, a > 0), the perihelion factor lies in (0, 1] and the aphelion factor lies in [1, 2), collapsing to fr,−(0) = fr,+(0) = 1 in the circular limit.

Algebraic & Geometric Identities

  • Additive Symmetry (Mean Apsidal Distance): Their arithmetic mean equals unity for every bound ellipse, ½[(1 − e) + (1 + e)] = 1, expressing the identity ½(q + Q) = a.
  • Linear Eccentricity Separation: Their half-difference equals the orbital eccentricity itself, ½[(1 + e) − (1 − e)] = e = c / a, where c = ae is the focal offset from the geometric center of the ellipse to the Sun.
  • Product & Ratio Identities: Their product yields the semi-latus rectum factor (1 − e)(1 + e) = 1 − e2 = p / a = qQ / a2, while their ratio gives the apsidal ratio (1 + e)/(1 − e) = Q / q = (vq / vc)2.
  • Solar Insolation Scaling: Because radiative flux varies as r−2, the apsidal distance factors govern peak and minimum insolation relative to mean circular flux F(a) via Fq / F(a) = (1 − e)−2 and FQ / F(a) = (1 + e)−2.

2. Comparative Overview Across Orbital Regimes

Orbital Regime Eccentricity e Perihelion Factor (1 − e) Aphelion Factor (1 + e) Physical Interpretation
Circular Orbit e = 0 1.0 1.0 q = Q = a (no radial excursion)
Bound Elliptic Orbit 0 < e < 1 (0, 1) (1, 2) 0 < q < a < Q < 2a
Parabolic Limit e = 1 0 (a → ∞) 2 (Q → ∞) q = p/2 must be parameterized directly
Hyperbolic Trajectory e > 1 (a < 0) < 0 (with a < 0) > 2 q = a(1 − e) > 0 remains positive; Q undefined

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

  • Direct Evaluation from Tabulated Eccentricity e: In MPCORB.DAT, orbital eccentricity e is stored in columns 71–79 (F9.7). Both apsidal distance factors (1 − e) and (1 + e) are computed directly from this single field without requiring a.
  • Gateway to Derived Apsidal Distances (q and Q): Neither q nor Q is stored in MPCORB.DAT; both are obtained by multiplying semi-major axis a (columns 93–103, F11.7) by (1 − e) and (1 + e), respectively, which underpin MPC dynamical class boundaries (e.g., Amor 1.017 < q ≤ 1.3 AU, Apollo q ≤ 1.017 AU, a ≥ 1.0 AU, Aten Q > 0.983 AU, a < 1.0 AU, Atira Q < 0.983 AU).
  • Numerical Precision Near e → 1−: Because e in MPCORB.DAT has 7-decimal-place resolution (Δe = ±5 × 10−8), the relative uncertainty of the perihelion factor Δ(1 − e)/(1 − e) = Δe/(1 − e) increases for high-eccentricity comets and sungrazers, whereas (1 + e) remains well-conditioned everywhere.
