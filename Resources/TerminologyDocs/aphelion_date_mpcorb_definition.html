Aphelion Date (TQ) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Keplerian Mean-Motion Kinematics, Epoch-to-Aphelion Time-of-Flight Equations, and Derivation from MPCORB

1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Chronological Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In two-body Keplerian celestial mechanics and ephemeris chronology, the aphelion date (or epoch of aphelion passage, denoted TQ, tQ, or JDQ, and paired half a revolution away with the perihelion date Tq)—is the instant in Terrestrial Time (TT, expressed as a Julian Date JDTT, Modified Julian Date MJD, or Gregorian calendar timestamp) at which a bound minor planet (0 ≤ e < 1) reaches the aphelion (Q) of its osculating Keplerian ellipse—the point of maximum heliocentric distance rmax = Q = a(1 + e), minimum orbital speed vmin = √[μ(1 − e) / (a(1 + e))], true anomaly ν = 180° (π radians), eccentric anomaly E = 180° (π radians), and mean anomaly M = 180° (π radians).

Under unperturbed two-body Keplerian motion, the mean anomaly M evolves linearly with Terrestrial Time t according to:

M(t) = M0 + n (t − t0)   (mod 360°)

where t0 is the osculating epoch (JDTT), M0 is the mean anomaly at epoch t0 (degrees), and n is the daily mean motion (deg/day), related to the semi-major axis a (AU) via Kepler’s third law n = kdeg a−3/2 with the Gaussian gravitational constant k = 0.01720209895 rad/day (kdeg = 0.9856076686 deg/day, neglecting minor-planet mass m ≪ M⊙).

Setting M(TQ) ≡ 180° (mod 360°) yields the exact osculating aphelion date TQ (JDTT). Depending on the analytical convention required, three canonical branches of TQ are defined relative to the osculating epoch t0 and orbital period P = 360° / n (days):

TQ,nearest = t0 + ΔMnearest n  ,     ΔMnearest = [(180° − M0 + 180°) mod 360°] − 180° ∈ (−180°, +180°]
TQ,next = t0 + (180° − M0) mod 360° n  ,     TQ,prev = TQ,next − P

Key dynamical and chronological properties of the aphelion date TQ include:

  • Half-Period Conjugacy with the Perihelion Date (Tq): Because aphelion (M = 180°) and perihelion (M = 0°) are separated by ΔM = ±180°, the osculating aphelion and perihelion dates are displaced by exactly half an orbital period P/2 = 180°/n:
    TQ = Tq ± P 2 = Tq ± 180° n
  • Minimum-Deviation Osculating Epoch (TQ,nearest): Selecting the branch ΔMnearest ∈ (−180°, +180°] minimizes |TQ − t0| ≤ P/2, thereby minimizing secular and planetary-perturbation drift between the osculating epoch t0 and the predicted aphelion passage.
  • Non-Existence for Unbound Trajectories (e ≥ 1): Unlike the perihelion date Tq—which remains well-defined for parabolic (e = 1) and hyperbolic (e > 1) comets and interstellar objects—the aphelion date TQ is physically undefined for e ≥ 1 because an open trajectory recedes to r → ∞ without an apocenter.
  • Circular Orbit Degeneracy (e = 0): For an exact circular orbit (e = 0), the line of apsides is geometrically indeterminate; by formal convention using the tabulated M0, the formulas evaluate smoothly to the instant where M(t) = 180°.

2. Decoding the MPC Packed Epoch & Time-Scale Conventions

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database published by the International Astronomical Union’s Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC), the osculating epoch t0 is stored in columns 21–25 using the MPC’s 5-character packed date format (CYYMD) on the Terrestrial Time (TT) scale at 0h TT (.5 fractional Julian Day):

  • Character 1 (C — Century code): I = 1800–1899, J = 1900–1999, K = 2000–2099 (e.g., K → 2000).
  • Characters 2–3 (YY — Two-digit year): Decimal year within the century (e.g., 25 → 2025).
  • Character 4 (M — Month code): Base-36 / hexadecimal digit: 1–9 for January–September, A = October (10), B = November (11), C = December (12).
  • Character 5 (D — Day-of-month code): Base-36 digit: 1–9 for days 1–9, A–V for days 10–31 (e.g., L = 21).
t0 (JDTT) = GregorianToJD(Y, M, D, 00:00:00 TT)

For example, packed epoch K25BL decodes to 2025-11-21 00:00:00 TT (JDTT = 2461000.5). Adding ΔMQ / n (in days) produces TQ directly in JDTT, which converts back to a calendar date in Terrestrial Time (or UTC via UTC = TT − ΔT, where ΔT ≈ 69.184 s in the 2020s).

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB database:

  • Implicit Derived Chronological Quantity (Not Tabulated): Unlike the MPC’s comet element file (COMET.DAT), which explicitly tabulates the perihelion date Tq, MPCORB.DAT parameterizes orbital phase exclusively via the mean anomaly at epoch M0 and does not store either Tq or TQ. Instead, TQ is derived directly from three osculating columns in every MPCORB.DAT record:
    • Epoch t0 (columns 21–25, packed 5-character format A5, 0h TT);
    • Mean anomaly at epoch M0 (columns 27–35, format F9.5, degrees);
    • Daily mean motion n (columns 81–91, format F11.8, deg/day)—or equivalently from semi-major axis a (columns 93–103, format F11.7, AU) via n = 0.9856076686 a−3/2.
  • Physical & Observational Significance in Minor-Planet Studies:
    • Minimum Heliocentric Insolation & Sublimation Quiescence: For main-belt comets (MBCs), active asteroids, and Jupiter-family comets, TQ marks the thermal minimum of the orbit where volatile sublimation (water/CO2 ice outgassing) reaches its lowest level and bare-nucleus photometry is least contaminated by a coma.
    • Outer-Solar-System Opposition & Aphelion Geometry: For resonant Trans-Neptunian Objects (TNOs) and Centaurs, TQ identifies the epoch of maximum heliocentric distance Q = a(1 + e) and faintest apparent visual magnitude Vmax.
Osculating Two-Body Approximation Caveat: Because MPCORB.DAT provides two-body osculating elements at epoch t0, computing TQ = t0 + ΔMQ / n assumes unperturbed Keplerian motion between t0 and TQ. For typical main-belt asteroids (P ≈ 3–6 yr), planetary perturbations shift the true barycentric/heliocentric aphelion passage by only a few hours to a few days over half an orbit; for long-period comets (P > 50 yr) or bodies undergoing close Jupiter encounters, full N-body numerical integration is required for exact aphelion timing.

4. Fixed-Width Source Columns & Computation from MPCORB.DAT

The table below summarizes the fixed-width columns (1-based indexing) in a standard 202-character MPCORB.DAT record required to compute TQ:

Parameter Columns (1-based) Python Slice (0-based) Fortran Format Unit & Role
Epoch (t0) 21–25 line[20:25] A5 Packed MPC date (0h TT); reference Julian Date t0
M (M0, Mean anomaly) 27–35 line[26:35] F9.5 Degrees (°); orbital phase at t0 (M = 180° at aphelion)
n (Daily mean motion) 81–91 line[80:91] F11.8 Degrees per day (deg/day); angular rate dM/dt
TQ (Aphelion date) Derived (not stored) t0_jd + (((180 - M0 + 180) % 360) - 180) / n Computed Julian Date (JDTT) / Gregorian TT timestamp at M = 180°
