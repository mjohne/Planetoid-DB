Reference (Ref, Columns 108–116)

IAU Minor Planet Center Publication Identifier (FORTRAN A9: MPO, MPEC, MPC & MPS) in MPCORB.DAT

1. Formal Definition

In the Minor Planet Center Orbit Database (MPCORB), the Reference (Ref, officially specified by the IAU Minor Planet Center as the “Reference” field in columns 108–116, 1-indexed, 9-character left-justified ASCII string formatted as FORTRAN A9, with 0-based substring slice [107..116]) is a compact bibliographic provenance identifier recording the exact publication, circular, or electronic batch in which the osculating orbital elements (columns 1–105) were officially published or updated:

Ref = trim(Record[107 : 116]) ∈ ΣASCII≤9 (Eq. 1)

where:

  • Bibliographic Traceability: Every orbital solution in MPCORB.DAT represents a specific, reproducible least-squares parameter estimation performed by an orbit computer (recorded in columns 151–160) using a frozen astrometric dataset (Nobs in columns 118–122). The 9-character Reference field links the numeric state vector directly to the archival MPC publication stream where the differential correction, observation weights, and residuals were announced.
  • Field Delimitation: Columns 108–116 are bounded on the left by a single space at column 107 (immediately following the 1-character Uncertainty Parameter U at column 106) and on the right by a single space at column 117 (preceding Nobs in columns 118–122).

2. Taxonomy of MPC Reference Prefixes

Because the FORTRAN A9 container is strictly 9 ASCII characters wide, the Minor Planet Center uses standardized prefix codes to compress full bibliographic citations into 9 bytes:

Prefix Pattern Full Publication Name Format Structure & Astronomical Scope
MPO###### Minor Planet Circulars Orbit Supplement MPO followed by a 6-digit page number (e.g., MPO925614). Introduced in June 2000 to separate bulk orbit updates of numbered and multi-opposition minor planets from the main Minor Planet Circulars.
EYYYY-H## Minor Planet Electronic Circular (MPEC) E + 4-digit year + hyphen + half-month letter (A = Jan 1–15, B = Jan 16–31, …, Y = Dec 16–31; I omitted) + 2- or 3-character issue number (e.g., E2025-R84, E2024-X112). Used for Daily Orbit Updates (DOUs) and urgent NEA/TNO/comet orbit announcements.
MPC###### / MPC ##### Minor Planet Circulars (MPCs) MPC followed by a 5- or 6-digit page number (space-padded for legacy 5-digit pages like MPC 35412, contiguous for 6-digit pages like MPC112450). Primary pre-2000 orbit archive and ongoing venue for new permanent numberings and names.
MPS###### Minor Planet Circulars Supplement MPS followed by a 6-digit page number (e.g., MPS142810). Electronic supplement archiving bulk astrometric observations and associated preliminary orbital updates.
MPEC... / Legacy Pre-1998 MPEC or Special Catalog Tag Before the 9-character compression EYYYY-H## was standardized, early electronic circulars appeared as MPEC YYYY-H## (truncated or compressed to fit A9), and rare imported orbits carry source tags (e.g., JPL... or blank).

3. Fixed-Width Format Specifications & Parsing Rules

  • Exact Column Slice: Columns 108–116 (1-indexed), corresponding to 0-based substring line[107..116] (length 9).
  • Alignment & Padding: Left-justified (A9) with trailing ASCII spaces (0x20) when the trimmed string has fewer than 9 characters (for example, "E2025-R8" + " ", whereas "MPO925614" and "E2025-R84" fill all 9 bytes completely, leaving only the single separator space at column 117 before Nobs).
  • Chronological & Version Ordering: Within a given publication series (such as MPO###### or EYYYY-H##), monotonically increasing page/issue numbers indicate more recent orbital solutions; however, across different series (e.g., an MPO monthly batch vs. a Daily Orbit Update EYYYY-...), the exact date of the last included observation is explicitly given in columns 195–202 (YYYYMMDD).

4. Worked Examples from MPCORB.DAT

The following two records from MPCORB.DAT (epoch K25BL = 2025 Nov 21.0 TT = JD 2461000.5) demonstrate how the 9-character Reference field sits at the start of the post-epoch metadata block (columns 108–202):

Example 1: (1) Ceres — Monthly Orbit Supplement Reference (MPO925614)

Excerpt of MPCORB.DAT columns 108–202 for 00001: 

MPO925614 11432  98 1801-2025 0.61 M-v 30h JPL 48     1000   (1) Ceres                  20251018
  • Parsed Reference (Cols 108–116): "MPO925614" (all 9 character slots occupied).
  • Bibliographic Decoding: Published on page 925,614 of the Minor Planet Circulars Orbit Supplement (MPO), documenting the JPL solution JPL 48 fitted to 11,432 observations across 1801–2025.

Example 2: 2025 QA14 — Minor Planet Electronic Circular Reference (E2025-R84)

Excerpt of MPCORB.DAT columns 108–202 for 2025 QA14: 

E2025-R84    27   1    14 days 0.28 M-v 38k MPCLINUX   8403            2025 QA14          20250911
  • Parsed Reference (Cols 108–116): "E2025-R84" (all 9 character slots occupied).
  • Bibliographic Decoding: Published in MPEC 2025-R84 (E = Electronic Circular, year 2025, half-month R = September 1–15, issue 84), announcing the initial 14-day discovery orbit computed via MPCLINUX.
