Semi-Axis Factor (b/a = √(1 − e2) & a/q = (1 − e)−1) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Semi-Minor-to-Semi-Major Axis Ratio and Apsidal Semi-Axis Scaling, and Derivation from MPCORB

1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Geometric Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In two-body Keplerian celestial mechanics and minor-planet catalog design, the semi-axis factor (also termed ellipse aspect-ratio factor or semi-minor axis scaling factor, denoted fb/a(e) = b / a = √(1 − e2)) is the dimensionless function of orbital eccentricity e (0 ≤ e < 1) that scales the semi-major axis a to the semi-minor axis b = a√(1 − e2). In cometary and parabolic-limit parameterization, the complementary perihelion semi-axis factor a / q = (1 − e)−1 scales perihelion distance q to semi-major axis a.

From the Cartesian equation of an ellipse (x/a)2 + (y/b)2 = 1 and the focal-offset definition c = ae = √(a2 − b2), the principal axes obey:

fb/a(e) ≡ ba = √1 − e2 = √(q · Q)a,        fa/q(e) ≡ aq = 11 − e

Geometric & Elliptic Properties

  • Orbital Area Scaling: The planar area enclosed by the Keplerian ellipse is A = πab = πa2√(1 − e2), so b/a equals the ratio of elliptic area to the circumscribed auxiliary circle area πa2.
  • Ellipse Flattening (f): The geometric flattening of the orbital ellipse is f = (a − b)/a = 1 − √(1 − e2) = 1 − fb/a(e).
  • Exact Identity with Specific Angular Momentum Factor: Because h = √(μ⊙a)√(1 − e2), the semi-minor-to-semi-major axis ratio b/a is numerically identical to the angular momentum ratio h/hc.
  • Hyperbolic Semi-Conjugate Axis (e > 1): For hyperbolic trajectories (a < 0), the semi-conjugate axis is b = |a|√(e2 − 1), where b equals the asymptotic impact parameter b∞ relative to the Sun.

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

  • Direct Evaluation from Tabulated Elements: MPCORB.DAT tabulates semi-major axis a in columns 93–103 (F11.7, AU) and eccentricity e in columns 71–79 (F9.7). The semi-axis factor √(1 − e2) is computed from e alone and multiplies a to yield the derived semi-minor axis b.
  • 2D/3D Orbit Rendering & Cartesian Parametric Equations: In graphical orbit visualizers (such as WinForms/OpenGL renderers of MPCORB.DAT), an elliptic orbit in the perifocal plane is sampled smoothly in eccentric anomaly E via x(E) = a(cos E − e) and y(E) = b sin E = a√(1 − e2) sin E.
