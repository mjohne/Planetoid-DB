Specific Relative Angular Momentum (h)

Vector Invariant, Orbit-Normal Orientation & Kepler's Second Law in MPCORB.DAT

1. Formal Definition

In astrodynamics, celestial mechanics, and derived vectorial invariants computed from the Minor Planet Center Orbit Database (MPCORB), the specific relative angular momentum (h, with scalar magnitude h = ‖h‖) of an orbiting minor body is the conserved cross product of its instantaneous heliocentric position vector r and heliocentric velocity vector v per unit reduced mass in the central two-body problem:

h = r × v = h \, \hat{w},    h = ‖h‖ = r v cos γ = √μ p = √μ a (1 − e2) ≥ 0 (Eq. 1)

where:

  • a is the osculating semimajor axis (columns 93–103 of MPCORB.DAT, in AU), e is the orbital eccentricity (columns 71–79), and p = a(1 − e2) = h2 / μ is the semi-latus rectum (orbital parameter).
  • \hat{w} = h / h is the orbit-normal unit vector perpendicular to the osculating orbital plane (pointing toward the northern orbital pole in the right-hand sense of orbital motion), whose orientation in the J2000.0 mean ecliptic coordinate system is uniquely fixed by the longitude of the ascending node Ω (columns 49–57) and the orbital inclination i (columns 60–68).
  • μ = GM☉(1 + m/M☉) ≈ GM☉ is the heliocentric gravitational parameter, equal to k2 = 2.959122082855911 × 10−4 AU3/d2 in canonical Gaussian units or 1.32712440018 × 1011 km3/s2 in SI/astronautical units.
Figure 1: Orientation of the specific relative angular momentum vector h = r × v in the J2000.0 heliocentric mean ecliptic frame. Perpendicular to the osculating orbital plane, h is tilted from the north ecliptic pole (+Z) by the orbital inclination i (columns 60–68 of MPCORB.DAT), with its ecliptic projection oriented 90° clockwise behind the ascending node Ω (columns 49–57).

2. Evaluation from MPCORB.DAT Orbital Elements

In the central two-body problem (r̈ = −(μ / r3)r), the time derivative dh/dt = r × r̈ = 0 vanishes identically, making both the magnitude h and the spatial direction \hat{w} exact constants of unperturbed Keplerian motion. Using the osculating elements tabulated in MPCORB.DAT—longitude of the ascending node Ω (columns 49–57), inclination i (columns 60–68), eccentricity e (columns 71–79), and semimajor axis a (columns 93–103)—the complete 3-D J2000.0 ecliptic angular momentum vector is:

h = √μ a (1 − e2)   ( sin i sin Ω,   −sin i cos Ω,   cos i )T (Eq. 2)

Because the heliocentric radius vector r and velocity vector v always lie within the osculating orbital plane, h is strictly orthogonal to every instantaneous state vector (h · r = 0 and h · v = 0). Conversely, given a Cartesian state vector (r, v) from a numerical integrator, h = r × v recovers i, Ω, and p directly:

p = h2μ = a(1 − e2),    i = arccos &left(hzh&right),    Ω = atan2(hx, −hy) mod 360° (Eq. 3)

3. Physical & Geometric Interpretations

Physical / Mathematical Law Governing Relation Interpretation in MPCORB.DAT
Kepler's Second Law (Areal Velocity) dAdt = 12 ‖r × v‖ = h2 = const. The heliocentric radius vector sweeps equal areas in equal times; total orbital area is A = π a b = 12 h P.
Azimuthal Velocity Law vθ = r dνdt = hr Transverse speed scales inversely with heliocentric distance r: vq / vQ = Q / q = (1 + e)/(1 − e).
Ecliptic Normal Component (hz) hz = h cos i = √μ a (1 − e2) cos i Determines orbital sense: prograde (hz > 0, i < 90°), polar (hz = 0, i = 90°), or retrograde (hz < 0, i > 90°).
Energy–Momentum Coupling e = √1 + 2 ε h2μ2 Together with specific orbital energy ε = −μ/(2a), h uniquely fixes orbital eccentricity e.

4. Astrodynamical & Secular Perturbation Significance

  • Kozai–Lidov Oscillations & Conservation of hz: In the hierarchical three-body problem (Sun–Jupiter–minor body), quadrupole secular perturbations induce coupled oscillations between eccentricity e and inclination i while conserving the vertical component of specific angular momentum hz = √μ a (1 − e2) cos i (the Kozai integral ΘK = (1 − e2)cos2 i = hz2 / (μ a)).
  • Proper Elements & Asteroid Family Identification: While osculating h precesses around the invariable plane of the Solar System under planetary torques (dh/dt = r × apert), filtering out short-period and secular perturbations yields quasi-invariant proper actions (ap, ep, sin ip) used to identify collisional asteroid families in MPCORB.DAT.
  • Unit Systems in Astrodynamics: In SI/astronautical units, h is expressed in km2/s (or AU · km/s), where Earth's circular orbit at a = 1 AU has h⊕ = √μ · 1 AU = 4.45550 × 109 km2/s = 29.78469 AU · km/s; in canonical Gaussian units, h is expressed in AU2/d with h⊕ = k = 0.01720209895 AU2/d.
