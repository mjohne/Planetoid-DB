Precise Indicator of Perturbers (Columns 147–149)

Hexadecimal Asteroid-Perturber Bitmask & JPL Planetary Ephemeris Code (FORTRAN A3: "30h", "38k")

1. Formal Definition

In the Minor Planet Center Orbit Database (MPCORB), the Precise Indicator of Perturbers (officially specified by the IAU Minor Planet Center as the “Precise indicator of perturbers” field in columns 147–149, 1-indexed, 3-character ASCII string formatted as FORTRAN A3, with 0-based substring slice [146..149]) is a structured two-part alphanumeric code—consisting of a 2-digit hexadecimal bitmask (columns 147–148) followed by a 1-character planetary ephemeris identifier (column 149)—that specifies the exact set of massive minor-planet perturbers, relativistic post-Newtonian corrections, and JPL planetary ephemeris version used in the N-body orbit integration:

Perturbersprecise = Record[146 : 149] = HexMask2 || EphChar1 (Eq. 1)

where:

  • Columns 147–148 (2-Digit Hexadecimal Perturber & Physics Bitmask, 00–FF): Encodes an 8-bit integer B = int(Record[146:148], 16) ∈ [0, 255] whose individual binary flags indicate which massive main-belt asteroids (such as (1) Ceres, (2) Pallas, (4) Vesta, (10) Hygiea, and extended 16- to 300+-body asteroid sets), Pluto, and general-relativistic Schwarzschild terms were active alongside the major planets listed in columns 143–145 (M-v).
  • Column 149 (1-Character Planetary Ephemeris Version Code): A single lowercase/alphanumeric character identifying the underlying solar-system planetary development ephemeris (e.g., h = JPL DE405/DE406 or modern DE430/DE431 compatibility class, k = extended MPC production ephemeris / DE431+300 asteroid suite, f = JPL DE403, e = JPL DE200).
  • Field Delimitation: Columns 147–149 are preceded by a space at column 146 (after the Coarse indicator of perturbers in columns 143–145) and followed by a space at column 150 (before the Computer name in columns 151–160).

2. Bitmask & Ephemeris Code Reference Tables

2.1 Hexadecimal Perturber Bitmask (Columns 147–148)

Hex Code Binary (8-bit) Active Non-Planetary Perturbers & Force-Model Components
00 0000 0000 Major planets only (as specified in columns 143–145); no asteroid perturbers.
30 0011 0000 Standard JPL Horizons small-body perturber set: Pluto + post-Newtonian relativity + the 16/24 massive main-belt perturbers ((1) Ceres, (2) Pallas, (4) Vesta, (10) Hygiea, etc.).
38 0011 1000 Modern MPC production force model (MPCLINUX): Pluto + post-Newtonian relativity + extended ~300 main-belt asteroid perturber set.
3E 0011 1110 Specialized extended perturber mask used for high-precision multi-decade or resonant minor-planet solutions requiring additional massive asteroids/TNOs.

2.2 Planetary Ephemeris Character (Column 149)

Char (Col 149) Underlying JPL Planetary Ephemeris Historical & Operational Era
e JPL DE200 / LE200 (B1950/J2000 transition) Legacy MPC orbits integrated in the 1990s.
f JPL DE403 / LE403 Late-1990s MPC and JPL orbits prior to DE405 adoption.
h JPL DE405 / DE406 (and compatible DE430/DE431 exports) Standard ICRS/J2000 planetary ephemeris identifier used across JPL Horizons imports and post-2000 MPC solutions.
k JPL DE431+ (MPC modern production baseline) Current default ephemeris code emitted by the MPC MPCLINUX / ORBFIT orbit-determination pipeline.

3. Fixed-Width Format Specifications & Parsing Rules

  • Exact Column Slice: Columns 147–149 (1-indexed), corresponding to 0-based substring line[146..149] (length 3 characters), delimited by spaces at columns 146 and 150.
  • Case Sensitivity: Hexadecimal letters in columns 147–148 are uppercase (0–9, A–F), whereas the ephemeris identifier in column 149 is lowercase (e, f, h, k). Parsers should preserve exact string casing or split into bitmaskHex = slice[0..2] and ephemerisChar = slice[2].
  • Blank Handling: When columns 143–145 are blank (unperturbed two-body orbit), columns 147–149 are likewise 3 spaces (" ").

4. Worked Examples from MPCORB.DAT

The following two records from MPCORB.DAT (epoch K25BL = 2025 Nov 21.0 TT = JD 2461000.5) illustrate the two most prevalent precise perturber codes in modern catalogs:

Example 1: (1) Ceres — JPL Horizons Force Model (30h)

Excerpt of MPCORB.DAT columns 108–202 for 00001: 

MPO925614 11432  98 1801-2025 0.61 M-v 30h JPL 48     1000   (1) Ceres                  20251018
  • Parsed Precise Indicator (Cols 147–149): "30h" → hex bitmask 0x30 (48 decimal; Pluto, relativistic terms, and 16/24 massive asteroid perturbers) + ephemeris 'h' (JPL DE405/DE430/DE431 compatibility class).
  • Consistency with Computer Name: Directly matches "JPL 48" in columns 151–160.

Example 2: 2025 QA14 — MPC Production Pipeline Force Model (38k)

Excerpt of MPCORB.DAT columns 108–202 for 2025 QA14: 

E2025-R84    27   1    14 days 0.28 M-v 38k MPCLINUX   8403            2025 QA14          20250911
  • Parsed Precise Indicator (Cols 147–149): "38k" → hex bitmask 0x38 (56 decimal; Pluto, relativistic terms, and ~300 main-belt asteroid perturbers) + ephemeris 'k' (MPC DE431+ production baseline).
  • Consistency with Computer Name: Directly matches "MPCLINUX" in columns 151–160.
