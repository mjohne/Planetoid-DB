Eccentric Anomaly (E)

Auxiliary-Circle Geometry, Transcendental Inversion & Perifocal Coordinates in MPCORB.DAT

1. Formal Definition

In celestial mechanics, astrodynamics, and derived orbital computations based on the Minor Planet Center Orbit Database (MPCORB), the eccentric anomaly (E, occasionally denoted u in classical European literature) is the auxiliary geometric angle measured at the geometric center (O) of the osculating orbital ellipse between the direction of perihelion (P) and the radial vector to the point Q' obtained by projecting the orbiting body's actual position Q orthogonally—perpendicular to the major axis—onto the auxiliary circle of radius a (the semimajor axis) circumscribed about the ellipse. For bound elliptical orbits (0 ≤ e < 1), E(t) bridges the time-linear mean anomaly M(t) stored in MPCORB.DAT and the physical true anomaly ν(t) via Kepler's transcendental equation:

M(t) = E(t) − e sin E(t), &quad; where   M(t) = M0 + n(t − t0) (Eq. 1)

where:

  • M0 is the mean anomaly at epoch t0 (MPCORB.DAT, columns 27–35, in degrees), n is the mean daily motion (columns 81–91, in degrees/day), and e is the orbital eccentricity (columns 71–79, dimensionless).
  • In Eq. (1), both M(t) and E(t) must be expressed in radians when evaluating e sin E(t); in degrees, the equation takes the form Mdeg = Edeg − (180°/π) e sin Edeg.
  • E(t) always occupies the exact same half-plane (0 ≤ E ≤ π for 0 ≤ M ≤ π, and π < E < 2π for π < M < 2π) and strictly satisfies E = M = ν at perihelion (0°) and aphelion (180°), while bounding the anomalies for 0 < M < 180° as M < E < ν (and ν < E < M for 180° < M < 360°).
Figure 1: Geometric construction of the eccentric anomaly (E) measured at the ellipse center O to the auxiliary-circle projection Q', contrasted with the true anomaly (ν) measured at the Sun (focus) to the body Q. Orthogonal compression of the circle along the minor axis by the factor b/a = √(1 − e2) maps Q' = (a cos E, a sin E) directly onto Q.

2. Mathematical Identities, Perifocal State Vector & Differential Rate

Because the ellipse is obtained by affine scaling of the auxiliary circle by √(1 − e2) parallel to the semiminor axis and shifting the origin from the geometric center O to the focus (Sun) by c = ae, the perifocal position (xp, yp), heliocentric distance r, and perifocal velocity (vx,p, vy,p) depend solely on E without requiring trigonometric evaluation of ν:

xp = r cos ν = a(cos E − e), &quad; yp = r sin ν = a√(1 − e2) sin E, &quad; r = a(1 − e cos E) (Eq. 2)

Differentiating Kepler's equation M = E − e sin E with respect to time yields the exact time derivative of E and the perifocal orbital velocity components (μ = GM⊙ = n2a3):

rac{dE}{dt} = rac{n}{1 − e cos E} = rac{na}{r}, &quad; vx,p = − rac{na2}{r} sin E, &quad; vy,p = rac{na2√(1 − e2)}{r} cos E (Eq. 3)

Furthermore, the classical half-angle tangent relation linking E and ν avoids quadrant ambiguity when implemented via the two-argument arctangent:

ν(t) = 2 \, \mathrm{atan2} &left( √(1 + e) \sin rac{E(t)}{2}, \; √(1 − e) \cos rac{E(t)}{2} &right) (Eq. 4)

3. Numerical Solution of Kepler's Equation from MPCORB.DAT

MPCORB.DAT does not store E directly; instead, it supplies M0 (columns 27–35), e (columns 71–79), and n (columns 81–91). Because M = E − e sin E cannot be inverted in closed elementary form (except via infinite Kapteyn/Bessel series E = M + 2 ∑k=1∞ rac{1}{k} Jk(ke) sin(kM)), E(t) is computed numerically at every propagation step:

Solution Method Iteration / Series Formula Convergence & Domain of Applicability
Newton–Raphson Iteration Ek+1 = Ek − rac{Ek − e sin Ek − M}{1 − e cos Ek} Quadratic convergence; reaches IEEE-754 double precision (< 10−15 rad) in 3–5 iterations for e < 0.8 when initialized at E0 = M + e sin M.
Danby's Quartic Method δ1 = −f/f',   δ2 = −f/(f' + rac{1}{2}\delta1f''),   δ3 = −f/(f' + rac{1}{2}\delta2f'' + rac{1}{6}\delta22f''') Fourth-order convergence; converges in 2–3 iterations even for extreme eccentricities (0.9 ≤ e < 0.9999) near perihelion (M ≈ 0).
Fourier–Bessel Expansion E = M + (e − rac{1}{8}e3) sin M + rac{1}{2}e2 sin 2M + rac{3}{8}e3 sin 3M + \mathcal{O}(e4) Analytical series valid for low-eccentricity orbits (e < 0.6627434..., the Laplace limit); widely used as a high-accuracy initial seed E0.

4. Astrodynamical & Numerical Significance

  • Elimination of Trigonometric Division in State Propagation: Computing heliocentric state vectors (r, v) directly from (cos E, sin E) via Eqs. (2)–(3) requires only a single transcendental evaluation (sin E, cos E) per epoch, avoiding the intermediate conversion to ν and eliminating coordinate singularities as e → 0.
  • Universal Variable Regularization (e → 1): For near-parabolic comets (e ≈ 1) or hyperbolic interstellar objects (e > 1, where E generalizes to the hyperbolic eccentric anomaly H satisfying Mh = e sinh H − H), E is replaced by the Sundman-transformed universal anomaly χ = √a \, E, which smoothly unifies elliptical, parabolic, and hyperbolic two-body propagation in a single Stumpff-function formulation.

5. Worked Examples from MPCORB.DAT

The following two numbered minor planets from MPCORB.DAT (epoch K25BL = 2025 Nov 21.0 TT = JD 2461000.5) demonstrate the numerical inversion of Kepler's equation for E0 and the resulting perifocal coordinates and heliocentric distance r(t0):

Example 1: (1) Ceres — Low Eccentricity (E0 = 227.94383°)

Excerpt from MPCORB.DAT (columns 1–103): 

00001    3.34  0.12 K25BL 231.53975   73.28579   80.25414   10.58688  0.0789158  0.21410467   2.7675059
  • Extracted Epoch Elements: M0 = 231.53975° (4.0411310 rad), e = 0.0789158, and a = 2.7675059 AU.
  • Solved Eccentric Anomaly & Perifocal Geometry: Solving E0 − 0.0789158 sin E0 = 4.0411310 rad converges in 4 Newton–Raphson steps to E0 = 3.9783704 rad = 227.94383° (note E0 − M0 = −3.59592° because Ceres is in the aphelion-to-perihelion half-orbit 180° < M0 < 360°). Evaluating Eq. (2) gives heliocentric distance r(t0) = 2.7675059 (1 − 0.0789158 cos 227.94383°) = 2.91367 AU and perifocal coordinates (xp, yp) = (−2.07213, −2.04789) AU.

Example 2: (2) Pallas — Moderate Eccentricity (E0 = 152.71189°)

Excerpt from MPCORB.DAT (columns 1–103):

Evaluating Eq. (2) gives r(t0) = 2.7702891 (1 − 0.2305124 cos 152.71189°) = 3.33781 AU and perifocal coordinates (xp, yp) = (−3.10035, +1.23632) AU.
