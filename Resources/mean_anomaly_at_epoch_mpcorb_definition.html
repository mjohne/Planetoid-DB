Mean Anomaly at the Epoch (M0)

Keplerian Phase Parameter, Equation of the Center & Time Propagation in MPCORB.DAT

1. Formal Definition

In celestial mechanics, two-body astrodynamics, and the Minor Planet Center Orbit Database (MPCORB), the mean anomaly at the epoch (M0 or M(t0)) is the angular orbital element that specifies the instantaneous orbital phase of a minor body along its osculating Keplerian ellipse at the reference osculation epoch t0 (expressed in Terrestrial Time, TT). Formally, M0 is defined as the fraction of the osculating orbital period P that has elapsed since the body's most recent passage through perihelion (Tp), scaled by 2π radians (or 360°):

M0 ≡ M(t0) = n · (t0 − Tp) = (2π / P) · (t0 − Tp) &pmod{2π} (Eq. 1)

Geometrically, M0 is not a direct physical angle measured at the dynamical focus (the Sun) or at the center of the ellipse; rather, it is the uniform phase angle of a fictitious body moving with constant angular speed—the mean daily motion n = √(μ / a3)—on an auxiliary reference circle concentric with the orbit, synchronized to coincide with the actual minor planet at perihelion (M = 0°) and aphelion (M = 180°). By Kepler's second law (conservation of specific angular momentum h = ||r × v||), M0 is directly proportional to the swept orbital sector area A(t0) normalized by the total ellipse area π a b:

M0 = 2π · [ A(t0) / (π a b) ]  [rad] (Eq. 2)
Figure 1: Geometric and dynamical relationship between the mean anomaly at the epoch (M0), the eccentric anomaly (E0), and the true anomaly (ν0). While E0 is subtended at the ellipse center C and ν0 at the solar focus F, M0 measures the normalized swept area 2π A(t0) / (π a b) and advances linearly with time at rate n.

2. Mathematical Coupling: Kepler's Equation & Series Expansions

To transform the catalog value M0 into the geometric position (r, ν) in the orbital plane at an arbitrary target epoch t (in TT), M0 is first propagated linearly via the osculating mean daily motion n (tabulated in MPCORB.DAT in degrees per day):

M(t) = M0 + n · (t − t0) &pmod{360°} (Eq. 3)

Next, the transcendental Kepler's equation relates M (in radians) to the eccentric anomaly E and orbital eccentricity e:

M = E − e · sin(E) (Eq. 4)

Because Eq. 4 has no closed-form algebraic inverse in elementary functions, it is solved numerically via Newton–Raphson iteration (Ek+1 = Ek − (Ek − e sin Ek − M) / (1 − e cos Ek), converging to machine precision in 3–5 iterations for e < 0.9) or expanded for small eccentricity via the Fourier–Bessel series (equation of the center):

ν − M = (2e − ¼e3) sin(M) + &frac54;e2 sin(2M) + ⅓/½ · ⅙ e3 sin(3M) + &mathcal;(&e;4) (Eq. 5)

Once E is obtained, the true anomaly ν and heliocentric distance r follow identically from:

ν = 2 atan2 &left;( √(1 + e) sin(E/2), √(1 − e) cos(E/2) &right;), &quad; r = a (1 − e cos E) (Eq. 6)

3. Representation & Precision in MPCORB.DAT

In the fixed-width ASCII record format of MPCORB.DAT, the mean anomaly at the epoch is stored immediately following the packed 5-character osculation epoch t0:

MPCORB Field Character Columns & Format Units, Domain & Catalog Conventions
Epoch (t0) Columns 21–25 (1-based), width 5 (A5) MPC packed date format in Terrestrial Time (TT), e.g., K25BL = 2025 Nov. 21.0 TT (JD 2461000.5). Standard MPCORB releases align t0 to a common 200-day grid (JD ending in .5).
Mean Anomaly at Epoch (M0) Columns 27–35 (1-based), width 9 (F9.5) Degrees in the half-open interval [0.00000°, 360.00000°); resolution 10−5 deg = 0.036 arcsec.
Mean Daily Motion (n) Columns 81–91 (1-based), width 11 (F11.8) Degrees per ephemeris day (deg/d), related to semimajor axis a (AU) via n = kdeg / a3/2 with Gaussian gravitational constant k = 0.01720209895 rad/d = 0.9856076686 deg/d.
Time of Perihelion (Tp) Derived (tabulated directly for comets) Nearest perihelion passage epoch: Tp = t0 − M0 / n (for M0 < 180°) or Tp = t0 + (360° − M0) / n (for M0 > 180°).

4. Physical & Astrodynamical Properties

  • Singularity at Zero Eccentricity (e → 0): Because perihelion is geometrically undefined for a circular orbit (e = 0), the argument of perihelion ω and the mean anomaly M0 become individually ill-conditioned as e → 0, whereas their sum—the mean argument of latitude uM = ω + M0 (or the mean longitude L0 = Ω + ω + M0)—remains smooth and well-defined.
  • Osculating Nature & Perturbations: Because MPCORB.DAT tabulates osculating two-body elements at epoch t0, planetary perturbations cause M0, ω, and n to drift slowly over time. Two-body propagation using Eq. 3 is accurate to a few arcseconds over |t − t0| ≲ 100 days for main-belt asteroids, whereas multi-year ephemerides or close planetary encounters require numerical N-body integration.
  • Parabolic & Hyperbolic Orbits (e ≥ 1): For parabolic (e = 1) or hyperbolic (e > 1) trajectories, the orbital period P is infinite, so standard elliptic M0 ∈ [0°, 360°) is not used; instead, MPC comet and interstellar records tabulate the perihelion passage time Tp and perihelion distance q directly.
