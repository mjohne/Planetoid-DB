1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Geometric Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In two-body Keplerian celestial mechanics, the semi-latus rectum factor (also termed focal parameter factor or squared angular-momentum factor, denoted fp(e) = 1 − e2) is the dimensionless quadratic function of orbital eccentricity e that scales the semi-major axis a to the semi-latus rectum p = a(1 − e2) and scales the circular specific angular momentum squared hc2 = μ⊙a to the actual specific orbital angular momentum squared h2 = μ⊙p.

In polar coordinates centered at the Sun (r(ν) = p / (1 + e cos ν)), the semi-latus rectum p is the heliocentric distance at true anomaly ν = ±90° (half the focal chord perpendicular to the apsidal line). For elliptic orbits (0 ≤ e < 1), p factors cleanly as:

fp(e) ≡ pa = 1 − e2 = (1 − e)(1 + e) = q · Qa2 = b2a2 = h2μ⊙a

Key Algebraic & Dynamical Relations

  • Harmonic Mean of Apsidal Distances: Because p = qQ / a = 2qQ / (q + Q), the semi-latus rectum is the harmonic mean of perihelion and aphelion distances, and (1 − e2) is the harmonic mean of (1 − e) and (1 + e).
  • Radius of Curvature at the Apsides: In differential geometry of an ellipse, p = b2/a = a(1 − e2) equals the exact osculating radius of curvature at both perihelion (ν = 0°) and aphelion (ν = 180°).
  • Square of the Angular Momentum Factor: fp(e) = [fh(e)]2 = [√(1 − e2)]2, directly governing the areal velocity squared (dA/dt)2 = ¼h2 = ¼μ⊙a(1 − e2).
  • Hyperbolic Sign Inversion (e > 1, a < 0): For hyperbolic trajectories, (1 − e2) < 0 while a < 0, so their product p = a(1 − e2) = |a|(e2 − 1) > 0 remains strictly positive.

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

  • Direct Evaluation from Eccentricity e: Computed directly from columns 71–79 (F9.7) of MPCORB.DAT via (1.0 − e) * (1.0 + e), which avoids subtractive cancellation near e → 1 compared to 1.0 − e * e.
  • Core Role in Polar Ephemeris & Orbital Integrators: Every polar position evaluation r(ν) = a(1 − e2) / (1 + e cos ν) and state-vector transformation from Keplerian elements (a, e, i, Ω, ω, M) to heliocentric Cartesian (r, v) requires p and h via (1 − e2).
  • Tisserand Invariant & Resonance Diagnostics: Together with a and inclination i (columns 60–68), √(1 − e2) forms the vertical angular momentum component Hz = √(μ⊙a(1 − e2)) cos i conserved in the Kozai–Lidov mechanism and entering the Tisserand parameter TJ.
