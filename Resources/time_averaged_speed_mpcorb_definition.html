1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Kinematic Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In two-body Keplerian celestial mechanics, time-averaged speed (also termed temporal mean orbital speed, mean elliptic speed, or period-averaged scalar velocity, denoted ⟨v⟩t or vavg) is the exact arithmetic temporal average of the instantaneous heliocentric orbital speed v(t) = ‖ṙ(t)‖ of a minor planet over one complete orbital period P along its osculating Keplerian ellipse (0 ≤ e < 1), expressed in km/s or AU/day (1 AU/day = 1731.4568368 km/s).

Because the scalar speed v(t) = ds / dt is the time derivative of arc length s along the trajectory, integrating v(t) over one closed orbital revolution of period P yields the total geometric perimeter Lellipse of the orbital ellipse divided by P:

⟨v⟩t ≡ 1 P ∫0P v(t) dt = 1 P ∮ ds = Lellipse P = 4 a E(e) P

where a is the osculating semi-major axis, e is the orbital eccentricity, Lellipse = 4a E(e) is the exact circumference of an ellipse of semi-major axis a and eccentricity e, and E(e) is the complete elliptic integral of the second kind (with elliptic modulus e):

Factorization in Terms of Mean Circular Velocity (vc)

Substituting Kepler’s third law P = 2πa / vc—where vc = √(μ⊙ / a) = k a−1/2 (AU/day) is the energy-equivalent mean circular velocity—separates ⟨v⟩t cleanly into a semi-major-axis scale factor vc and a dimensionless eccentricity attenuation factor Φ(e) = 2E(e)/π:

⟨v⟩t = vc · 2 E(e) π = √ μ⊙ a · 2 E(e) π = vc ( 1 − 14e2 − 364e4 − 5256e6 − 17516384e8 − … )

Key mathematical and physical properties of ⟨v⟩t include:

  • Strict Inequality ⟨v⟩t ≤ vc: Because 2E(e)/π is strictly monotonically decreasing on e ∈ [0, 1) from 1 (at e = 0) to 2/π ≈ 0.63661977 (as e → 1−), a body on an eccentric orbit always has a smaller time-averaged scalar speed than a body on a circular orbit of the same semi-major axis a (and same energy). Physically, by Kepler’s second law (r2ν̇ = h), the body spends disproportionately more time near aphelion (r > a), where its instantaneous speed is lowest.
  • Distinction from Vector Velocity Average (⟨v⟩t = 0) and RMS Speed (√⟨v2⟩t = vc): Over any closed periodic orbit, the time average of the vector velocity vanishes identically (⟨v⟩t = (r(P) − r(0))/P = 0), whereas by the virial theorem (⟨T⟩t = −&frac{1}{2}⟨U⟩t = μ⊙/(2a)), the root-mean-square speed equals vc identically regardless of eccentricity e. By Jensen’s inequality, ⟨v⟩t ≤ √⟨v2⟩t = vc.
  • Eccentric Anomaly Integral Representation: Using Kepler’s differential relation dt = (1 − e cos E) dE / n and v(E) = vc √[(1 + e cos E)/(1 − e cos E)], the time integral simplifies directly to:

3. Specific Status and Numerical Evaluation in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database published by the International Astronomical Union’s Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC):

  • Implicit Derived Quantity (Not Tabulated): MPCORB.DAT does not store ⟨v⟩t directly, because it is completely determined by two tabulated osculating elements: orbital eccentricity e (columns 71–79, format F9.7) and semi-major axis a (columns 93–103, format F11.7, AU)—or equivalently daily mean motion n (columns 81–91, format F11.8, deg/day).
  • Domain Restriction (0 ≤ e < 1 and a > 0): Because the time average is defined over one finite orbital period P, ⟨v⟩t exists exclusively for bound elliptic orbits (0 ≤ e < 1, a > 0). For parabolic (e = 1, P = ∞) and hyperbolic (e > 1, a < 0) trajectories in MPCORB.DAT, the orbital period diverges and the infinite-time limit of scalar speed approaches the asymptotic hyperbolic excess speed v∞ = √(−μ⊙ / a) (with v∞ = 0 for e = 1).
  • High-Precision Numerical Computation of E(e): For low-to-moderate eccentricities (e ≲ 0.3, encompassing the vast majority of main-belt asteroids), the power series converges rapidly, whereas for high-eccentricity Near-Earth Asteroids (NEAs), Damocloids, and Centaurs (e → 1), the series exhibits slow algebraic convergence (O(m−2)). In production astrodynamics code, E(e) is evaluated to IEEE 754 double-precision accuracy (~10−16) across all e ∈ [0, 1) in ≤ 6 iterations via the Arithmetic–Geometric Mean (AGM) algorithm:
    a0 = 1,   b0 = √1 − e2,   c02 = e2  ⟹  E(e) = π 2 a∞ ( 1 − 1 2 ∑n=0∞ 2n cn2 )
  • Physical Applications in Minor-Planet Studies:
    • Mean Heliocentric Path Length & Solar Irradiance Integration: Gives the exact path length traversed per unit time (ds/dt averaged over P) and serves as the baseline scalar speed in secular orbital averaging, micrometeoroid flux models, and Poynting–Robertson / Yarkovsky drift analyses.
    • Population-Wide Kinematic Profiling: Quantifies the systematic slow-down bias of high-eccentricity populations (e.g., Apollo/Amor/Aten NEAs and Jupiter-family comets) relative to their energy-equivalent circular speed vc.
Terminological Caution (⟨v⟩t vs. vc): Do not confuse time-averaged speed (⟨v⟩t = 4a E(e)/P) with mean circular velocity (vc = 2πa/P). Only at e = 0 do the two coincide (E(0) = π/2); for every non-circular bound orbit (0 < e < 1), ⟨v⟩t < vc because the perimeter of an ellipse of semi-major axis a is strictly smaller than 2πa (4a ≤ Lellipse < 2πa).
