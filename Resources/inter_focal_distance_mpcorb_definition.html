Inter-Focal Distance (2c) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Bi-Focal Conic Separation, Vacant-Focus Kinematics, and Evaluation from MPCORB

1. Fundamental Conic-Geometric & Astrodynamical Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In analytical conic geometry and two-body celestial mechanics, the inter-focal distance (also termed the focal separation or focal distance, and in German astrometry Brennpunktabstand)—universally denoted by 2c (or 2ae)—is the Euclidean distance separating the two foci F1 (the primary occupied focus, coinciding with the center of mass of the Sun) and F2 (the secondary, vacant geometric focus) along the line of apsides of a central conic trajectory. It is identically twice the linear eccentricity c.

For any bound elliptic Keplerian orbit (0 ≤ e < 1) characterized by semi-major axis a, semi-minor axis b, numeric eccentricity e, perihelion distance q, and aphelion distance Q, the inter-focal distance 2c is expressed identically by:

|F1F2| = 2c = 2 a e = 2 √ a2 − b2 = Q − q

Whereas the major axis 2a = Q + q represents the sum of the apsidal distances (and the constant sum of focal radii r1 + r2 = 2a at every point on the ellipse), the inter-focal distance 2c = Q − q represents the apsidal span difference—that is, the total radial excursion between perihelion and aphelion:

  • Triangle Inequality & Boundedness (0 ≤ 2c < 2a): In the non-degenerate triangle ▵PF1F2 formed by an orbiting body P and the two foci, the base length |F1F2| = 2c is strictly less than the sum of the two focal radii |PF1| + |PF2| = r1 + r2 = 2a for every bound elliptic orbit (0 ≤ e < 1).
  • Circular Coalescence (e = 0 ⇒ 2c = 0 AU): Both foci coincide at the geometric center O (F1 = F2 = O), yielding zero inter-focal separation and zero radial excursion (Q − q = 0).
  • Near-Parabolic Elongation (e → 1− ⇒ 2c → 2a−): As eccentricity approaches unity, the inter-focal distance approaches the full length of the major axis (2c → 2a), with F1 approaching perihelion and F2 approaching aphelion.
  • Parabolic Divergence (e = 1): In a parabolic trajectory, the secondary focus F2 recedes to spatial infinity along the aphelion ray (2c → +∞), leaving a single finite focus F1.

2. Kinematic & Astrodynamical Significance of the Vacant Focus (F2)

In two-body celestial mechanics, the separation 2c between the Sun (F1) and the vacant focus (F2) governs several classical kinematic and geometric theorems:

2.1 3D Heliocentric Position of the Vacant Focus (F2)

Let P̂ denote the unit vector in the instantaneous osculating orbital plane directed from the Sun (F1) toward perihelion (determined by inclination i, longitude of the ascending node Ω, and argument of perihelion ω). Because F2 lies on the aphelion side of the Sun at distance 2c, its exact 3D heliocentric position vector in J2000.0 mean ecliptic coordinates is:

rF2 = −2c P̂ = −2 a e P̂ = −(Q − q) P̂

2.2 Vacant-Focus Angular Rate Approximation (Equant Property)

By Kepler’s second law, the true anomaly ν measured at the occupied focus F1 varies non-uniformly with time (ν̇ = h/r12). Conversely, the polar angle ν2 measured at the vacant focus F2—separated from F1 by 2c—evolves nearly uniformly in time to first order in eccentricity (ν̇2 = n + O(e2), where n is the mean motion), realizing Ptolemy’s classical equant point at displacement 2c = 2ae from the attracting mass.

2.3 Law of Cosines in the Focal Triangle ▵PF1F2

Applying the law of cosines to ▵PF1F2 with base 2c and side r2 = 2a − r1 recovers the polar conic equation directly from bi-focal geometry:

(2a − r1)2 = r12 + (2c)2 + 2 r1 (2c) cos ν   ⟹   r1(ν) = a2 − c2 a + c cos ν = a(1 − e2) 1 + e cos ν

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database published by the International Astronomical Union’s Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC):

  • Implicit Derived Quantity (Not Tabulated): The inter-focal distance 2c is not stored as an explicit fixed-width column in MPCORB.DAT. Instead, it is computed directly from the osculating numeric eccentricity e (columns 71–79, format F9.7) and osculating semi-major axis a (columns 93–103, format F11.7) via 2c = 2 · a · e.
  • Guaranteed Finite & Non-Negative Across All MPCORB.DAT Entries: Because MPCORB.DAT catalogs strictly bound elliptic minor planet orbits (0 ≤ e < 1, a > 0), the inter-focal distance satisfies 0 ≤ 2c < 2a for every record in the catalog without singularities (vanishing cleanly to 0.0000000 AU when e = 0).
  • Osculating Heliocentric Reference & Unit: Derived from MPCORB elements at the record’s packed Epoch (columns 21–25) in the J2000.0 mean ecliptic frame, 2c is an instantaneous osculating heliocentric length expressed in Astronomical Units (AU) (1 AU ≡ 149,597,870,700 m).
  • Physical Interpretation in Solar System Dynamics: For main-belt asteroids with low eccentricity (e.g., e ≈ 0.05, a ≈ 2.7 AU), the inter-focal distance 2c ≈ 0.27 AU lies well inside the orbit of Mercury; conversely, for high-eccentricity Jupiter-family comets, Damocloids, or extreme trans-Neptunian objects (e.g., (90377) Sedna, a ≈ 506 AU, e ≈ 0.85), 2c = Q − q ≈ 860 AU spans almost the entire major axis of the orbit.
Numerical Evaluation Note: Always compute the inter-focal distance via direct multiplication inter_focal = 2.0 * a * e rather than subtracting aphelion and perihelion distances (Q - q) or taking 2.0 * Math.Sqrt(a * a - b * b). For near-circular orbits (e → 0), both Q - q and a * a - b * b lose significant mantissa digits due to catastrophic floating-point cancellation, whereas 2.0 * a * e preserves full IEEE-754 precision.
