Orbital Semi-Perimeter (s = L/2) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Apsidal-to-Apsidal Semi-Elliptic Arc Length, Elliptic Integral Formulation, and Evaluation from MPCORB

1. Fundamental Differential-Geometric & Astrodynamical Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In differential geometry and two-body celestial mechanics, the orbital semi-perimeter (also termed the semi-circumference of the orbit, apsidal-to-apsidal arc length, or in German astrometry halber Bahnumfang)—typically denoted by s (or L/2, U/2)—is exactly half of the closed Euclidean arc length L of an instantaneous osculating Keplerian ellipse, corresponding physically to the Euclidean path length traversed by an orbiting body along either symmetric semi-ellipse connecting perihelion (E = 0, ν = 0°) to aphelion (E = π, ν = 180°), or vice versa. It is a dimensional length quantity expressed in Astronomical Units (AU).

Integrating the Euclidean differential arc-length element ds = a√(1 − e2 cos2 E) dE over the outbound half-revolution from perihelion (E = 0) to aphelion (E = π)—or equivalently over the inbound return branch (E ∈ [π, 2π))—yields the exact formulation of the orbital semi-perimeter s in terms of the complete elliptic integral of the second kind Eell(e):

s = L 2 = ∫0π a √ 1 − e2 cos2 E dE = 2 a ∫0π/2 √ 1 − e2 sin2 φ dφ = 2 a Eell(e)

Geometrically, whereas the major axis 2a = Q + q is the straight-line chord connecting perihelion and aphelion along the line of apsides, the orbital semi-perimeter s = 2a Eell(e) is the curved elliptic arc joining those same two apsidal vertices. Consequently, s satisfies strict geometric inequalities and asymptotic limits:

  • Strict Chord-to-Semicircle Bounds (2a < s ≤ πa): Because a straight line is the shortest Euclidean path between perihelion and aphelion (2a) and a semicircle of radius a maximizes the arc length for a given semi-major axis (πa), s is strictly bounded by 2a < s ≤ πa for all bound elliptic orbits (0 ≤ e < 1).
  • Circular Limit (e = 0 ⇒ s = πa): Since Eell(0) = π/2, the semi-perimeter equals the Euclidean semicircle length s = πa ≈ 3.1415927a.
  • Degenerate Collinear Limit (e → 1− ⇒ s → 2a+): Since Eell(1) = 1, as the ellipse flattens onto the line of apsides (b → 0+), the curved apsidal-to-apsidal arc converges to the straight-line major axis chord s → 2a.
  • Parabolic Divergence (e = 1): Because aphelion recedes to infinity (a → ∞), the apsidal-to-apsidal semi-perimeter is infinite (s = +∞).

2. Kinematic Symmetry, Series Expansions & Quadrature

In unperturbed two-body Keplerian motion, the apsidal line divides the orbit into two geometrically congruent halves of equal arc length s and equal traversal duration P/2, governed by the following mathematical identities:

2.1 Temporal & Kinematic Duality Between Outbound and Inbound Branches

By Kepler’s equation M = E − e sin E, the elapsed time from perihelion (E = 0, M = 0) to aphelion (E = π, M = π) is exactly half the sidereal orbital period (Δt = P/2), and the spatial arc length traversed during that half-period is identically the orbital semi-perimeter s = L/2. Consequently, the time-averaged scalar speed over the outbound branch (perihelion to aphelion), over the inbound branch (aphelion to perihelion), and over the full orbit are identical:

v̄half = s P / 2 = 2 a Eell(e) π a3/2 / √μ = L P = v̄

Note, however, that the ascending-node to descending-node arc length (sΩ→☊, the northern ecliptic excursion) equals the orbital semi-perimeter s if and only if the line of nodes coincides with the line of apsides (ω = 0° or 180°); for arbitrary argument of perihelion ω, the nodal split divides L = 2s asymmetrically unless sin ω = 0.

2.2 Ivory–Bessel Series in the Third Flattening (h)

Using the squared third flattening parameter h = (a − b)2/(a + b)2 = e4 / [1 + √(1 − e2)]4, the orbital semi-perimeter s is expanded directly as:

s = π 2 (a + b) [1 + 14 h + 164 h2 + 1256 h3 + 2516384 h4 + ···]

2.3 Arithmetic-Geometric Mean (AGM) Quadrature

For arbitrary eccentricities across the entire elliptic domain 0 ≤ e < 1 (including high-eccentricity comets and Damocloids), the Arithmetic-Geometric Mean iteration (a0 = 1, b0 = √(1 − e2), c0 = e) yields s to full IEEE-754 double precision in ≤ 6 steps:

s = π a aN [1 − ∑n=0N 2n−1 cn2]

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database published by the International Astronomical Union’s Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC):

  • Implicit Derived Quantity (Not Tabulated): The orbital semi-perimeter s is not stored as an explicit column in MPCORB.DAT. Instead, it is computed directly from the osculating numeric eccentricity e (columns 71–79, format F9.7) and osculating semi-major axis a (columns 93–103, format F11.7) via s = 2a Eell(e) = L / 2.
  • Universal Finiteness & Bounds Across All MPCORB.DAT Entries: Because every record in MPCORB.DAT represents a bound elliptic orbit (0 ≤ e < 1, a > 0), the orbital semi-perimeter s is strictly finite, positive, and bounded by 2a < s ≤ πa across 100% of catalog records.
  • Osculating Heliocentric Reference & Unit: Derived from MPCORB elements at the record’s packed Epoch (columns 21–25) in the J2000.0 mean ecliptic frame, s represents the instantaneous osculating outbound or inbound apsidal path length expressed in Astronomical Units (AU) (1 AU ≡ 149,597,870,700 m).
  • Practical Applications in Minor Planet Analysis:
    • Apsidal Transit Path Length: Quantifies the exact heliocentric distance traveled by a comet or high-eccentricity asteroid during a single inbound leg from aphelion to perihelion (e.g., for solar radiation exposure and volatile sublimation modeling).
    • Geometric Tortuosity / Arc-to-Chord Ratio (s/(2a)): Measures how much longer the curved orbital semi-perimeter s is compared to the straight-line major axis 2a, decreasing monotonically from π/2 ≈ 1.5708 (circular) toward 1.0000 (needle-like comet orbit).
Implementation & Precision Note: When storing or displaying both the orbital perimeter L and the orbital semi-perimeter s in an MPCORB processing pipeline, compute s directly as double s = 2.0 * a * CompleteEllipticE(e); (or s = 0.5 * L). Multiplying by 0.5 in IEEE-754 binary64 arithmetic simply decrements the binary exponent by 1, introducing zero rounding error.
