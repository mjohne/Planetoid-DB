Synodic Period (Psyn) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Mean Relative Angular Rate, Opposition Cadence, Retrograde & Co-Orbital Regimes, and Evaluation from MPCORB

1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Observational Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In observational astronomy and two-body celestial mechanics, the synodic period (also termed the mean synodic revolution period, conjunction/opposition recurrence period, or in German astrometry synodische Umlaufzeit)—denoted by Psyn or S—is the mean time interval required for a minor planet to return to the same heliocentric ecliptic elongation (such as opposition at 180° or superior conjunction at 0°) relative to the Sun as observed from a reference observer (by default, Earth). It is a dimensional time quantity expressed in days (d) or Julian years (yr, 365.25 d).

In the coplanar circular mean-motion approximation, the synodic period Psyn is governed by the absolute difference between the sidereal mean daily motion of Earth (n⊕ = 360° / P⊕) and the osculating sidereal mean daily motion of the minor planet (n = 360° / P, or −n for retrograde orbits with inclination i > 90°):

1 Psyn = |n⊕ − neff| 360° = | 1 P⊕ − σ P |   ⇒   Psyn = P · P⊕ |P − σP⊕| = 360° |n⊕ − σn|

where P⊕ = 365.256363004 d (n⊕ = 0.98560911303°/d) is Earth’s sidereal orbital period (corresponding to a⊕ = 1.000001018 AU), and σ ∈ {+1, −1} encodes orbital sense (σ = +1 for prograde orbits with 0° ≤ i < 90°; σ = −1 for retrograde orbits with 90° < i ≤ 180°). Key dynamical regimes include:

  • Superior Prograde Orbits (a > 1 AU, P > P⊕, σ = +1): Earth overtakes the minor planet once per synodic cycle; Psyn = (P · P⊕) / (P − P⊕) > P⊕. For Main-Belt asteroids (P ≈ 3–6 yr), Psyn ≈ 1.2–1.5 yr; for distant TNOs (P → ∞), Psyn → P⊕+ (≈ 365.26 d).
  • Inferior Prograde Orbits (a < 1 AU, P < P⊕, σ = +1): Atira and Aten asteroids overtake Earth; Psyn = (P · P⊕) / (P⊕ − P), approaching P as a → 0 and diverging as a → 1−.
  • 1:1 Co-Orbital Singularity (a = a⊕, P = P⊕, σ = +1): For quasi-satellites, horseshoe, and Earth Trojan orbits (n → n⊕), the mean synodic period diverges (Psyn → +∞), corresponding to libration rather than circulation of the synodic angle.
  • Retrograde Orbits (90° < i ≤ 180°, σ = −1): Because the minor planet moves opposite to Earth’s revolution, angular rates add (nrel = n⊕ + n), yielding Psyn,retro = (P · P⊕) / (P + P⊕) < P⊕ (strictly less than 1 sidereal year, with no co-orbital singularity at a = 1 AU).

2. Eccentricity Modulation & True vs. Mean Opposition Intervals

Because real minor planet orbits in MPCORB.DAT have non-zero eccentricity (e > 0) and inclination (i > 0), the analytical formula Psyn = (P · P⊕) / |P − σP⊕| defines the mean synodic period averaged over a full commensurability cycle:

2.1 Variations Between Successive Oppositions (Δtopp)

By Kepler’s second law, a minor planet moves faster near perihelion (ωtrue = √(μp)/q2) and slower near aphelion (ωtrue = √(μp)/Q2). Consequently, the actual elapsed time Δtopp between two consecutive oppositions fluctuates around the mean Psyn:

  • Perihelic Oppositions: When opposition occurs near the minor planet’s perihelion, its instantaneous angular velocity exceeds its mean motion n, lengthening the interval until the next opposition (Δtopp > Psyn for prograde superior bodies).
  • Aphelic Oppositions: When opposition occurs near aphelion, the minor planet moves more slowly in ecliptic longitude, shortening the interval to the subsequent opposition (Δtopp < Psyn).

2.2 Synodic Period Relative to Arbitrary Observer Planets

Replacing Earth’s sidereal period P⊕ with that of another planet Pobs yields the mutual synodic encounter cadence (useful for assessing Jupiter or Mars encounter frequencies):

Psyn,obs = P · Pobs |P − σPobs|

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database published by the International Astronomical Union’s Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC):

  • Implicit Derived Quantity (Not Tabulated): The synodic period Psyn is not stored as an explicit column in MPCORB.DAT. Instead, it is computed directly from the osculating mean daily motion n (columns 81–91, format F11.8) or semi-major axis a (columns 93–103, format F11.7), optionally incorporating the osculating inclination i (columns 60–68, format F9.5) to distinguish prograde (i ≤ 90°) from retrograde (i > 90°) orbits.
  • Co-Orbital Singularity Handling (|n − n⊕| → 0): Unlike the sidereal period P (which is strictly finite for all MPCORB.DAT entries), Psyn has a pole at a ≈ 1.000001 AU (P = P⊕, i < 90°). For Earth co-orbitals such as (3753) Cruithne (a ≈ 0.9977 AU) or (469219) Kamo‘oalewa (a ≈ 1.0012 AU), Psyn spans hundreds of years and should be guarded against division-by-zero (|P − P⊕| < ε ⇒ +∞).
  • Observational Cadence Application in MPCORB Pipelines: Used in survey planning and recovery scheduling to determine how frequently an asteroid reaches opposition geometry (minimum geocentric distance and maximum solar elongation) for astrometric follow-up.
Implementation & Sign Convention Note: In standard scalar MPCORB tables where synodic period is defined strictly as a positive recurrence interval without retrograde sign reversal (σ = +1), use P_syn = (P * P_earth) / Math.Abs(P - P_earth). If distinguishing retrograde orbital motion (i > 90°, such as Centaurs/Damocloids like (20461) Dioretsa or (514107) Ka‘epaoka‘awela), apply σ = (i > 90°) ? −1 : +1 so the relative ecliptic angular rate |n⊕ − σn| accurately reflects the physical opposition cadence.
