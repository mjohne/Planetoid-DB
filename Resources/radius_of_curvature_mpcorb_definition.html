Orbital Radius of Curvature (ρ) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Differential-Geometric Osculating Circle, Normal Acceleration Coupling, and Evaluation from MPCORB

1. Fundamental Differential-Geometric & Astrodynamical Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In differential geometry and two-body celestial mechanics, the orbital radius of curvature (also termed the instantaneous radius of curvature of the osculating trajectory or in German astrometry Krümmungsradius der Bahn)—typically denoted by ρ or Rc—is the reciprocal of the planar curvature κ = 1/ρ of the instantaneous osculating Keplerian ellipse at a given orbital phase (E or ν). Geometrically, ρ is the radius of the unique osculating circle sharing second-order tangency with the trajectory at the body’s instantaneous position. It is a dimensional length quantity expressed in Astronomical Units (AU).

In perifocal Cartesian coordinates xω(E) = a(cos E − e), yω(E) = b sin E (with b = a√(1 − e2)), evaluating the planar curvature formula ρ = (ẋω2 + ẏω2)3/2 / |ẋωÿω − ẏωẍω|—or equivalently using polar conic geometry with semi-latus rectum p = a(1 − e2) and flight-path angle γ—yields the exact closed-form representations of ρ:

ρ(E) = a (1 − e2 cos2 E)3/2 √(1 − e2) = p cos3 γ = p [ 1 + 2e cos ν + e2 (1 + e cos ν)2 ]3/2

where γ is the instantaneous flight-path angle (elevation angle between the velocity vector v and the local transverse horizontal, satisfying tan γ = (e sin ν)/(1 + e cos ν)). Key geometric properties and extremal bounds include:

  • Strict Global Bounds (p = b2/a ≤ ρ ≤ a2/b): Along any bound ellipse (0 ≤ e < 1), ρ oscillates smoothly twice per revolution between its apsidal minima and co-vertex maxima.
  • Apsidal Minimum at Perihelion & Aphelion (E = 0, π ⇒ ρmin = p = a(1 − e2)): Because γ = 0° at both apsides, the radius of curvature at both perihelion and aphelion equals the semi-latus rectum p = b2/a (the sharpest turning radius of the ellipse).
  • Co-Vertex Maximum (E = ±π/2 ⇒ ρmax = a2/b = a / √(1 − e2)): At the minor-axis vertices (r = a), the ellipse is flattest, attaining maximum radius of curvature ρmax = a2/b ≥ a.
  • Circular Invariance (e = 0 ⇒ ρ ≡ a): For a circular orbit, p = b = a and γ ≡ 0°, so the radius of curvature is constant everywhere and identical to the orbital radius a.

2. Kinematical & Dynamical Coupling in Two-Body Motion

In Newtonian orbital mechanics, the radius of curvature ρ directly governs the centripetal (normal) acceleration and instantaneous turning rate of a minor planet along its trajectory:

2.1 Normal Acceleration (an) & Angular Velocity of the Tangent Vector

Decomposing the heliocentric gravitational acceleration a = −(μ/r2) r̂ into tangential (T̂) and inward principal normal (N̂) Frenet–Serret components relates ρ directly to the orbital speed v = √(μ(2/r − 1/a)) and flight-path angle γ:

an = v2 ρ = μ r2 cos γ   ⇒   ρ = r2 v2 μ cos γ = (r v)3 μ h

where h = r v cos γ = √(μp) is the constant specific orbital angular momentum. Because ρ = v / |ψ̇|, ρ also determines the instantaneous rate ψ̇ = v / ρ at which the heliocentric velocity vector rotates in the orbital plane.

2.2 Circumference-Averaged Radius of Curvature (⟨ρ⟩arc)

Integrating ρ with respect to the tangent direction angle ψ around one full closed revolution (0 ≤ ψ < 2π) yields the total orbital perimeter L = ∫02π ρ(ψ) dψ = 4a Eell(e), so the tangent-angle-averaged radius of curvature is L/(2π).

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database published by the International Astronomical Union’s Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC):

  • Implicit Derived Quantity (Not Tabulated): The orbital radius of curvature ρ is not stored as an explicit column in MPCORB.DAT. Instead, its static extremal values (ρmin = p and ρmax = a2/b) are computed solely from the osculating numeric eccentricity e (columns 71–79, format F9.7) and semi-major axis a (columns 93–103, format F11.7), while the instantaneous epoch value ρ(t0) additionally requires solving Kepler’s equation M0 = E0 − e sin E0 from the tabulated mean anomaly M0 (columns 27–35, format F9.5).
  • Universal Finiteness & Positivity Across All MPCORB.DAT Entries: Because every record in MPCORB.DAT represents a bound elliptic orbit (0 ≤ e < 1, a > 0), ρ(E) is strictly positive and finite everywhere along every cataloged orbit, bounded within a(1 − e2) ≤ ρ(E) ≤ a / √(1 − e2).
  • Practical Applications in Astrodynamics & Orbit Determination:
    • Numerical Integrator Step-Size Control: Local truncation error in RK4/RKF45 orbital propagators scales with trajectory curvature κ = 1/ρ, peaking at perihelion where ρ = ρmin = p.
    • Short-Arc & Vaisala / Gauss Orbit Determination: Distinguishing nearly rectilinear short-arc trajectories (ρ ≫ r, near co-vertices of high-e orbits) from strongly curved apsidal passages (ρ = p).
Numerical Evaluation Note: When computing ρ(E) in double precision for high-eccentricity objects (e → 1−) near perihelion (cos E ≈ 1), rewrite 1 − e2 cos2 E = (1 − e2) + e2 sin2 E using the factored identity 1 − e2 = (1 − e)(1 + e) to prevent catastrophic cancellation when e > 0.9999.

The table below summarizes the fixed-width columns (1-based indexing) in a standard 202-character MPCORB.DAT record required to compute the instantaneous and extremal radii of curvature:
