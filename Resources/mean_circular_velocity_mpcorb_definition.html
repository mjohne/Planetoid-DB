Mean Circular Velocity (vc) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Energy-Equivalent Reference Circular Speed, Relation to Time-Averaged Speed & Apsidal Extrema, and Derivation from MPCORB

1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Energetic Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In two-body Keplerian celestial mechanics, mean circular velocity (also termed characteristic circular orbital speed, reference circular speed at semi-major axis a, or energy-equivalent circular speed, denoted vc or v0) is the constant inertial heliocentric orbital speed of a fictitious body moving on a circular orbit (e = 0) of radius r = a equal to the minor planet’s osculating semi-major axis, which possesses the exact same specific mechanical orbital energy ε = −μ⊙ / (2a) and orbital period P as the actual elliptic orbit (0 ≤ e < 1), expressed in km/s or AU/day (1 AU/day = 1731.4568368 km/s).

Setting r(t) = a in the vis-viva equation v2 = μ⊙(2/r − 1/a)—or equivalently equating centripetal acceleration vc2/a to heliocentric gravitational acceleration μ⊙/a2—defines vc purely as an invariant function of the osculating semi-major axis a (or daily mean motion n):

vc = √ μ⊙ a = 2πa P = nrad a = k a−1/2  (AU/day)

where μ⊙ = G(M⊙ + m) ≈ GM⊙ = 1.32712440041 × 1011 km3 s−2 = k2 AU3 day−2, with the Gaussian gravitational constant k = 0.01720209895 AU3/2 day−1, 1 AU = 149,597,870.700 km, and 1 day = 86,400 s. Evaluating the numerical prefactor yields the standard practical scaling laws:

vc = 29.78469183 √aAU  km/s   =   0.01720209895 √aAU  AU/day

Key mathematical and astrodynamical identities connecting vc to elliptic orbital kinematics include:

  • Geometric Mean of Perihelion and Aphelion Speeds: On any bound elliptic orbit (0 ≤ e < 1), vc equals the exact geometric mean of the maximum (perihelion, vq = vc√[(1 + e)/(1 − e)]) and minimum (aphelion, vQ = vc√[(1 − e)/(1 + e)]) orbital speeds:
    vc = √(vq · vQ) = √(vmax · vmin)
  • Instantaneous Attainment at the Minor-Axis Vertices (r = a): By the vis-viva equation, the actual instantaneous orbital speed v(t) equals vc at precisely the two points on the ellipse where the heliocentric radius equals the semi-major axis (r = a), corresponding to eccentric anomaly E = 90° (outbound minor-axis vertex, M = 90° − edeg) and E = 270° (inbound minor-axis vertex, M = 270° + edeg).
  • Relation to True Time-Averaged Orbital Speed (⟨v⟩t): Because a body moves more slowly near aphelion and spends more time in the outer half of its orbit (r > a), the time-averaged scalar speed over one orbital period P is strictly less than vc for all non-circular orbits (0 < e < 1) and is given by the complete elliptic integral of the second kind E(e):
    ⟨v⟩t = 1 P ∫0P v(t) dt = Lellipse P = vc · 2 E(e) π ≈ vc ( 1 − 1 4 e2 − 3 64 e4 − … ) ≤ vc
    By contrast, the root-mean-square (RMS) speed over an orbital period equals vc identically: √⟨v2⟩t = vc (virial theorem).

2. Contrast Among Characteristic Orbital Velocity Metrics

In astrodynamical catalog software built on MPCORB.DAT, mean circular velocity (vc) serves as the epoch-independent baseline speed set solely by the semi-major axis a, distinct from instantaneous speed, apsidal extrema, and time-averaged elliptic speed:

Mean Circular Velocity (vc) √(μ⊙ / a) = 2πa / P a (or n) only Speed on energy-equivalent circular orbit (r = a); equals √(vq vQ) and RMS speed √⟨v2⟩t
Time-Averaged Speed (⟨v⟩t) vc · [2 E(e) / π] a, e Exact arithmetic temporal mean Lellipse / P over one period (⟨v⟩t ≤ vc)
Current Orbital Velocity (v(t)) √[μ⊙ (2/r(t) − 1/a)] a, e, M(t) Instantaneous heliocentric speed at evaluation epoch t (vQ ≤ v(t) ≤ vq)

Aphelion Velocity (vQ / vmin) vc √[(1 − e) / (1 + e)] a, e Minimum speed attained at aphelion (r = Q)

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database published by the International Astronomical Union’s Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC):

  • Implicit Derived Invariant (Not Tabulated): MPCORB.DAT does not store vc explicitly, because vc is uniquely determined by the osculating semi-major axis a (columns 93–103, format F11.7, AU) or equivalently by the daily mean motion n (columns 81–91, format F11.8, deg/day):
    vc = √ μ⊙ a = (μ⊙ nrad)1/3 = k2/3 ( π 180 ndeg/d )1/3  (AU/day)
  • Domain Restriction (a > 0 / 0 ≤ e < 1): Real-valued vc requires a positive semi-major axis a > 0 (bound elliptic orbit 0 ≤ e < 1). For hyperbolic orbits (e > 1, a < 0), the characteristic velocity scale is instead the hyperbolic excess speed v∞ = √(−μ⊙ / a).
  • Physical & Statistical Applications in Minor-Planet Dynamics:
    • Dynamical Family & Tisserand Parameter Normalization: In asteroid family identification (Hierarchical Clustering Method, HCM) and comet/asteroid classification, relative velocity perturbations δv between family members scale directly in units of vc via Gauss’s planetary equations (δa / a ≈ 2 δvT / vc).
    • Collisional Energy & Disruption Scaling: Mean impact velocities among main-belt asteroids (a ≈ 2.1–3.3 AU, vc ≈ 16.4–20.6 km/s) are proportional to vc √(e2 + sin2 i), governing catastrophic disruption thresholds (Q*D).
Terminological Caution (vc vs. ⟨v⟩t): In orbital mechanics, “mean circular velocity” (vc = 2πa/P) refers to the speed along the reference circle of radius a—not the time-averaged speed ⟨v⟩t along the ellipse. Because Kepler’s second law causes an eccentric body to linger near aphelion, ⟨v⟩t / vc = 2E(e)/π < 1 whenever e > 0 (for example, at e = 0.5, ⟨v⟩t = 0.9342 vc).
