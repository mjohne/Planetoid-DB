“Semi-Mean Axis” (ā / ap) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Terminological Clarification, Perturbation-Averaged Orbital Scale, and Relationship to Osculating MPCORB Elements

1. Terminological Clarification in Celestial Mechanics

Nomenclature Note: In formal IAU astrometry and celestial mechanics, “semi-mean axis” is a non-standard portmanteau blending semi-major axis (a) and mean orbital element (ā or ap). Depending on the analytical context, the term refers either to the mean semi-major axis (ā, obtained by averaging short-period planetary perturbations over time) or to the proper semi-major axis (ap, an invariant quasi-integral of motion in synthetic/analytical perturbation theory), and occasionally in geometric contexts to the arithmetic mean of the semi-axes (a + b)/2.

In the context of small Solar System body dynamics and the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database, the scientifically rigorous concept underlying “semi-mean axis” is the mean semi-major axis (ā) and its long-term dynamical counterpart, the proper semi-major axis (ap). Unlike the osculating semi-major axis (a) tabulated directly in MPCORB.DAT, which reflects an instantaneous two-body Keplerian tangent ellipse at a single epoch, the mean semi-major axis filters out short-period gravitational oscillations induced by the major planets (predominantly Jupiter and Saturn) as well as the solar barycentric reflex wobble.

2. Mathematical & Astrodynamical Formulation

Under realistic N-body gravitational dynamics in the Solar System, the instantaneous osculating semi-major axis a(t) of a minor planet evolves according to Lagrange's planetary equations (or Gauss's variational equations):

da dt = 2 n a ∂ℛ ∂λ

where n is the mean daily motion, ℛ is the planetary disturbing function, and λ = M + ω + Ω is the mean longitude of the minor planet. Because ℛ contains periodic terms depending on the fast orbital angles (λ and the mean longitudes λj of the perturbing planets), a(t) undergoes continuous high-frequency fluctuations around a nearly constant baseline.

2.1 Time-Averaged (Mean) Semi-Major Axis (ā)

The mean semi-major axis ā is defined by eliminating all short-period terms (of order 𝒪(mj/M☉)) via canonical perturbation transformations (such as Von Zeipel or Lie–Hori averaging) or via numerical digital filtering over a window T spanning multiple orbital and synodic periods:

ā = 1 T ∫t0 − T/2t0 + T/2 aosc(t) dt = aosc(t) − Δashort-period(t)

By Poisson's theorem on the invariability of semi-major axes, ā contains no secular terms at first or second order in planetary masses (dā/dt = 0 away from mean-motion resonances), making it vastly more stable over 104–106 years than the osculating value stored in MPCORB.

2.2 Geometric Mean of Semi-Axes (Secondary Interpretation)

In pure ellipse geometry, when comparing the semi-major axis (a) and semi-minor axis (b = a√(1 − e2)), the arithmetic average of the two semi-axes is occasionally referred to informally as the mean semi-axis:

ageom = a + b 2 = a 2 &Bigl(1 + √(1 − e2)&Bigr)

While ageom appears in Ramanujan's approximations for the orbital perimeter C, it is not an orbital element in MPCORB and should be strictly distinguished from the dynamically averaged mean semi-major axis ā.

3. Relationship to the MPCORB Database

The MPCORB catalog distributed by the IAU Minor Planet Center provides osculating heliocentric elements at a single common standard epoch, rather than mean or proper elements. Understanding the distinction between MPCORB's osculating a and the mean/proper ā (ap) is critical when processing MPCORB.DAT:

  • Osculating Values in MPCORB.DAT (Columns 93–103): Every record in MPCORB.DAT tabulates the instantaneous osculating heliocentric semi-major axis aosc in Astronomical Units (AU, format F11.7) at the record's packed Epoch (columns 21–25). Neither ā nor ap is stored in MPCORB.DAT.
  • Heliocentric vs. Barycentric Reflex Oscillation: Because MPCORB elements are referenced to the heliocenter rather than the Solar System barycenter, the osculating semi-major axis of outer Solar System bodies (Centaurs and Trans-Neptunian Objects with a > 30 AU) exhibits large artificial variations of Δa ≈ ±0.3–1.5 AU over a single 11.86-year Jovian revolution simply due to the Sun's reflex motion around the barycenter. Transforming MPCORB state vectors to the barycentric frame and averaging yields ā, revealing the true dynamical family or resonant trapping.
  • Mean-Motion Resonance & Asteroid Family Identification: Because short-period perturbations smear osculating MPCORB values of a by ∼10−3–10−2 AU in the Main Belt, collisional asteroid families (e.g., Vesta, Eunomia, Koronis, Eos, Themis) and narrow Kirkwood resonance boundaries cannot be sharply resolved from raw MPCORB columns alone; they require numerical integration of MPCORB initial conditions to compute synthetic proper elements (ap, ep, sin ip), as cataloged in the AstDyS database.
Practical Workflow for MPCORB Users: Never treat the raw semi-major axis in columns 93–103 of MPCORB.DAT as a long-term invariant (ā). For precision ephemeris propagation, use the exact osculating elements at the stated Epoch; for dynamical taxonomy (e.g., TNO resonant classification or Main-Belt family clustering), first convert MPCORB osculating elements to state vectors, transform to the Solar System barycenter, and integrate forward/backward to extract the time-averaged mean semi-major axis ā.
