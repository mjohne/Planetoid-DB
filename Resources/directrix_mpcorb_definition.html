Directrix of a Keplerian Orbit in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Focus–Directrix Locus Geometry, and Spatial Determination from the IAU Minor Planet Center Orbit Catalog

1. Fundamental Conic-Geometric & Astrodynamical Definition

In analytical conic geometry and two-body celestial mechanics, the directrix (plural: directrices) of a Keplerian trajectory is a fixed reference straight line lying in the instantaneous osculating orbital plane, oriented strictly perpendicular to the line of apsides (the major axis) on the perihelion side of the primary focus (occupied by the Sun), such that for every instantaneous position P of the orbiting body, the ratio of its heliocentric distance r = |PF1| to its perpendicular Euclidean distance ddir from the directrix is constant and identically equal to the orbital eccentricity e:

r ddir = e   ⟺   r = e · ddir

For any non-circular elliptic orbit (0 < e < 1) with semi-major axis a and semi-latus rectum p = a(1 − e2), the ellipse possesses two symmetric directrices orthogonal to the major axis at distances ±a/e from the geometric center of the ellipse. In heliocentric orbital mechanics, the dynamically relevant primary directrix is the one paired with the occupied focus F1 (the Sun), located beyond perihelion at the following canonical distances:

xdir, center = a e  ,    Ddir, Sun = a e − a e = a(1 − e2) e = p e  ,    ddir, peri = q e = a(1 − e) e

where xdir, center is the distance from the ellipse center to the directrix, Ddir, Sun is the perpendicular distance from the Sun (F1) to the directrix, and ddir, peri is the clearance between the perihelion vertex and the directrix. Notice the ordering along the line of apsides from the center outward toward perihelion (0 < e < 1):

a e (Sun) < a (Perihelion) < a e (Primary Directrix)

As the orbit approaches a circle (e → 0+), both xdir, center and Ddir, Sun recede to spatial infinity (→ +∞); thus, a strictly circular orbit (e = 0) has no finite directrix in the Euclidean plane (its directrix lies on the line at infinity of the projective plane). Conversely, at the parabolic escape limit (e = 1), Ddir, Sun = p = 2q and ddir, peri = q, placing the perihelion vertex exactly midway between the Sun and the directrix.

2. Polar Conic Derivation & 3D Heliocentric Ecliptic Representation

The focus–directrix property r = e · ddir is the foundational geometric axiom from which Kepler’s polar trajectory equation is derived directly.

2.1 Derivation of the Polar Trajectory Equation

Let ν denote the true anomaly measured at the Sun (F1) from the perihelion direction (ν = 0 toward the directrix). Projecting the radius vector r onto the line of apsides gives the perpendicular distance from the orbital point P(r, ν) to the primary directrix:

ddir(ν) = Ddir, Sun − r cos ν = p e − r cos ν

Substituting this expression into r = e · ddir(ν) yields r = p − e r cos ν, which solves algebraically for the canonical polar conic equation:

r(ν) = p 1 + e cos ν = e · Ddir, Sun 1 + e cos ν

2.2 3D Spatial Line Representation in Heliocentric Ecliptic J2000.0 Coordinates

In three-dimensional heliocentric J2000.0 mean ecliptic coordinates, let the orthogonal perifocal basis unit vectors be P̂ (directed from the Sun toward perihelion), Q̂ (in the orbital plane at ν = +90° in the direction of motion), and Ŵ = P̂ × Q̂ (orbit normal), determined from the inclination i, longitude of the ascending node Ω, and argument of perihelion ω:

P̂ = ( cos Ω cos ω − sin Ω sin ω cos i ,\; sin Ω cos ω + cos Ω sin ω cos i ,\; sin ω sin i )
Q̂ = ( −cos Ω sin ω − sin Ω cos ω cos i ,\; −sin Ω sin ω + cos Ω cos ω cos i ,\; cos ω sin i )

For any non-circular orbit (e > 0), the primary directrix is the 3D affine line passing through the foot point rdir, 0 along the perihelion axis and extending parallel to Q̂:

rdirectrix(s) = a(1 − e2) e P̂ + s Q̂,    s ∈ (−∞, +∞)

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database published by the International Astronomical Union’s Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC):

  • Implicit Geometric Property (Not Tabulated): The directrix position Ddir, Sun (or xdir, center) is not stored as a fixed-width column in MPCORB.DAT. Instead, it is computed directly from the osculating orbital eccentricity e (columns 71–79, format F9.7) and osculating semi-major axis a (columns 93–103, format F11.7) via Ddir, Sun = a(1 − e2)/e, while its 3D spatial orientation is fixed by the osculating angular elements ω (columns 38–46), Ω (columns 49–57), and i (columns 60–68).
  • Singularity Behavior at Low Eccentricity (e → 0): Because MPCORB.DAT lists osculating eccentricities rounded to 7 decimal places (e.g., 0.0000000 for nearly circular main-belt asteroids or Trojans), parsers computing Ddir, Sun or xdir, center must guard against division by zero when e = 0 (returning +∞ or NaN/undefined).
  • Osculating Heliocentric Reference & Unit: Derived from MPCORB elements at the record’s packed Epoch (columns 21–25) in the J2000.0 mean ecliptic frame, all directrix distances are instantaneous osculating heliocentric lengths expressed in Astronomical Units (AU) (1 AU ≡ 149,597,870,700 m).
  • Cross-Catalog Continuity with COMET.DAT: In COMET.DAT, where perihelion distance q and eccentricity e are stored, the Sun–directrix distance evaluates smoothly across elliptic, parabolic (e = 1), and hyperbolic (e > 1) orbits via Ddir, Sun = q(1 + e)/e.
Implementation Guardrail for MPCORB Software: Always check e > 0.0 before evaluating d_dir_sun = (a * (1.0 - e) * (1.0 + e)) / e. For near-circular orbits in MPCORB.DAT with e < 1e-7, Ddir, Sun exceeds 107 AU (well outside the Solar System’s Hill sphere) and should be flagged as unbounded/at infinity in UI displays.
