Length of the Perihelion Node Arc (sq) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Elliptic Arc-Length Integral Formulation, Nodal-Apsidal Geometry, and Evaluation from MPCORB

1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Geometric Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In two-body Keplerian orbital geometry and elliptic-integral astrodynamics, the length of the perihelion node arc (denoted sq or LΩq…, and complementary to the aphelion node arc sQ)—is the physical spatial arc length (measured in Astronomical Units, AU) along the osculating Keplerian ellipse extending from the ascending node (Ω, where true anomaly νΩ = −ω, or 360° − ω) through perihelion (q, where ν = 0°) to the descending node (…, where ν… = 180° − ω).

Every inclined, bound Keplerian orbit (0 ≤ e < 1, 0° < i < 180°) intersects the J2000.0 reference ecliptic plane along the line of nodes at two antipodal focal points: the ascending node (Ω, ecliptic latitude crossing β : − → +) and the descending node (…, ecliptic latitude crossing β : + → −). This focal chord partitions the closed orbital perimeter C into two mutually exclusive, complementary orbital arcs:

C = sq + sQ = 4a E(e)

where a is the semi-major axis (AU), e is the numeric eccentricity, E(e) = ∫0π/2 √(1 − e2 sin2 θ) dθ is the complete elliptic integral of the second kind, sq is the perihelion node arc (spanning Ω → q → … across the northern ecliptic hemisphere β ≥ 0 for prograde orbits), and sQ is the aphelion node arc (spanning … → Q → Ω across the southern ecliptic hemisphere β ≤ 0).

Expressed in terms of the classical Keplerian elements (a, e, ω), the exact arc length sq is given by the definite line integral along the elliptic trajectory r(E) = (a(cos E − e), a√(1 − e2) sin E) parameterized by the continuous signed eccentric anomaly E ∈ [EΩ, E…] spanning perihelion (E = 0):

sq = a ∫EΩE… √(1 − e2 cos2 E) dE = a [ E(φ… | e2) − E(φΩ | e2) ]

where E(φ | m) = ∫0φ √(1 − m sin2 θ) dθ is the incomplete elliptic integral of the second kind with amplitude φ = E + π/2 (or evaluated directly via numerical Gauss–Legendre quadrature of ds = a√(1 − e2 cos2 E) dE over [EΩ, E…]), and the signed nodal eccentric anomalies EΩ ≤ 0 ≤ E… (for 0° ≤ ω ≤ 180°) are uniquely determined by the argument of perihelion ω:

EΩ = −2 arctan ( √(1 − e) √(1 + e) tan ω 2 )  ,     E… = 2 arctan ( √(1 − e) √(1 + e) tan π − ω 2 )

(with branches wrapped smoothly so that EΩ ≤ 0 ≤ E… and 0 < E… − EΩ < 2π for all 0° ≤ ω < 360°). Key geometric and physical properties of sq include:

  • Apsidal Line Symmetry (ω = 0°, 180°): When the line of nodes coincides with the line of apsides (ω = 0° or 180°), the focal chord bisects the ellipse into two congruent halves, yielding sq = sQ = 2a E(e) = C/2 regardless of eccentricity e.
  • Minimal & Maximal Perihelion Arc Extrema (ω = 90°, 270°): When the line of nodes is orthogonal to the line of apsides (ω = 90°, perihelion at maximum northern ecliptic latitude), the latus rectum passes through both nodes and sq attains its global minimum for fixed (a, e): sq,min < C/2 < sQ,max. Conversely, at ω = 270° (perihelion at maximum southern ecliptic latitude), sq attains its global maximum sq,max > C/2 > sQ,min.
  • Circular Orbit Limit (e → 0): For a circular orbit (e = 0), the perihelion node arc reduces identically to the semicircle sq = πa.
  • Distinction from Angular Span (Δνq = 180°): Because the line of nodes passes directly through the primary focus (the Sun), the true-anomaly angular span of the perihelion node arc is always exactly Δνq = (180° − ω) − (−ω) = 180° (π radians), whereas its physical metric length sq (AU) varies strongly with ω and e because the perihelion focus lies closer to the perihelion vertex (rq = a(1 − e) ≤ rQ = a(1 + e)).

2. Quadrature Formulation & Series Approximation

Because the incomplete elliptic integral of the second kind cannot be expressed in elementary functions for 0 < e < 1, sq is evaluated in high-precision astrodynamical pipelines via 16-point or 32-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature over the continuous interval E ∈ [EΩ, E…] (achieving full IEEE-754 double precision ≈ 10−16 across all minor-planet eccentricities e < 0.99) or Carlson symmetric elliptic integrals RF and RD.

For small-to-moderate eccentricities (e ≪ 1), expanding the arc-length differential ds/dE = a√(1 − e2 cos2 E) = a(1 − ½e2 cos2 E − ⅛e4 cos4 E − …) and substituting E… − EΩ = π − 2e sin ω + 𝒪(e3) yields the analytical asymptotic expansion:

sq = a [ π − 2e sin ω − π 4 e2 + 𝒪(e3) ]

The leading eccentricity-orientation coupling term −2ae sin ω demonstrates that sq falls below the semi-perimeter C/2 ≈ πa(1 − e2/4) whenever perihelion lies north of the ecliptic (0° < ω < 180°, sin ω > 0) and exceeds C/2 whenever perihelion lies south of the ecliptic (180° < ω < 360°, sin ω < 0), preserving sq + sQ = C identically.

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database published by the International Astronomical Union’s Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC):

  • Implicit Derived Metric Quantity (Not Tabulated): MPCORB.DAT distributes osculating orbits exclusively in classical Keplerian elements and does not tabulate sq (or sQ or the orbital circumference C) as explicit columns. Instead, sq is computed directly from three osculating columns present in every record:
    • Argument of perihelion ω (columns 38–46, format F9.5, degrees);
    • Numeric eccentricity e (columns 71–79, format F9.7, dimensionless);
    • Semi-major axis a (columns 93–103, format F11.7, AU).
  • Note on Inclination & Coplanar Limit (i = 0°): While i (columns 60–68) and Ω (columns 49–57) do not enter the numerical value of sq explicitly once ω is specified, the physical line of nodes is geometrically defined only for inclined orbits (0° < i < 180°). For exact coplanar records (i = 0°, Ω = 0° by convention), the integral evaluates smoothly using the tabulated ω (representing the arc from ecliptic longitude 0° through perihelion to 180°).
  • Physical & Astrodynamical Applications in MPCORB Analysis:
    • Inner-Solar-System Thermal & Cometary Outgassing Path Length: For Near-Earth Objects (NEOs), Jupiter-family comets, and sun-grazing bodies, sq quantifies the exact spatial path length traversed through the high-insolation inner heliocentric region (r ≤ pslr when ω = 90°, 270°) between ascending and descending ecliptic plane crossings.
    • Earth-Crossing NEO & Meteoroid Stream Geometry: Characterizes the spatial geometry of the inner nodal loop where minimum orbital intersection distances (MOIDs) with the terrestrial planets occur.
Implementation & Branch-Continuity Note: To guarantee a continuous integration interval spanning perihelion (E = 0) for any ω ∈ [0, 2π), map ω into (−π, π] before evaluating EΩ = −2 * Math.Atan2(Math.Sqrt(1 - e) * Math.Sin(0.5 * omegaSigned), Math.Sqrt(1 + e) * Math.Cos(0.5 * omegaSigned)), and compute E… on [0, 2π) via ν… = (Math.PI - omegaNorm) mod 2π, adding 2π to E… if E… < EΩ. This ensures EΩ ≤ 0 ≤ E… with 0 < E… − EΩ < 2π across all quadrants without branch jumps.
