Major Axis (2a) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Line of Apsides Geometry, and Derivation from the IAU Minor Planet Center Orbit Catalog

1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Geometric Definition

In celestial mechanics, orbital astrometry, and analytic conic geometry, the major axis—denoted algebraically as 2a (twice the semi-major axis a)—is the longest principal diameter of a bound elliptic Keplerian orbit (0 ≤ e < 1). Geometrically, it is the unique chord of the orbital ellipse that passes concurrently through the geometric center of the ellipse, both foci (the primary focus occupied by the Sun and the empty focus), and the two apsidal vertices: perihelion (point of minimum heliocentric distance) and aphelion (point of maximum heliocentric distance).

Within the orbital plane, the infinite straight line containing the major axis is termed the line of apsides, whose spatial orientation relative to the ascending node (Ω) is specified by the argument of perihelion (ω). The total scalar length of the major axis equals the exact sum of the perihelion distance (q) and the aphelion distance (Q):

Dmajor = 2a = q + Q = a(1 − e) + a(1 + e)

By the defining locus property of an ellipse, the major axis 2a also equals the constant sum of the Euclidean distances from any instantaneous orbital position P of the minor planet to the primary focus F1 (the Sun) and to the vacant focus F2:

|PF1| + |PF2| = r + r′ = 2a

For a strictly circular trajectory (e = 0), the two foci coalesce at the geometric center and the major axis degenerates into the uniform orbital diameter (2a = 2r = d).

2. Physical and Dynamical Significance

Because the major axis 2a is strictly proportional to the semi-major axis a, it determines the total spatial span of the minor planet's heliocentric excursion and is directly coupled to the specific orbital energy (ℰ) and sidereal orbital period (P).

2.1 Specific Orbital Energy (Vis-Viva Formulation)

In the heliocentric two-body approximation, the specific mechanical orbital energy ℰ is inversely proportional to the major axis 2a:

ℰ = v2 2 − μ r = − μ 2a   ⟺   2a = − μ ℰ

where v is the instantaneous heliocentric velocity, r is the instantaneous heliocentric distance, and μ = G(M☉ + m) ≈ GM☉ is the heliocentric gravitational parameter. Notably, the denominator 2a in the vis-viva energy integral ℰ = −μ/(2a) is literally the full major axis of the orbit.

2.2 Relationship to Mean Daily Motion (n) and Orbital Period (P)

Via Kepler's Third Law, the major axis 2a is linked to the mean daily motion n (tabulated in MPCORB) and the sidereal orbital period P:

2a = 2 &Bigl( k n &Bigr)2/3 = 2 &Bigl( μ P2 4π2 &Bigr)1/3

where k = 0.01720209895 AU3/2·d−1 is the Gaussian gravitational constant (kdeg ≈ 0.9856076686 deg·AU3/2·d−1).

3. Specific Status and Conventions in the MPCORB Database

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) catalog published by the International Astronomical Union's Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC), orbital size is standardized via the semi-major axis (a) rather than the full major axis (2a). Users parsing or analyzing MPCORB.DAT should observe the following conventions:

  • Not Stored Directly in MPCORB.DAT: To adhere to canonical astrodynamical convention, MPCORB.DAT stores the semi-major axis a in columns 93–103 (Fortran format F11.7). The full major axis is obtained immediately by multiplying the parsed column value by two (2a).
  • Osculating Heliocentric Reference: Because a in MPCORB is an osculating heliocentric element valid at the record's packed Epoch (columns 21–25) and referred to the J2000.0 mean ecliptic frame, the derived major axis 2a is likewise an instantaneous osculating heliocentric quantity subject to planetary N-body perturbations over time.
  • Unit of Measurement: Expressed in Astronomical Units (AU), where 1 AU ≡ 149,597,870,700 m (exact, per IAU 2012 Resolution B2).
  • Geometric Crossing Criterion: In Solar System small-body dynamics, the full major axis 2a = q + Q represents the radial span of the torus swept by the minor planet between perihelion and aphelion, serving as a fundamental metric in orbital intersection (MOID) and planet-crossing classifications.
Conic Section Restriction (0 ≤ e < 1): A finite positive major axis (2a > 0) exists exclusively for closed elliptic orbits (0 ≤ e < 1), which comprise the entirety of standard numbered and unnumbered minor planets in MPCORB.DAT. Parabolic trajectories (e = 1) possess an infinite major axis (2a → ∞), while hyperbolic trajectories (e > 1) have a negative transverse axis (2a < 0) representing the distance between the two vertices of the hyperbola branches; such objects are cataloged separately in COMET.DAT parameterized by perihelion distance q.
