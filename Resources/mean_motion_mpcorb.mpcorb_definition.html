Mean Motion

Uniform Keplerian Orbital Frequency and Propagation Parameter in MPCORB.DAT

1. Formal Definition

In celestial mechanics, astrodynamics, and the Minor Planet Center Orbit Database (MPCORB), the mean motion (n) is the constant, orbit-averaged angular frequency at which a minor planet completes one full sidereal revolution of 2π radians (360°) around the Sun in an unperturbed two-body Keplerian system.

Whereas the instantaneous orbital angular velocity (ν̇ = dν/dt) varies continuously along an eccentric trajectory according to Kepler's second law, the mean motion n defines the uniform angular progression of an idealized auxiliary body revolving on a concentric reference circle of radius equal to the osculating semimajor axis a with the identical sidereal orbital period P. By definition, n is the constant time derivative of the mean anomaly M(t):

n = dM / dt = 2π / P  [rad / time] = 360° / P  [deg / time] (Eq. 1)
Figure 1: Schematic geometry of mean motion n versus instantaneous true anomaly rate ν̇. While the physical minor planet P accelerates near perihelion (q) and decelerates near aphelion (Q) along the osculating ellipse, the mean anomaly M(t) advances at the strictly uniform angular rate n on the concentric auxiliary circle of radius a.

2. Mathematical Formulation in the MPCORB Two-Body Framework

In the IAU Minor Planet Center's heliocentric dynamical model, the mean motion n is uniquely determined by the osculating semimajor axis a (in astronomical units, AU) through Kepler's Third Law of Planetary Motion and the Gaussian gravitational constant k:

nrad = √(μ / a3) = k · a−3/2  [rad / day] (Eq. 2)

Because orbital angles in MPCORB.DAT are tabulated in degrees and time is measured in ephemeris days (1 d = 86,400 s of TT/TDB), the angular rate in degrees per day (commonly termed mean daily motion) is:

ndeg = (180 / π) · k · a−3/2 ≈ 0.985607668601425 · a−3/2  [deg / day] (Eq. 3)

where:

  • k = 0.01720209895 AU3/2 d−1 is the classical Gaussian gravitational constant, defining the heliocentric gravitational parameter μ = GM⊙ = k2 ≈ 2.959122082855911 × 10−4 AU3 d−2 (or 1.32712440018 × 1011 km3 s−2).
  • kdeg = (180/π) k ≈ 0.9856076686 deg AU3/2 d−1 is the Gaussian constant expressed in degrees per day, equal to the mean motion of an idealized massless test particle at a = 1 AU.
  • a is the osculating semimajor axis in AU (stored in MPCORB.DAT columns 93–103). In accordance with IAU MPC standards for minor planets, the asteroid's own mass m is treated as negligible (m / M⊙ < 10−9), so μ = G(M⊙ + m) ≈ GM⊙.

3. Storage & Role in MPCORB.DAT

In the fixed-width ASCII schema of MPCORB.DAT, both the semimajor axis a and the mean motion n are explicitly stored in every object record. Providing n directly eliminates the need to compute fractional powers (a−3/2) during bulk ephemeris propagation across catalogs exceeding 1.4 million minor planets.

4. Osculating vs. Proper Mean Motion & Dynamical Resonances

  • Osculating vs. Proper Elements: Each line in MPCORB.DAT represents an osculating Keplerian orbit fitted at a common tabular epoch (typically updated every 200 days at 0h TT). Gravitational perturbations from the major planets cause a and n to exhibit short-period fluctuations around their long-term averages. Removing these periodic perturbations via synthetic numerical integration or analytical perturbation theory yields the proper mean motion (np), which serves as a quasi-integral of motion used to identify collisional asteroid families.
  • Mean-Motion Resonances (MMRs): Commensurabilities between a minor planet's mean motion n and that of a perturbing planet—predominantly Jupiter (nJ ≈ 0.083091 deg/d at aJ ≈ 5.204 AU)—occur when n / nJ ≈ p / q for small integers p, q. Unstable resonances carve the Kirkwood gaps in the main asteroid belt (e.g., the 3:1 resonance at n ≈ 0.295 deg/d, a ≈ 2.50 AU; 5:2 at a ≈ 2.82 AU; 2:1 at a ≈ 3.28 AU), whereas phase-protected resonances stabilize populations such as the Hilda asteroids (3:2, n ≈ 0.125 deg/d) and Jupiter Trojans (1:1, n ≈ 0.0831 deg/d).
  • Relation to Synodic Period: With respect to Earth (n⊕ ≈ 0.985608 deg/d), the synodic period S between consecutive oppositions of an exterior minor planet is governed directly by the difference in mean motions: S = 360° / |n⊕ − n|.
