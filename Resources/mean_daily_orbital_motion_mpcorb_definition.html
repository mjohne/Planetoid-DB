Mean Daily Orbital Motion

Orbit-Averaged Daily Spatial Path Length (Linear Distance per Day) in MPCORB.DAT

1. Formal Definition

In heliocentric orbital mechanics and derived astrodynamical analytics based on the Minor Planet Center Orbit Database (MPCORB), the mean daily orbital motion (s̄d or Δsd) is defined as the orbit-averaged linear path length (spatial distance) traversed by a minor planet along its Keplerian ellipse per ephemeris day (1 d = 86,400 s of TT/TDB), expressed in units of length per day (such as astronomical units per day, AU/d, or kilometers per day, km/d).

In direct linear-spatial analogy to the angular mean daily motion n (which divides a complete revolution of 360° or 2π rad by the sidereal orbital period P), the mean daily orbital motion divides the total geometric circumference (perimeter) L of the osculating orbital ellipse by the sidereal orbital period P. Because 1 d is a fixed unit interval of time, this quantity is numerically identical to the mean orbital speed (v̄orb) scaled to a daily epoch step, representing the constant spatial arc increment covered per day by a fictitious uniform-speed body completing one full circuit L in time P:

s̄d = L / P = (L · ndeg) / 360° = (L · nrad) / (2π)  [AU / d] (Eq. 1)
Figure 1: Spatial interpretation of mean daily orbital motion (s̄d = L / P, green arc) along an osculating Keplerian ellipse of total perimeter L. While the true daily path length Δs(t) = ∫ v(t) dt peaks at perihelion (Δsq, amber) and reaches its minimum at aphelion (ΔsQ, slate), the mean daily orbital motion represents the uniform linear distance traversed per ephemeris day across one complete revolution.

2. Analytical Derivation from MPCORB Elements

Unlike the angular mean daily motion n—which depends solely on the semimajor axis a—the linear mean daily orbital motion s̄d depends on both the osculating semimajor axis a (in AU) and the orbital eccentricity e (0 ≤ e < 1), because the geometric circumference L of an ellipse shrinks as e increases at fixed a.

The exact perimeter L of the osculating ellipse is given by the complete elliptic integral of the second kind E(e):

L = 4a · E(e) = 4a ∫0π/2 √(1 − e2 sin2θ) dθ = 2πa &left;[ 1 − ∑m=1∞ ((2m)! / (22m (m!)2))2 · (e2m / (2m − 1)) &right;]  [AU] (Eq. 2)

Combining L with the Keplerian sidereal orbital period P = 2π / (k · a−3/2) = 360° / ndeg [d] yields the exact analytical expression for the mean daily orbital motion:

s̄d = L / P = (2 / π) · k · a−1/2 · E(e) = (4a · E(e) · ndeg) / 360°  [AU / d] (Eq. 3)

where:

  • k = 0.01720209895 AU3/2 d−1 is the Gaussian gravitational constant (k · a−1/2 equals the circular orbital speed at radius a in AU/d).
  • E(e) is the complete elliptic integral of the second kind with modulus e (E(0) = π/2 for a circle, decreasing monotonically to E(1) = 1 as e → 1).
  • For a circular orbit (e = 0), L = 2πa and E(0) = π/2, so Eq. 3 reduces to s̄d,circ = k · a−1/2 = 2πa · (ndeg / 360°) [AU/d].
  • For high-precision numerical evaluation without infinite series truncation, E(e) is computed via the Arithmetic-Geometric Mean (AGM) algorithm or approximated to better than 10−15 using Ramanujan's second perimeter formula with b = a√(1 − e2) and h = ((a − b)/(a + b))2: L ≈ π(a + b)[1 + 3h / (10 + √(4 − 3h))].

3. Derivation from MPCORB.DAT Parameters & Unit Conversions

MPCORB.DAT does not store the linear perimeter L or the linear daily path s̄d directly; instead, both are uniquely determined from three tabulated columns in each minor planet record:

Semimajor Axis (a) MPCORB.DAT cols 93–103 (F11.7) AU; for Ceres: a = 2.7675059 AU.

Standard unit conversion factors using the IAU 2012 exact definition of the astronomical unit (1 AU = 149,597,870.700 km):

  • Kilometers per ephemeris day: s̄d [km/d] = s̄d [AU/d] × 149,597,870.700
  • Mean orbital velocity (v̄orb): v̄orb [km/s] = s̄d [km/d] / 86,400

4. Comparison of Mean Daily Orbital Motion with Extremal Daily Path Lengths

Because orbital velocity varies along an eccentric trajectory according to the vis-viva equation v(r) = k√(2/r − 1/a), the linear mean daily orbital motion s̄d lies strictly between the instantaneous daily spatial displacement at perihelion (r = q = a(1 − e)) and at aphelion (r = Q = a(1 + e)):

  • Circular Orbit Limit (e = 0): s̄d = k · a−1/2. All daily path increments are identical around the orbit.
  • Eccentricity Scaling (0 < e < 1): At constant semimajor axis a, the orbital period P remains invariant while the ellipse circumference L = 4a E(e) decreases from 2πa (at e = 0) to 4a (in the degenerate radial limit e → 1). Consequently, s̄d decreases by a factor of 2/π ≈ 0.63662 across the full bound eccentricity range e ∈ [0, 1).
  • Relation to Time-Averaged Speed vs. Path-Averaged Speed: Because s̄d = (1/P) ∫0P v(t) dt, the mean daily orbital motion equals the exact time-averaged speed over one orbital period (multiplied by 1 d), whereas the geometric mean of perihelion and aphelion speeds √(vq vQ) = k · a−1/2 equals the circular speed at distance a.
