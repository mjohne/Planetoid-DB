Focal Parameter (p) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Conic-Section Scale Invariant, Directrix Coupling, and Evaluation from MPCORB

1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Conic-Geometric Definition

Nomenclature & Equivalence: In analytical conic geometry, projective astrometry, and European/Russian celestial mechanics traditions (e.g., Russian фокальный параметр, German Brennparameter), the focal parameter—universally denoted by the lowercase symbol p—is the canonical scale parameter of a Keplerian conic trajectory, identically equal to the semi-latus rectum (p = a(1 − e2) = q(1 + e)).

Geometrically, the focal parameter p admits two equivalent, fundamental definitions for any non-rectilinear conic section (ellipse, parabola, or hyperbola) with eccentricity e ≥ 0:

  1. Orthogonal Focal Radius (Semi-Latus Rectum): p is the heliocentric distance r(ν) from the primary focus F1 (occupied by the Sun) to the orbital trajectory measured perpendicular to the line of apsides (at true anomaly ν = ±π/2 rad = ±90°).
  2. Directrix–Focus Scale Law: By the focus–directrix property of conic sections, every point on the orbit satisfies r = e · ddir, where ddir is the perpendicular distance to the corresponding directrix line. If Ddir denotes the perpendicular distance from the primary focus F1 to the directrix (Ddir = a/e − ae = a(1 − e2)/e for e > 0), then the focal parameter is the invariant product:
    p = e · Ddir = a(1 − e2) = q(1 + e) = b2 a = 2 q Q q + Q

Notice why p is called the focal parameter: whereas the semi-major axis a becomes infinite at the parabolic escape boundary (e = 1) and negative on hyperbolic trajectories (e > 1), and the directrix distance Ddir diverges at the circular limit (e → 0+), their product p = e Ddir = a(1 − e2) remains strictly finite, continuous, and positive (0 < p < ∞) across all non-degenerate conic sections.

2. Astrodynamical Role in Two-Body & Perturbed Motion

In celestial mechanics, the pair (p, e) forms a singularity-free shape-and-scale parameterization of any two-body Keplerian trajectory around a central mass with gravitational parameter μ = G(M☉ + m) ≈ GM☉.

2.1 Universal Conic Equation & Angular Momentum Scaling

In heliocentric polar coordinates (r, ν), the trajectory is governed identically across elliptic, parabolic, and hyperbolic regimes by the focal parameter p and eccentricity e:

r(ν) = p 1 + e cos ν   &text{with}   p = h2 μ

where h = |r × v| = √(μ p) is the magnitude of the specific relative orbital angular momentum. Consequently, p measures the angular-momentum-controlled radial scale of the orbit, whereas e measures the dimensionless focal offset.

2.2 Gauss’s Variational Equations for Osculating Elements

When propagating an MPCORB osculating orbit forward or backward in time under non-Keplerian perturbing accelerations—expressed in the local orbital frame (R radial, T transverse/along-track, N normal)—Gauss’s planetary equations link the time rate of change of the focal parameter p directly and exclusively to the transverse perturbing acceleration T:

dp dt = 2 r p h T = 2 r √ p μ T

Unlike the variational equations for a and e individually, dp/dt has no dependence on the radial perturbing force R and exhibits no 1/e or 1/(1 − e) singularities—making p an indispensable state variable in high-precision asteroid and comet orbit determination.

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database published by the International Astronomical Union’s Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC):

  • Implicit Storage in MPCORB.DAT: The focal parameter p is not stored as an explicit fixed-width column in MPCORB.DAT. Because MPCORB.DAT catalogs bound elliptic minor planet orbits (0 ≤ e < 1), it stores the osculating eccentricity e (columns 71–79, format F9.7) and osculating semi-major axis a (columns 93–103, format F11.7), from which p is derived via p = a(1 − e2) = a(1 − e)(1 + e).
  • Cross-Catalog Unification with COMET.DAT: For parabolic (e = 1) and hyperbolic (e > 1) objects in COMET.DAT, where a is undefined or negative, the focal parameter is evaluated from perihelion distance q and eccentricity e via p = q(1 + e). Thus, converting both MPCORB.DAT and COMET.DAT records into the canonical (p, e, i, Ω, ω, ν) element set provides a single, unified orbital representation across all Solar System small bodies.
  • Osculating Heliocentric Reference & Unit: Derived from MPCORB elements at the record’s packed Epoch (columns 21–25) in the J2000.0 mean ecliptic frame, p is an instantaneous osculating heliocentric length expressed in Astronomical Units (AU) (1 AU ≡ 149,597,870,700 m).
  • Primary Role in Ephemeris & MOID Pipelines: Astrometric ephemeris generators and algebraic Minimum Orbit Intersection Distance (MOID) algorithms compute p immediately upon parsing each MPCORB.DAT line so that heliocentric radius r = p / (1 + e cos ν) and perifocal velocities √(μ/p) are evaluated without singularities.
Terminology & Precision Note: In English astrodynamics literature, “focal parameter” and “semi-latus rectum” are exact synonyms for p = a(1 − e2). When implementing MPCORB parsers in IEEE-754 double precision, compute p = a * (1.0 - e) * (1.0 + e) to preserve maximum numerical accuracy for high-eccentricity objects (e → 1−).
