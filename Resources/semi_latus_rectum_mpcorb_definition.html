Semi-Latus Rectum (p) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Conic Parameterization, and Derivation from the IAU Minor Planet Center Orbit Catalog

1. Fundamental Astrodynamical & Geometric Definition

In celestial mechanics, analytical conic geometry, and orbital astrometry, the semi-latus rectum (also called the focal parameter or simply the parameter of a conic section), universally denoted by the lowercase symbol p (occasionally ℓ), is one-half of the latus rectum—the chord of a Keplerian trajectory passing through the primary focus (occupied by the Sun) perpendicular to the line of apsides (the major axis).

Equivalently, p is the heliocentric radial distance r(ν) of a minor planet when its true anomaly equals ν = ±π/2 rad (±90°) (i.e., when the heliocentric position vector is orthogonal to the perihelion vector). Uniquely among orbital length scales, the semi-latus rectum remains strictly finite and positive across all non-rectilinear conic sections—elliptic (0 ≤ e < 1), parabolic (e = 1), and hyperbolic (e > 1):

p = a(1 − e2) = q(1 + e) = b2 a = 2 q Q q + Q

where a is the semi-major axis, e is the orbital eccentricity, b = a√(1 − e2) is the semi-minor axis, q = a(1 − e) is the perihelion distance, and Q = a(1 + e) is the aphelion distance. The final identity demonstrates a remarkable geometric symmetry: whereas a is the arithmetic mean of q and Q, and b is their geometric mean, the semi-latus rectum p is the exact harmonic mean of the perihelion and aphelion distances. For a circular orbit (e = 0), p = a = b = q = Q.

2. Physical & Dynamical Significance in Celestial Mechanics

In two-body astrodynamics, the semi-latus rectum p is the fundamental geometric invariant directly proportional to the square of the specific relative orbital angular momentum (h = r × v), independent of the specific orbital energy (ℰ).

2.1 Direct Coupling to Specific Orbital Angular Momentum

Under a central inverse-square gravitational field with parameter μ = G(M☉ + m) ≈ GM☉, the semi-latus rectum is determined purely by the magnitude h = |h|:

p = h2 μ   ⟺   h = √ μ p

Consequently, any two Keplerian orbits possessing identical specific angular momentum share the exact same semi-latus rectum p, regardless of whether their trajectories are circular, elliptic, parabolic, or hyperbolic.

2.2 Universal Polar Equation of Motion & State-Vector Transformations

The semi-latus rectum serves as the numerator in the universal polar conic equation governing heliocentric radial distance r(ν) as a function of true anomaly ν across all eccentricity regimes:

r(ν) = p 1 + e cos ν = a(1 − e2) 1 + e cos ν

Furthermore, in perifocal (PQW) coordinate transformations used to convert MPCORB orbital elements into 3D heliocentric Cartesian position (r) and velocity (v) state vectors, the perifocal velocity components are scaled directly by √(μ/p):

vPQW = √ μ p &Bigl( −sin ν P̂ + (e + cos ν) Q̂ &Bigr)

Using p in state-vector evaluation avoids singularities at e = 1 (parabolic limit) and seamlessly unifies elliptic minor planet propagation with hyperbolic comet/interstellar object propagation.

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database published by the International Astronomical Union's Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC), the semi-latus rectum p is not tabulated as an explicit column in MPCORB.DAT. Instead, it is computed deterministically from two primary osculating elements stored in every record:

  • Computational Derivation from MPCORB.DAT: Parse the osculating orbital eccentricity e (columns 71–79, Fortran format F9.7) and the osculating semi-major axis a (columns 93–103, Fortran format F11.7), and evaluate p = a(1 − e2) = a(1 − e)(1 + e).
  • Derivation from COMET.DAT (Parabolic/Hyperbolic Regime): For comets and interstellar objects parameterized by perihelion distance q and eccentricity e, evaluate p = q(1 + e), which remains completely free of division-by-zero singularities at e = 1.
  • Osculating Heliocentric Reference & Unit: Because a and e in MPCORB.DAT are osculating heliocentric elements valid at the record's packed Epoch (columns 21–25) in the J2000.0 mean ecliptic frame, the derived semi-latus rectum p is likewise an instantaneous osculating heliocentric length expressed in Astronomical Units (AU) (1 AU ≡ 149,597,870,700 m).
  • Role in MOID & Orbit Intersection Solvers: Analytical and algebraic algorithms for computing the Minimum Orbit Intersection Distance (MOID) between two MPCORB objects (such as Gronchi's resultant method and Baluev–Mikryukov trigonometric polynomial solvers) reformulate both trajectories in terms of p and the eccentricity vector components (e cos ω, e sin ω) to eliminate transcendental anomaly dependencies.
Numerical Stability Tip for MPCORB Parsers: When computing p in IEEE-754 double precision from MPCORB.DAT for high-eccentricity objects (e → 1−), factor the expression as p = a * (1.0 - e) * (1.0 + e) rather than a * (1.0 - e * e) to reduce rounding error in the (1 − e) factor.
