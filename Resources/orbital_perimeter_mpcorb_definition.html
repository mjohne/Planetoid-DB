Orbital Perimeter (L or U) in the Context of the MPCORB Database

Scientific Definition, Complete Elliptic Integral Formulation, Rapid Series & AGM Evaluation, and Derivation from MPCORB

1. Fundamental Differential-Geometric & Astrodynamical Definition

Definition & Nomenclature: In differential geometry and two-body celestial mechanics, the orbital perimeter (also termed the orbital circumference, elliptic arc-length circumference, or in German astrometry Bahnumfang)—typically denoted by L, U, or C—is the total closed Euclidean arc length traversed by an orbiting body along its instantaneous osculating Keplerian ellipse during one complete orbital revolution (0 ≤ E < 2π). It is a dimensional length quantity expressed in Astronomical Units (AU).

Parametrizing a closed elliptic Keplerian orbit (0 ≤ e < 1) of semi-major axis a and semi-minor axis b = a√(1 − e2) in perifocal Cartesian coordinates via the eccentric anomaly E as xω(E) = a cos E − ae, yω(E) = b sin E, the differential Euclidean line element is:

ds = √ dxω2 + dyω2 = √ a2 sin2 E + b2 cos2 E dE = a √ 1 − e2 cos2 E dE

Integrating ds around the full closed orbit (E ∈ [0, 2π), corresponding to four symmetric quadrant arcs of length L/4 between perihelion and the minor-axis crossings) yields the exact expression for the orbital perimeter L in terms of the complete elliptic integral of the second kind Eell(e):

L = ∮ ds = 4 a ∫0π/2 √ 1 − e2 sin2 φ dφ = 4 a Eell(e)

where φ = π/2 − E is the complementary eccentric angle and Eell(e) = Eell(m = e2) denotes Legendre’s complete elliptic integral of the second kind with elliptic modulus e (parameter m = e2). Because Eell(e) cannot be expressed in finite elementary algebraic functions for 0 < e < 1, L exhibits the following strict monotonic bounds and asymptotic limits:

  • Strict Monotonic Bounds (4a < L ≤ 2πa): As numeric eccentricity e increases from 0 to 1− at fixed semi-major axis a, Eell(e) decreases strictly monotonically from π/2 to 1, so the orbital perimeter shrinks monotonically from 2πa ≈ 6.2831853a down to 4a.
  • Circular Limit (e = 0 ⇒ L = 2πa): Since Eell(0) = π/2, the orbital perimeter reduces to the classical circle circumference L = 2πa.
  • Degenerate Needle Limit (e → 1− ⇒ L → 4a+): Since Eell(1) = 1, an infinitely flattened ellipse of major axis 2a collapses into a double traversal of the line segment between perihelion and aphelion of total round-trip length 2 × (2a) = 4a.
  • Parabolic Divergence (e = 1): Because a parabola is an open curve (a → ∞), its total orbital perimeter is infinite (L = +∞).

2. Exact Series Expansions & High-Precision Algorithms

Because Eell(e) is non-elementary, scientific astrodynamics libraries evaluate the orbital perimeter L using rapidly convergent series or iterative quadrature schemes:

2.1 Ivory–Bessel Series in the Third Flattening (h)

Defining the dimensionless geometric shape parameter h (the squared third flattening, n2 in geodesy) in terms of a and b:

h = (a − b)2 (a + b)2 = (1 − √ 1 − e2 )2 (1 + √ 1 − e2 )2 = e4 (1 + √ 1 − e2 )4  ∈ [0, 1)

Ivory (1796) and Bessel (1825) established the rapidly converging binomial power series in h:

L = π (a + b) ∑k=0∞ [(2k)!]2 (2k k!)4 (1 − 2k)2 hk = π (a + b) [1 + 14 h + 164 h2 + 1256 h3 + 2516384 h4 + ···]

Notice that for main-belt asteroids (e ≈ 0.15), h ≈ 3.2 × 10−5, so truncating after the h3 term already yields full 16-digit IEEE-754 double precision (< 10−18 relative error).

2.2 Gauss–Kummer / Arithmetic-Geometric Mean (AGM) Quadrature

For extreme high-eccentricity comets or Damocloids in MPCORB.DAT (e → 1−, where h → 1− slows series convergence), the Arithmetic-Geometric Mean (AGM) algorithm computes Eell(e) to machine precision in 4–6 iterations via a0 = 1, b0 = √(1 − e2), c0 = e, iterating an+1 = (an + bn)/2, bn+1 = √(anbn), cn+1 = (an − bn)/2 until cN < 10−16:

L = 2 π a aN [1 − ∑n=0N 2n−1 cn2]

2.3 Ramanujan’s Second Closed-Form Approximation

When lightweight analytical speed is prioritized over sub-nanometer accuracy across millions of catalog rows, Ramanujan’s second formula provides an algebraic approximation accurate to better than 4 × 10−9 for all e ≤ 0.9 and 4 × 10−4 even as e → 1−:

L ≈ π (a + b) [1 + 3 h 10 + √ 4 − 3 h ]

2.4 Time-Averaged Orbital Speed (v̄)

Combining the orbital perimeter L with the Keplerian sidereal orbital period P = 2π√(a3/μ) yields the exact time-averaged scalar orbital speed v̄ over one complete revolution (distinct from the circular speed vcirc = √(μ/a) whenever e > 0):

v̄ = L P = 4 a Eell(e) 2 π a3/2 / √μ = 2 Eell(e) π √ μ a ≤ vcirc

3. Specific Status and Derivation in the MPCORB Catalog

In the MPCORB (Minor Planet Center Orbit) database published by the International Astronomical Union’s Minor Planet Center (IAU MPC):

  • Implicit Derived Quantity (Not Tabulated): The orbital perimeter L is not tabulated in MPCORB.DAT. Instead, it is computed directly from the osculating numeric eccentricity e (columns 71–79, format F9.7) and osculating semi-major axis a (columns 93–103, format F11.7) via L = 4a Eell(e).
  • Universal Finiteness Across All MPCORB.DAT Records: Because every record in MPCORB.DAT represents a bound elliptic orbit (0 ≤ e < 1, a > 0), the orbital perimeter L is strictly finite, positive, and bounded by 4a < L ≤ 2πa across 100% of catalog entries.
  • Osculating Heliocentric Reference & Unit: Evaluated from MPCORB elements at the record’s packed Epoch (columns 21–25) in the J2000.0 mean ecliptic frame, L represents the closed path length of the instantaneous osculating Keplerian ellipse (neglecting planetary perturbations over the cycle) expressed in Astronomical Units (AU) (1 AU ≡ 149,597,870,700 m).
  • Applications in Astrodynamics & Spatial Indexing:
    • Time-Averaged Orbital Velocity: Computing v̄ = L / P for dynamical population comparisons across orbital resonances and asteroid families.
    • Uniform Arc-Length Discretization: Computing the step size Δs = L / Nseg when subdividing an osculating orbit into Nseg equal-arc-length polyline segments for 3D visualization or minimum orbit intersection distance (MOID) coarse-grid sampling.
Numerical Stability Note: When computing h for the Ivory–Bessel series or Ramanujan’s formula at small eccentricities (e → 0), avoid writing (a - b) / (a + b) directly, as subtracting a − b causes catastrophic floating-point cancellation when e ∼ 10−6. Instead, use the cancellation-free algebraic identity double h = Math.Pow(e / (1.0 + Math.Sqrt(1.0 - e * e)), 4);, which remains exact down to e = 0.
